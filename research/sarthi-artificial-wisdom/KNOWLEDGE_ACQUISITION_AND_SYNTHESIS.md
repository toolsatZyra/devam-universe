# Knowledge acquisition and synthesis

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Status:** recommended programme design; no acquisition or external contact authorized

## 1. Principle

Artificial-wisdom work does not relax Devam's library-first standard. Sarthi needs both:

- complete, identity-preserved source and interpretation lanes; and
- derived, inspectable knowledge products that let retrieval select the right evidence for the right situation.

The second cannot substitute for the first. Metadata, graph edges, snippets, cases, or patterns do not make an unacquired or structurally incomplete source complete.

## 2. What Devam must acquire

### A. Source originals and structurally complete expressions

For every wisdom vertical:

- identified work, edition/witness, expression, language, representation, extent, and completeness state;
- exact original-language source where rights permit;
- complete product-usable Hindi and English expressions for the selected lane;
- materially important variant witnesses, not a single silently canonicalised text;
- stable passage coordinates and source-object fixity;
- rights lane separately for storage, internal processing, quotation, translation, and product display.

Priority is edition-specific end-to-end acquisition. A wisdom pattern built from a few famous verses must be labelled evidence-bounded and cannot stand in for the work or tradition.

### B. Interpretive traditions

Acquire commentaries and modern scholarship as separately identified sources, including materially different:

- sampradaya and philosophical readings;
- regional and vernacular reception;
- historical layers and later reinterpretations;
- ritual, devotional, ethical, literary, social, and philosophical readings;
- critiques, minority readings, and disputes about attribution or application.

Each interpretation record must answer “who reads what, in which textual/traditional frame, when, for what purpose, and with what evidence?” Do not create a synthetic “Hindu view.”

### C. Procedures and applicability

For ritual and observance guidance, acquire the complete product contract:

- deterministic calendar/precedence rule;
- applicability by geography, sampradaya, institution, household, family, life stage, and setting;
- significance and source-labelled origin narratives;
- typical practice, distinguished from instruction;
- preparation, materials, substitutions, ordered steps, timing, closing, after-practice, and cautions;
- minimum, household, fuller family/teacher/priest-led forms where evidence supports them;
- conflicts, variants, and lane-specific evidence.

If practical evidence is missing, preserve the gap. Do not use model fluency to complete a vidhi.

### D. Cases

A case library should include several evidence roles rather than only epic exemplars:

1. **Textual cases:** a bounded event or dilemma in an identified source and narrative context.
2. **Commentarial cases:** how a named interpreter understands that event or principle.
3. **Historical cases:** documented actions and consequences with scholarly provenance.
4. **Legal/social cases:** only where reliable evidence and rights permit; never collapse descriptive precedent into sacred norm.
5. **Living-practice cases:** community, temple, family, or practitioner accounts with consent, scope, and access restrictions.
6. **Synthetic evaluation cases:** fictional scenarios authored for testing, always labelled synthetic and never served as evidence.

Case acquisition captures dilemma, actors, relationships, duties, power, constraints, knowledge available at the time, options, action, immediate and later consequences, contested interpretations, and missing information. Narrative sequence must remain linked to source passages; a case abstraction is not the story itself.

### E. Counterevidence and failure cases

Actively acquire:

- counterexamples to attractive generalisations;
- cases where the same action had different consequences;
- cases that share superficial language but differ in power, consent, duty, or risk;
- internal critiques and competing interpretations;
- harmful modern uses of fatalism, karma blame, caste/gender universalisation, or false sacred authority;
- product failures: irrelevant quotation, over-questioning, false balance, preachiness, and non-actionable consolation.

Counterevidence is a first-class acquisition target, not an editorial afterthought.

### F. Current and living-practice evidence

Living practice changes and can be institution-, family-, and place-specific. Records need:

- narrator/community/institution identity at the granularity consent permits;
- role and basis of authority, without Devam declaring spiritual rank;
- date, place, language, community/tradition, setting, and intended audience;
- elicitation method, interviewer/researcher, prompts, recording/transcript provenance, and translation;
- whether the account is description, prescription, memory, interpretation, or institutional programme;
- consent terms, allowed uses, review rights, embargo/access, withdrawal/correction path, attribution preference, and sensitive/sacred restrictions;
- corroboration and disagreement, without forcing consensus.

UNESCO's ethical principles place communities, groups, and practitioners in the primary safeguarding role and require transparent collaboration plus free, prior, sustained, and informed consent ([UNESCO ICH ethics](https://ich.unesco.org/en/ethics-and-ich-00866)). Oral History Association guidance similarly requires ongoing consent, attention to power, context, preservation, and access terms ([OHA Core Principles](https://oralhistory.org/oha-core-principles/)). CARE principles are relevant when working with Indigenous peoples and data governance, but must not be generalised to every Hindu or Indian community without their applicability and authority ([GIDA CARE](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples)).

No living-practice acquisition should begin under this research programme; external contact requires explicit later authorization and a reviewed protocol.

### G. User-context evidence

Sarthi needs only context that can change the answer:

- current question and desired form of help;
- location/timezone when timing or local practice depends on it;
- language;
- tradition, sampradaya, institution, or family practice when material;
- setting, participants, constraints, accessibility, and safety considerations;
- user's values, commitments, and options as stated in the conversation;
- consent for any persistence.

Do not infer caste, initiation, health, pregnancy, trauma, finances, or family structure from proxies. Sensitive context remains session-specific unless the user explicitly asks to save it.

## 3. Acquisition priority model

Score candidate work by user need and research leverage, not graph novelty:

| Factor | Question | Weight hypothesis |
|---|---|---:|
| MVP demand | Does it support a hero universe, Sep–Dec ritual, or first wisdom vertical? | 5 |
| Decision consequence | Could absence cause materially wrong or harmful guidance? | 5 |
| Source completeness gap | Is a required edition/expression missing or partial? | 5 |
| Interpretive diversity | Does one current source risk flattening material differences? | 4 |
| Living applicability | Is text insufficient to describe current practice? | 4 |
| Rights feasibility | Can it be lawfully acquired and used in the required lane? | 4 |
| Counterexample leverage | Does it test a high-risk pattern or analogy? | 3 |
| Reuse | Does it support many evaluated questions without becoming generic? | 3 |

The weights are a planning hypothesis. Rights restrictions and completeness gaps remain hard gates, not numbers that high demand can erase.

## 4. From source to usable knowledge

### Stage 0 — immutable acquisition

Register object hash, provenance, rights, work/expression/edition identity, representation, language, expected extent, and structural completeness. Never duplicate source bytes into the wisdom layer.

### Stage 1 — source-aligned segmentation

Create addressable passages, narrative units, procedure steps, commentary units, and media/time segments. Preserve source order and exact coordinates.

### Stage 2 — typed evidence assertions

Editors create atomic claims with:

- claim text and language;
- assertion type: factual, definitional, interpretive, narrative, procedural, applicability, causal, value, consequence, or current/live;
- speaker/source role;
- evidence span and entailment relation;
- scope dimensions;
- confidence based on evidence status;
- conflicts and qualification;
- product and rights status.

Generated extraction can propose records, but source-aligned checks and editorial approval control publication.

### Stage 3 — contextual relations

Create only useful, attributable relations: identity/variant, part/sequence, actor/role, applies-to, interprets, supports, contradicts, qualifies, precedes, follows, causes/allegedly-causes, analogous-to, disanalogous-to, and derived-from. A relation can itself require evidence and scope.

### Stage 4 — case abstraction

Create a versioned abstraction linked to all source and interpretation evidence. Separate:

- observed narrative/historical facts;
- attributed interpretation;
- editor-coded dimensions;
- unresolved ambiguity;
- later Devam use as analogy.

Two reviewers should independently code high-consequence case dimensions during schema validation. Disagreement becomes data for rubric/schema revision, not a majority-vote “truth.”

### Stage 5 — candidate pattern induction

Patterns may be proposed through editor research or model-assisted clustering only after multiple cases exist. A candidate must include:

- concise lens, not an imperative;
- supporting cases from more than one passage/case where the “recurring” claim depends on recurrence;
- negative/counter cases;
- preconditions, mechanism hypothesis, scope and exclusions;
- affected interests and values in tension;
- possible actions, likely trade-offs, and cautions;
- competing readings;
- uncertainty and evidence gaps;
- derivation method and prompt/model/tool versions if assisted.

### Stage 6 — adversarial review

Reviewers attempt to break the pattern:

- Is it a generic platitude?
- Does it merely restate one famous quotation?
- Does it flatten distinct traditions or periods?
- Are source roles mixed?
- Does a counterexample reverse the recommendation?
- Can a strong grounded-RAG response achieve the same result without storing the pattern?
- Could it enable blame, fatalism, caste/gender hierarchy, abuse, or false authority?
- Are proposed actions actually entailed, or editorial invention?

Status is `candidate`, `reviewed_internal`, `published`, `deprecated`, or `rejected`. Rejected material stays available for research history but is never retrieved to users as advice.

### Stage 7 — evaluation and publication

Patterns and cases earn product use only through held-out scenario evaluation against the strong-RAG baseline. Publish a specific version with named review roles, scope, evidence set, evaluation results, and expiry/review trigger.

## 5. Offline versus inference-time synthesis

### Synthesize offline

- source/expression/edition identity and structural maps;
- passage-aligned claims and procedure steps;
- ritual applicability and deterministic decision rules;
- researched story/case dossiers;
- conflicts and interpretation maps;
- reviewed pattern and counter-pattern records;
- multilingual terminology/alignment decisions;
- safety boundaries and authority/deference rules;
- evaluation scenarios, rubrics, and expected evidence.

These objects recur, need review, and must be stable enough for audit.

### Synthesize at inference time

- query classification and current intent;
- decision-relevant context gaps;
- retrieval plan and evidence bundle;
- which reviewed interpretations/cases/patterns fit the current situation;
- explicit similarity/disanalogy to the user's situation;
- stakeholder/timescale comparison;
- proportionate wording and next action;
- concise rationale and uncertainty statement.

These depend on the current user and should not become general knowledge automatically.

### Never auto-promote

- a model's novel analogy;
- a user-specific inference;
- an inference-time “lesson”;
- a generated translation;
- an unreviewed conflict resolution;
- model self-confidence;
- conversation content lacking explicit consent.

Promotion requires a new offline candidate with provenance and review.

## 6. Contradiction and uncertainty policy

Do not force every disagreement into true/false. Record:

- `logical_conflict`: propositions cannot both hold in the same scope;
- `scope_difference`: both may hold for different time/place/tradition/setting;
- `source_variant`: witnesses or editions differ;
- `interpretive_difference`: readers disagree about meaning or consequence;
- `practice_variation`: communities or institutions do different things;
- `freshness_conflict`: current facts changed or source dates differ;
- `evidence_quality_difference`: claims rest on different source roles;
- `apparent_conflict_unresolved`: insufficient analysis.

For every conflict, store the compared proposition IDs, shared/different scopes, evidence, editorial status, and response policy: choose by user scope, present variants, ask a question, defer, or report unresolved.

Uncertainty also needs typed causes: missing source, partial source, rights-limited quotation, extraction doubt, translation ambiguity, attribution dispute, applicability ambiguity, current-information expiry, model routing uncertainty, or genuine normative underdetermination.

## 7. Translation and terminology

- Preserve the original term and source language.
- Attach translations to an identified translator/model, date, method, source passage, review state, and licence.
- Maintain term-level alternatives and tradition-specific senses; avoid one English label silently collapsing `dharma`, `karma`, `bhakti`, `jnana`, `viveka`, `vairagya`, `pramana`, or `seva`.
- A translation may support retrieval but cannot be cited as the original.
- Hindi and English product versions must preserve the same scope, uncertainty, and authority boundaries even when wording differs.

## 8. Quality and coverage measures

Track separately:

- full source acquisition and structural reconciliation;
- source-role and rights eligibility;
- claim/evidence coverage;
- interpretation and counterevidence diversity;
- case-dimension completeness and coder agreement;
- pattern support/counterexample/reviewer completeness;
- ritual contract coverage by named applicability lane;
- retrieval/evaluation performance;
- known gaps.

Do not use node count, pattern count, citation density, or source-provider count as a proxy for a complete library or a wise product.

## 9. First-vertical acquisition packet

For a narrow family/duty/fear/conflict vertical using Bhagavadgita and Ramayana lenses, the minimum defensible packet is:

1. complete product-usable primary expressions for the selected passages and their surrounding narrative/argument units;
2. at least two materially distinct, attributable commentary lanes where interpretation affects guidance;
3. case dossiers that include narrative context, actors, relationships, constraints, actions, outcomes, and disputed readings;
4. explicit countercases and failed analogies;
5. contemporary scholarship that prevents anachronistic or decontextualised use;
6. living-practice evidence only under a separately authorised ethical protocol;
7. synthetic evaluation scenarios, never served as tradition evidence;
8. a documented completeness/gap statement.

Until those conditions hold, the vertical can run as internal research with private or citation-only evidence, but not claim product-complete source-grounded guidance.
