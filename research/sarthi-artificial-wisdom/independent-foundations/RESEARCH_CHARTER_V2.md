# Independent foundations charter: Artificial Wisdom and adjacent cognitive capabilities

**Programme start:** 2026-08-07  
**Status:** active, first-principles research  
**Relationship to prior work:** the files above this directory are preserved as an implementation-oriented first hypothesis. They are evidence about one application design, not premises of this programme.

## 1. Mission

Develop a domain-general, scientifically and philosophically defensible theory and candidate architecture for Artificial Wisdom, while separately defining and architecting the adjacent capabilities with which wisdom is commonly confused:

- intelligence;
- expertise;
- insight;
- rationality;
- judgment;
- morality;
- prudence;
- common sense;
- charisma and persuasive expression.

The general architecture must be defined independently of Devam, Sārthi, RAG, Postgres, or any current implementation. Sārthi will later be treated as one vertical instantiation: a system intended to carry and exercise wisdom grounded in Indian religions, cultures, and knowledge traditions.

## 2. The corrected research question

The programme is not asking only how to make an LLM answer safely or helpfully. It asks:

> What is wisdom as a human, philosophical, psychological, social, and practical phenomenon; which components are distinct from intelligence and knowledge; which components are computationally realizable; and what architecture could reliably generate, select, apply, communicate, and learn from insights and judgments across domains and time?

Three targets remain separate:

1. **wise-looking output** — language perceived as profound, compassionate, confident, or authoritative;
2. **wisdom-like process** — a process that integrates evidence, models, values, perspectives, uncertainty, experience, and consequences;
3. **wise system performance** — stable, transferable, self-correcting, outcome-sensitive performance across unfamiliar situations and time.

A system does not qualify at level 3 merely because it sometimes produces level-1 outputs.

## 3. Independence and anti-bias protocol

### Conceptual firewall

During the foundations phases:

- do not use the current application schema, stack, or roadmap as a criterion for what wisdom is;
- do not assume that RAG, a knowledge graph, an LLM, a case library, agents, or any particular database is the answer;
- do not reject an architecture as “overengineered” before stating the cognitive function it attempts to realize;
- do not define the construct by what current models can conveniently implement;
- do not treat existing Sārthi documents as scientific evidence about wisdom;
- do not use religious or cultural vocabulary as branding for ordinary software functions;
- do not turn philosophical disagreement into premature engineering consensus.

### Controlled application phase

Only after a domain-general theory, component model, architecture space, and evaluation programme exist may the research ask:

1. Which components does Sārthi need?
2. What Indian religious/cultural knowledge and forms of practical reasoning instantiate them?
3. Which current Devam capabilities can host them?
4. What must be changed or newly built?

Application fit can select among otherwise defensible alternatives; it cannot retroactively define wisdom.

## 4. Competing hypotheses to investigate

No hypothesis is privileged at the outset.

| ID | Hypothesis | What would support it | What would challenge it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Wisdom is accumulated life expertise. | performance rises with diverse, reflected experience and case discrimination | experts remain narrow, biased, unethical, or poor outside their domain |
| H2 | Wisdom is meta-pattern recognition. | cross-domain abstractions predict/explain novel cases better than local models | brilliant insights fail to yield sound action or humane ends |
| H3 | Wisdom is superior rationality under uncertainty. | coherence, calibration, decision quality, and resource allocation explain wise judgments | rationally coherent agents pursue foolish or immoral ends |
| H4 | Wisdom is moral virtue or right orientation. | good character/values reliably govern knowledge and action | good intention without understanding produces harmful judgment |
| H5 | Wisdom is practical judgment or phronesis. | context-specific selection among competing goods/rules explains expert exemplars | construct remains culturally/normatively underdetermined or unmeasurable |
| H6 | Wisdom is epistemic humility and metacognitive regulation. | knowing limits and when to defer predicts fewer catastrophic errors | humility alone becomes indecision or lacks generative insight |
| H7 | Wisdom is prosocial, relational, and perspective-integrating. | coordination of affected interests and compassionate action predicts judgments | false balance or social conformity substitutes for truth |
| H8 | Wisdom requires embodiment, emotion, responsibility, and lived consequence. | disembodied systems fail durable transfer, salience, care, or accountability | functional substitutes reproduce the relevant performance |
| H9 | Wisdom is a meta-governor over distinct cognitive capacities. | no single component suffices, but their context-sensitive orchestration predicts performance | “meta-governor” merely renames unexplained competence |
| H10 | Wisdom is a family-resemblance construct, not one essence. | different traditions/tasks share overlapping but non-universal properties | a smaller causal core explains the variance and transfer |

The programme may conclude that Artificial Wisdom is a graded functional achievement, a composite architecture, a socio-technical process, or not fully realizable by present systems. It must not force a positive answer.

## 5. Research domains

### Human and philosophical foundations

- philosophy of wisdom, practical wisdom, virtue, prudence, judgment, and the good life;
- psychology of wisdom, expertise, insight, metacognition, decision competence, moral cognition, and common sense;
- cognitive science of abstraction, analogy, causal learning, transfer, memory, intuition, reflection, and skill;
- anthropology and sociology of elders, practical authority, cultural models, traditions, and collective wisdom;
- histories and philosophies of Indian, Greek, Chinese, Buddhist, Jain, Islamic, African, Indigenous, and other traditions without forcing equivalence;
- neuroscience only where it adds causal or discriminating evidence rather than decorative localization.

### Computational foundations

- artificial general intelligence definitions and rational-agent models;
- cognitive architectures, neuro-symbolic and model-based systems;
- causal, analogical, case-based, program-induction, and abstraction learning;
- continual, meta-, curriculum, active, and reinforcement learning;
- world models, social models, value learning, preference learning, constitutional and deliberative methods;
- metacognition, uncertainty, calibration, self-models, tool use, planning, and verification;
- collective intelligence, multi-agent institutions, debate, markets, and governance;
- memory systems, outcome tracking, counterfactual audit, regret, and temporal credit assignment;
- persuasion, charisma, explanation, trust, and detection of profound-sounding nonsense.

## 6. Evidence discipline

Each material claim must be labelled as one of:

- **EMP:** empirical evidence;
- **META:** systematic review or meta-analysis;
- **PHIL:** philosophical argument or conceptual analysis;
- **HIST:** historical/primary-source interpretation;
- **TECH:** demonstrated computational method;
- **CONV:** professional or technical convention;
- **HYP:** testable programme hypothesis;
- **SYN:** cross-domain synthesis produced by this programme.

The evidence register records date, stable link/DOI, exact bounded finding, limitations, construct relevance, and whether the source is being used descriptively or normatively.

Current model claims require exact model/version, task, date, and experimental conditions. Future model capability is not evidence.

## 7. Core research questions

1. Are the target constructs latent traits, processes, outputs, social attributions, or families of capabilities?
2. What are their necessary, sufficient, typical, and confounding features?
3. How can each be measured without relying on self-report, eloquence, outcome luck, or culturally narrow gold answers?
4. Which capacities are domain-general and which require deep domain apprenticeship?
5. How are abstraction, meta-patterns, analogy, causal explanation, and novel insight generated and validated?
6. How do expertise and common sense supply priors while avoiding rigidity and majority bias?
7. How do rationality, morality, and prudence constrain or conflict with one another?
8. What makes judgment sensitive to particulars without becoming inconsistent?
9. What roles do emotion, embodiment, identity, social responsibility, time, and consequence play?
10. Can functional substitutes for human lived experience be built from episodic memory, simulation, feedback, and institutional review?
11. How can a system learn from outcomes without confusing correlation, popularity, reward, or hindsight with wisdom?
12. What architecture separates insight generation, validation, selection, action, and persuasive communication?
13. What failure tests distinguish wise performance from intelligence, expertise, moral compliance, common-sense imitation, or charisma?
14. Is Artificial Wisdom best located in a model, a modular system, an agent over time, or a human–AI institution?
15. What common core can support multiple vertical applications without imposing one culture's values or ontology?

## 9. Success and failure conditions

The programme succeeds if it produces:

- a discriminating taxonomy rather than synonyms;
- a definition of wisdom that explains both admired exemplars and obvious counterexamples;
- an architecture whose components correspond to falsifiable cognitive functions;
- tests that can distinguish a wise process from a persuasive imitation;
- a domain-instantiation contract that supports Sārthi and unrelated verticals;
- explicit limits where no defensible computational substitute is known.

It fails if it:

- starts with the present Sārthi implementation and generalizes its components;
- equates wisdom with RAG, safety, politeness, or multi-step reasoning;
- rewards profound language without predictive, explanatory, or decision value;
- treats morality as one universal list or rationality as sufficient;
- hides normative choices in system prompts, reward models, reviewer labels, or database schemas;
- claims that a current LLM possesses wisdom because it passes curated conversational tests.
