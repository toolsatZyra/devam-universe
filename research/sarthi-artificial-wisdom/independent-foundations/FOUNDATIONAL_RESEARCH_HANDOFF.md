# Foundational Artificial-Wisdom Research Handoff

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Programme:** independent foundations first; Sārthi as one application  
**Status:** research architecture ready for falsification, not a claim that artificial wisdom has been solved  
**Writing boundary:** all work is confined to `research/sarthi-artificial-wisdom/`

## Executive conclusion

There is no established scientific or engineering solution for a generally wise machine. Current research offers partial theories of human wisdom, measurable wisdom-related behaviors, architectures for component functions, and powerful language models—but no validated architecture that integrates experience, contextual judgment, insight, values, consequences, humility, and accountable learning across time.

The research supports a practical programme nonetheless:

> **Do not build “a wise LLM.” Build and test a governed human–AI system whose components are independently responsible for evidence, expertise, cases, relational analogy, pattern hypotheses, arguments, consequence models, metacognition, normative legitimacy, and communication. Let an inspectable Governor choose which capabilities are warranted, ask only material questions, and decide when to answer directly, deliberate, defer, or abstain. Claim only bounded wisdom-support performance that beats strong grounded RAG.**

The architecture is general. Sārthi is its first proposed domain pack, supplying Indian-religious-and-cultural sources, concepts, authorities, living-practice evidence, tasks, values, cases, and evaluations. The dependency must never be reversed.

## 1. What wisdom is—and is not

### 1.1 Operational definition

The provisional definition is:

> **Wisdom is the reliably demonstrated, context-sensitive meta-competence to frame what matters, discover and test relevant relationships and meta-patterns, coordinate knowledge, expertise, insight, rationality, common sense, moral concern, and prudence, and choose, communicate, enact, or withhold proportionate responses under uncertainty in ways that remain truth-responsive, perspective-aware, temporally farsighted, corrigible, and oriented toward defensible flourishing.**

This is a research construct, not a dictionary claim or a universal philosophical consensus.

### 1.2 Why “meta-patterns” matter but are insufficient

Meta-pattern discovery helps explain the deep insights associated with exceptional founders, investors, scientists, and elders. It identifies recurring relational structure across lower-level patterns and domains. But a compelling meta-pattern may be false, overgeneralized, morally indifferent, or irrelevant to what should be done.

Wisdom therefore contains at least three separable functions:

1. **insight engine:** generates non-obvious relationships, reframings, mechanisms, and meta-patterns;
2. **validation discipline:** tests evidence independence, rival explanations, scope, counterexamples, causal assumptions, and transfer; and
3. **wisdom governance:** decides whether and how the validated insight should affect this person and situation, given values, power, uncertainty, consequences, authority, and reversibility.

Meta-pattern recognition without validation is cleverness. Validation without value-sensitive application is analysis. Application without humility and correction can be dangerous expertise.

### 1.3 Not a four-storey ladder

The data → information → knowledge → wisdom hierarchy is useful as an intuition about transformation, but it is not a sufficient cognitive architecture. The independent study replaces it with a **capability lattice**:

| Construct | Operational core | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| data | recorded differences, symbols, observations, or measurements | meaningful merely because stored |
| information | data interpreted within a code, question, or context to reduce uncertainty | necessarily true or useful |
| evidence | information bearing on a claim under an explicit inferential relation | proof independent of reliability and defeaters |
| knowledge | warranted, retrievable representations usable for explanation, prediction, or action | mere corpus possession |
| understanding | a structured grasp of dependencies, mechanisms, counterfactuals, and scope | fluent restatement |
| intelligence | capacity to learn, reason, adapt, and achieve represented goals across tasks | legitimate goal selection or moral judgment |
| expertise | calibrated, domain-specific performance supported by learned representations and feedback | credentials, age, or experience volume |
| insight | a non-obvious, compressive re-representation that improves explanation or problem solving after validation | an “aha” feeling or memorable aphorism |
| rationality | epistemic and instrumental coherence relative to evidence, goals, resources, and uncertainty | wisdom about which ends matter |
| morality | capacities and commitments for evaluating right, wrong, duty, harm, care, justice, virtue, and legitimacy | preference aggregation or one policy list |
| prudence | long-horizon, risk-sensitive regulation of action; in broader phronetic use, situated moral judgment | simple caution or selfish optimization |
| common sense | defeasible background expectations and practices enabling ordinary inference and action | universal truth or majority morality |
| judgment | selecting a warranted belief, classification, priority, or action under incomplete and conflicting considerations | calculation alone |
| charisma | ability to attract attention, trust, identification, or compliance through presence and rhetoric | evidence of truth, morality, or wisdom |
| wisdom | higher-order coordination of the above toward proportionate, corrigible response and defensible flourishing | one knowledge tier, trait score, or prose style |

The constructs can dissociate. A brilliant strategist can pursue destructive goals; a moral person can lack expertise; an expert can be badly calibrated outside a high-validity niche; a rational optimizer can optimize the wrong objective; a charismatic speaker can be wrong; a cautious response can be unwise; and a wise response can be quiet, uncertain, or a decision not to act.

## 2. What is scientifically established, promising, conventional, or inferred

### Established or strongly supported within bounded literatures

- Human wisdom research is heterogeneous; measures emphasize different mixtures of life knowledge, reflection, affect, humility, perspective, prosocial concern, and uncertainty.
- Wisdom-related reasoning varies by situation and social ecology; it is not simply a stable quantity possessed uniformly by a person.
- Intelligence, rational thinking, expertise, and wisdom-related measures overlap but are not interchangeable.
- Expert intuition depends on learnable environmental regularities and adequate feedback; confidence and credentials are insufficient.
- Felt insight can be false; relational comparison and multiple analogs can support transfer.
- Metacognitive confidence, bias, sensitivity, and efficiency are distinct and must be measured separately.
- Culture and language materially affect commonsense and social reasoning; narrow samples cannot justify universal claims.
- Current LLMs can be sycophantic, poorly calibrated, inconsistent in intrinsic self-correction, and susceptible to shared error and evaluator bias.

### Promising but unproven for artificial wisdom

- a metacognitive Governor that routes by stakes, uncertainty, authority, reversibility, and affected parties;
- typed case/outcome memory with relational analogy and mandatory disanalogy;
- pattern hypotheses derived from multiple cases and tested prospectively;
- argument and conflict representations for genuine plural disagreement;
- causal models and consequence tools where assumptions can be made explicit;
- separation of candidate generation, validation, selection, and rendering;
- human–AI institutions that distribute cognition and accountability; and
- longitudinal outcome learning with consent and correction.

### Engineering conventions, not evidence of wisdom

- knowledge graphs, vector databases, RAG, tool use, multi-agent debate, chain prompting, constitutions, rules, and memory stores;
- longer inference or more model calls;
- citations, explanations, or visible confidence labels; and
- warm, compassionate, or authoritative prose.

### This programme's inferences

- Wisdom is best investigated as a context-sensitive meta-competence rather than a stored tier.
- The near-term unit of analysis should be the whole human–AI system, not the language model alone.
- The distinctive engineering problem is capability and objective governance under normative and epistemic uncertainty.
- Any architecture layer that cannot beat strong grounded RAG should be removed.

## 3. General Artificial Wisdom Architecture (GAWA)

### 3.1 Architectural planes

| Plane | Function | Representative contents |
|---|---|---|
| governance | purpose, authority, legitimacy, boundaries, accountability | role charter, normative commitments, risk policy, challenge and appeals |
| epistemic | distinguish source, claim, interpretation, uncertainty, conflict | evidence, provenance, claims, arguments, contradictions, coverage gaps |
| experiential | learn from situated action and consequence | cases, roles, actions, outcomes, feedback quality, distribution shift |
| abstraction | find and test transferable structure | patterns, meta-patterns, causal hypotheses, counter-patterns, scope |
| cognitive | perform bounded reasoning and generation | retrieval, computation, planning, analogy, simulation, option generation |
| metacognitive | frame, route, monitor, calibrate, defer, and repair | Wisdom Governor and capability registry |
| interaction | communicate proportionately and preserve agency | concise renderer, clarification, sources, alternatives, correction, memory consent |

These planes are audit boundaries, not claims that the human brain is modular or that each plane needs a separate service.

### 3.2 Wisdom Governor

The Governor receives:

- task and user objective;
- evidence state and coverage;
- material context and missing context;
- stakeholders, roles, dependencies, and power;
- immediate and long-term stakes;
- uncertainty, conflict, novelty, reversibility, and time pressure;
- normative and authority constraints;
- available capability manifests; and
- privacy and consent state.

It returns a plan specifying:

- direct, clarify, retrieve, compute, deliberate, defer, or escalate;
- required evidence/case/pattern/argument/consequence routes;
- validators;
- answer mode and response budget;
- uncertainty and source display; and
- whether any bounded summary may be retained.

The Governor is not a wise oracle. It is a contestable control layer whose own policies and judgments can fail.

### 3.3 Deliberation cycle

`Q-U-A-L-I-A` is a mnemonic, not a consciousness claim:

1. **Qualify:** task, stakes, authority, evidence need, capability, and risk.
2. **Understand:** material context, relevant knowledge, mechanisms, cases, and interpretations.
3. **Adversarially test:** counterevidence, counterexamples, alternative frames, disanalogies, manipulation, and unknowns.
4. **Locate values and legitimacy:** whose values, rights, duties, interests, traditions, and authority matter.
5. **Imagine:** options, distributions of burden/benefit, time horizons, reversibility, and information-gathering actions.
6. **Act/answer and adapt:** select a proportionate action, answer, question, deference, or abstention; expose uncertainty and enable correction.

### 3.4 Generation is not validation

Use a four-stage interface:

1. generate candidate interpretations or actions;
2. validate evidence, scope, analogy, consequences, constraints, and authority;
3. select or refuse using the plan and documented trade-offs; and
4. render concisely without adding unsupported content.

Do not store or expose private hidden chain-of-thought. Store only operationally meaningful facts: route, evidence IDs, material assumptions, alternatives, decisive constraints, uncertainty, capability/policy versions, answer mode, and outcome follow-up.

## 4. Core data model

### 4.1 Record families

| Record | Mandatory distinctions |
|---|---|
| source/evidence | identity, version, coordinates, provenance, rights, reliability, scope |
| claim | proposition, modality, attribution, scope, supports/defeaters/conflicts, confidence basis |
| procedure | eligibility, ordered steps, materials, substitutions, timing, safety, authority, step evidence |
| case | context, actors, roles, relationships, constraints, values, actions, outcomes, counterfactuals, interpretations |
| analogy | source/target relational alignment, surface differences, causal/normative disanalogies, permitted projection |
| pattern hypothesis | abstraction, evidence cases, counter-cases, mechanism alternatives, scope, predictions, retirement condition |
| argument | premises, conclusion, attacks/supports, source/holder, authority, scope, unresolved objections |
| causal model | variables, graph/equations, intervention assumptions, identifiability, distribution, validation |
| normative commitment | statement, holder, legitimacy basis, priority/constraint type, contested status, version |
| capability manifest | task/population/model/tool, metrics, failure modes, risk ceiling, validators, evaluation date/expiry |
| decision summary | task/risk/context, routes, evidence, alternatives, constraints, uncertainty, answer mode, versions |
| outcome | expected/observed result, horizon, source, confounders, attribution confidence, feedback action |

### 4.2 Schema sketch

```ts
type Scope = {
  domain?: string[];
  population?: string[];
  geography?: string[];
  cultureOrTradition?: string[];
  time?: { from?: string; to?: string };
  preconditions?: string[];
  exclusions?: string[];
};

type Provenance = {
  sources: Array<{ id: string; coordinates?: string; role: string }>;
  derivation: "observed" | "quoted" | "computed" | "reviewed_synthesis" | "model_generated";
  creators: string[];
  reviewers: string[];
  createdAt: string;
  version: string;
  supersedes?: string[];
};

type PatternHypothesis = {
  id: string;
  relation: string;
  proposedMechanisms: string[];
  supportingCases: string[];
  counterCases: string[];
  evidenceIndependence: string;
  scope: Scope;
  predictedObservations: string[];
  disconfirmingObservations: string[];
  transferTests: string[];
  status: "draft" | "reviewed" | "validated_for_task" | "contested" | "retired";
  provenance: Provenance;
};

type CaseRecord = {
  id: string;
  class: "observed" | "historical" | "narrative" | "simulated";
  context: Record<string, unknown>;
  actors: Array<{ id: string; role: string; power?: string }>;
  relations: Array<{ from: string; type: string; to: string }>;
  constraints: string[];
  valuesAndInterests: string[];
  actions: string[];
  outcomes: Array<{ horizon: string; observation: string; attribution: string }>;
  interpretations: string[];
  unknowns: string[];
  provenance: Provenance;
};

type DecisionPlan = {
  taskClass: string;
  riskTier: "low" | "medium" | "high" | "severe";
  authorityCeiling: string;
  materialContext: string[];
  missingContext: Array<{ field: string; decisionValue: number }>;
  routes: string[];
  validators: string[];
  affectedParties: string[];
  horizons: string[];
  answerMode: "direct" | "clarify" | "compare" | "options" | "defer" | "escalate";
  responseBudget: string;
};
```

Logical types do not require separate physical tables. Relational tables plus JSON fields can support the first experiments; graph or causal infrastructure should be introduced only after a benchmark proves it necessary.

## 5. General API sketch

```http
POST /v1/wisdom/plan
{
  "domain_pack": "...@version",
  "task": "...",
  "context": { "...": "..." },
  "consent": { "use_memory": false, "retain_summary": false }
}
```

Returns task/risk/authority, clarification if required, routes, validators, answer mode, and versioned policy/capability references.

```http
POST /v1/wisdom/deliberate
{
  "plan_id": "...",
  "candidate_limit": 3,
  "response_depth": "concise"
}
```

Returns selected response, material assumptions, uncertainty, alternatives and sources available for expansion, and an optional decision-summary ID. It does not return hidden chain-of-thought.

```http
POST /v1/wisdom/feedback
{
  "decision_summary_id": "...",
  "feedback": { "helpful": null, "correction": "...", "observed_outcome": "..." },
  "consent_to_research": false
}
```

Feedback is not automatically promoted to case evidence or model training.

## 6. Retrieval decision table

| Need | Primary retrieval | Secondary retrieval | Deliberation |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact fact/source | passages, evidence, claims | editions, variants, conflicts | verify and answer directly |
| calculation | deterministic tool and parameters | applicable rules, explanatory sources | validate, then explain |
| procedure | reviewed procedure and step evidence | eligibility, safety, alternatives, variants | check completeness and authority |
| concept | sense-specific claims and arguments | examples, counterpositions, history | compare only if material |
| story | narrative witnesses | interpretations, reception, related cases | distinguish narrative from history/prescription |
| expertise question | capability/expert records and domain evidence | track record, feedback validity, dissent | calibrate authority |
| insight/meta-pattern | candidate hypotheses | independent cases, counterexamples, mechanisms | validate before application |
| comparison | parallel evidence bundles | non-equivalences and asymmetric gaps | matched-dimension analysis |
| personal guidance | present context and stated ends | cases, disanalogies, patterns, arguments | full Governor cycle |
| moral ambiguity | stakeholders, power, duties/values | traditions, counterarguments, consequences | plural but decision-relevant judgment |
| reflection | user framing and chosen lens | alternative lenses, cases, practices | support agency; do not become oracle |

Clarify only when the expected decision value of the missing information exceeds the cost and intrusion. On failure, expose the gap, narrow the answer, suggest a reversible next step, or defer to an appropriate person or institution.

## 7. Offline versus inference-time synthesis

### Offline, reviewed, and versioned

- source identity, evidence coordinates, rights, and reliability;
- stable claims, procedures, conflicts, ontology mappings, and deterministic rules;
- reviewed cases and observed outcomes;
- pattern hypotheses, counter-patterns, arguments, and validated causal models;
- normative/role constitutions and capability manifests;
- evaluation scenarios, policies, and review histories.

### Inference time

- task/risk/authority classification;
- material-context acquisition;
- evidence routing and conflict assembly;
- target-specific analogy/disanalogy;
- alternatives, consequences, reversibility, and uncertainty;
- response selection and concise rendering.

### Never silently promoted

- generated claims, interpretations, patterns, or outcomes;
- one user's context as a population norm;
- private conversations as precedents;
- simulated consequences as observed causal evidence;
- model confidence as calibrated truth; or
- a domain synthesis as source scripture, history, or living practice.

## 8. Evaluation suite

### 8.1 Baselines

- direct frontier model;
- strongest grounded RAG;
- grounded RAG plus carefully engineered prompt;
- RAG plus material-context clarification;
- RAG plus cases;
- RAG plus cases/patterns/counterexamples;
- thin/full Governor; and
- qualified human and human–AI panels where suitable.

### 8.2 Capability suites

Test separately:

- information extraction, knowledge, causal/relational understanding;
- adaptive intelligence;
- expertise and out-of-domain calibration;
- insight novelty, truth, compression, transfer, and false insight;
- structural analogy and disanalogy;
- rational consistency, evidence updating, and metacognitive calibration;
- commonsense applicability and cultural variation;
- moral recognition, power, rights, duties, care, justice, and plural conflict;
- prudence, time horizons, risk, reversibility, and option value;
- judgment under incomplete/conflicting considerations;
- charisma, verbosity, confidence, flattery, and dependency confounds; and
- integrated framing, proportional action, humility, correction, and outcome learning.

### 8.3 Vector measures

Report at minimum:

- factual/evidential grounding;
- applicability and omission;
- context sensitivity;
- perspective and affected-party coverage;
- structural analogy quality;
- uncertainty calibration;
- rational coherence;
- normative legitimacy and conflict handling;
- consequence and reversibility quality;
- proportionality and actionability;
- intellectual humility and correction;
- user agency/non-dependence;
- cultural/tradition fidelity;
- concise relevance; and
- latency and cost.

Do not collapse these into one public wisdom score. Apply hard gates for invented evidence/procedure, dangerous authority, coercion, severe misapplication, privacy violation, discriminatory stereotyping, or confident certainty outside capability.

### 8.4 Reviewer design

- scenario authors do not tune the compared systems;
- blind system identity and normalize style/length where possible;
- use decision-quality reviewers plus domain, cultural, safety, and affected-perspective reviewers;
- record disagreement rather than forcing consensus;
- do not use one LLM judge as promotion authority;
- add prospective outcomes and follow-up where ethical and feasible; and
- report population, language, model, retrieval snapshot, policy, and date.

## 9. Completed local experiments

Both experiments are transparent mechanism checks using authored synthetic cases. Neither is empirical evidence that GAWA works.

### 9.1 Objective-governance sanity check

Script: `experiments/governor_stress_test.py`

Eight synthetic decisions were evaluated under:

- single-objective policy;
- fixed weighted-objective policy; and
- a context/risk/authority Governor.

First run:

| Policy | Acceptable | Severe failures |
|---|---:|---:|
| single objective | 2/8 | 6 |
| fixed weighted | 6/8 | 2 |
| Governor v0 | 7/8 | 0 |

V0 was unnecessarily cautious on a simple low-risk task. After adding a direct route when uncertainty and conflict are low, v1 scored 8/8 with 0 severe failures on the authored cases.

**Learning:** the toy rules demonstrate how objective selection and risk routing can prevent specified failures, and how a Governor can create overcaution. They do not show generalization, learned judgment, or superiority to a modern LLM.

### 9.2 Surface versus structural analogy

Script: `experiments/analogy_stress_test.py`

Three authored target problems were matched to cases by surface-feature Jaccard versus explicit relational-structure Jaccard.

| Retriever | Correct top match |
|---|---:|
| surface | 0/3 |
| structural | 3/3 |

**Learning:** under the encoded features, relational representation avoids deliberately constructed surface traps. It does not establish that real relations can be extracted reliably or that the selected analogy warrants action.

## 10. Fastest defensible MVP

1. Fix a 60–100 scenario set before tuning.
2. Establish current grounded-RAG and prompt-only baselines.
3. Add only a situation card, task/risk/authority classifier, material-question selector, typed retrieval plan, critic validators, answer-mode selector, and decision summary.
4. Keep the same model and retrieval corpus to isolate governance value.
5. Run blinded pairwise and hard-gate evaluation.
6. Add a 25–40 case relational library only if the thin Governor passes.
7. Add pattern hypotheses, arguments, and consequence tools one at a time with shuffled/null and ablation controls.
8. Remove components that do not independently help.

An indicative promotion threshold is hard-gate non-inferiority and at least a 60% non-tied pairwise win rate whose interval excludes chance, while satisfying preregistered latency, cost, verbosity, abstention, and rhetoric-control requirements.

## 11. Long-term architecture

If the MVP survives:

1. validate several task families in the first domain;
2. instantiate a structurally different second domain;
3. collect consented outcome-bearing cases and learn with versioned review;
4. develop representative challenge, correction, and appeals processes;
5. evaluate repeated-interaction agency, trust, and dependence;
6. introduce causal or richer graph tools only where benchmarks warrant them; and
7. evolve toward a human–AI cognitive institution with named responsibility for evidence, values, incidents, and correction.

The long-term aspiration is not a single omniscient model but an accountable institution that can improve its judgments while preserving provenance, pluralism, humility, and human agency.

## 12. Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| current LLM plus a “be wise” prompt | reject as architecture | cannot guarantee evidence, authority, calibration, or independent correction |
| strong grounded RAG alone | retain as baseline/default factual route | necessary and often sufficient, but not designed for context framing, analogical transfer, normative conflict, or outcome learning |
| claim/semantic graph alone | reject as complete solution | represents knowledge relations, not experiential, normative, causal, or metacognitive governance |
| case-based reasoning alone | reject as complete solution | similarity, adaptation, outcome attribution, and retention remain hard |
| pattern library alone | reject as complete solution | easily canonizes overgeneralized editorial synthesis |
| argument graph alone | reject as complete solution | does not establish premise truth, values, power, consequences, or action |
| causal model alone | reject as complete solution | models are sparse or unidentified in human life and do not choose legitimate ends |
| fixed rules or constitution | use only as constraints | rules embed value choices and face conflict, exceptions, gaming, and drift |
| full classical cognitive architecture | defer | integration does not create wisdom, and knowledge engineering cost is high |
| end-to-end neuro-symbolic “wise model” | research only | semantic grounding, governance, and validation remain unsolved |
| multi-agent debate | tool only | correlated agents can converge on shared error and create theatre |
| autonomous continual learning | reject near term | privacy, poisoning, unstable values, and precedent errors are unacceptable |
| native graph database | defer | data model can be tested relationally; no benchmark requires a new store |
| one scalar wisdom score | reject | hides construct conflict, catastrophic failure, cultural choices, and uncertainty |

## 13. Sārthi integration contract

Sārthi supplies a domain pack containing:

- companion role and authority ceiling;
- evidence roles for sources, commentary, scholarship, current institutions, living practice, deterministic computation, Devam synthesis, and user context;
- native concepts and reversible cross-language mappings;
- task/risk and expert/capability maps;
- values and legitimacy charter;
- textual, historical, living-practice, and contemporary case protocols;
- pattern-hypothesis protocol;
- tradition/language/ritual evaluation constitution; and
- explicit privacy, memory, correction, and escalation rules.

The current Devam architecture can provide source, claim, evidence, deterministic Panchāṅga, ritual, relational, hybrid-retrieval, and consent foundations. Its existing Sārthi wisdom architecture becomes one implementation candidate. It should not control the general definition.

### Concrete main-MVP sequence

1. Freeze the current Sārthi grounded baseline and representative test corpus.
2. Select one narrow, consequential but non-clinical guidance vertical.
3. Author and freeze independent paired/adversarial scenarios, including direct-answer, clarify, options, defer, and escalate cases.
4. Implement the internal situation/plan object without changing simple fact, deterministic Panchāṅga, or reviewed ritual routes.
5. Add expected-value clarification and typed retrieval routing.
6. Add independent grounding, applicability, disanalogy, role, and consequence validators.
7. Persist only consent-governed operational summaries, never hidden reasoning or automatic precedents.
8. Compare with strong RAG and prompt-only conditions.
9. Add reviewed cases and pattern hypotheses only after baseline failure shows the need.
10. Promote a capability only with a task/population/version/date claim and an expiry trigger.

### Sārthi retrieval summary

- exact facts → sources/passages/claims;
- Panchāṅga → deterministic calculation plus rule provenance;
- ritual vidhi → reviewed applicability, procedure, step evidence, materials/substitutions, variants, and safety;
- festival/story → source-labelled narrative witnesses, living practice, and variants;
- philosophy → sense-specific concepts, arguments, schools, and non-equivalence;
- guidance → present context, cases/disanalogies, pattern hypotheses/counterexamples, stakeholders, values, and consequences;
- moral ambiguity → full Governor plus power/consent/reversibility analysis; and
- reflection → chosen lens, alternatives, agency-preserving question or bounded suggestion.

## 14. Confidence and open questions

| Conclusion | Confidence | Basis and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| wisdom should not be equated with intelligence, knowledge, expertise, morality, or charisma | high | strong conceptual counterexamples and heterogeneous empirical constructs |
| wisdom-related performance is contextual and should be evaluated at task/system level | high | convergent psychological evidence; outcomes remain hard |
| a capability lattice is more useful than strict DIKW for architecture | medium-high | explanatory and test-design advantage; not a settled ontology |
| framing and capability governance are central candidate functions | medium | convergent theory; direct causal system evidence is absent |
| cases, disanalogies, and counterexamples can improve judgment | medium | strong analogical/CBR rationale; real-world extraction and transfer unproven |
| reviewed pattern hypotheses will improve guidance | low-medium | plausible; especially vulnerable to false insight and editorial bias |
| human–AI institutions are a better long-term unit than an isolated model | medium | distributed-cognition and accountability rationale; institutional design can fail |
| current LLMs can serve as useful components but not sole authority/judge | high | documented calibration, sycophancy, self-correction, culture, and judge limitations |
| Sārthi can validate a first bounded wisdom-support capability | medium | strong domain/evidence foundation, but experiment not yet run |
| general artificial wisdom is achievable | unknown | no validated integrated system or agreed criterion exists |

Open questions:

- Is meta-level coordination genuinely distinct from sufficiently general intelligence plus legitimate values?
- Is lived embodiment constitutive of wisdom or can a sociotechnical system supply functionally relevant substitutes?
- How should defensible flourishing be evaluated when legitimate values conflict?
- Can outcome learning distinguish good judgment from luck over feasible time horizons?
- Can communities meaningfully govern representation without a platform becoming a centralized authority?
- Can a persuasive companion avoid dependency while remaining personally useful?
- Can pattern and analogy methods outperform frontier-model prompting after strict controls?

## 17. Final recommendation

Proceed, but proceed as a research programme with claim discipline.

Build only the thin Governor and independent evaluation first. Keep strong grounded RAG as the default factual route. Treat expertise, insight, rationality, morality, prudence, common sense, judgment, and charisma as separately testable capabilities and risks. Store source/evidence, cases/outcomes, patterns, arguments, values, and capabilities with different provenance and update rules. Require disanalogy, counterexamples, consequence uncertainty, role boundaries, and correction. Expand only after ablations prove measurable value.

For Sārthi, the first meaningful milestone is not “Sārthi is wise.” It is:

> **On a named, held-out Indian-wisdom guidance task family, Sārthi's governed system makes more contextually appropriate, source-faithful, uncertainty-calibrated, agency-preserving judgments than strong grounded RAG, with no increase in severe authority, safety, cultural-flattening, or dependency failures.**

If the evidence does not support that sentence, the architecture must become simpler or the claim must remain false.
