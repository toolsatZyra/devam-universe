# Independent evidence register: wisdom and adjacent capabilities

**Research cut-off:** 2026-08-07  
**URL verification date:** 2026-08-07  
**Scope:** domain-independent foundations for Artificial Wisdom; Sārthi is not used as a premise  
**Status:** living research register, not a claim that any architecture below has been validated

## Evidence policy

Codes used in this register:

- **EMP** — empirical study;
- **META** — systematic review or meta-analysis;
- **PHIL** — philosophical argument or conceptual analysis;
- **HIST** — historical or primary-source interpretation;
- **TECH** — implemented computational method with bounded evidence;
- **CONV** — technical or professional convention;
- **HYP** — testable hypothesis in this programme;
- **SYN** — synthesis made by this programme.

An entry records what a source can support and, equally importantly, what it cannot. Human self-report, perceived wisdom, performance on hypothetical dilemmas, expert consensus, and successful real-world action are different evidence classes. Likewise, an AI paper that proposes an architecture is not evidence that the architecture produces wisdom.

## A. The DIKW hypothesis and philosophical conceptions of wisdom

| ID | Type | Source | Bounded finding or argument | Limitation and use here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | PHIL | Frické (2009), [The Knowledge Pyramid](https://doi.org/10.1177/0165551508094050) | Critiques automatic transformations and weak definitions in the data–information–knowledge–wisdom hierarchy. | Does not supply a complete replacement. It establishes that DIKW is a hypothesis, not a discovered natural law. |
| F02 | HIST/PHIL | Bernstein (2011), [The DIKW Hierarchy and its Antithesis](https://doi.org/10.7152/nasko.v2i1.12806) | Traces the detailed hierarchy to Ackoff and analyses its filtering/transformation assumptions. | Information-science history; not a psychological theory of wisdom. |
| F03 | PHIL | [SEP: Wisdom, Spring 2025](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2025/entries/wisdom/) | Surveys humility, accuracy, knowledge, hybrid, and rationality accounts; each faces counterexamples. | Analytic-philosophy survey, not cross-cultural consensus or empirical proof. It supplies rival theories. |
| F04 | PHIL | [SEP: Wisdom in Contemporary Analytic Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wisdom-analytic/) | Practical wisdom is often treated as conflict-sensitive coordination of virtues and particulars, with skill and experience playing contested roles. | One philosophical literature. Supports separating theoretical wisdom, practical wisdom, and ordinary attribution. |
| F05 | PHIL | [SEP: Understanding, Summer 2026](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2026/entries/understanding/) | Understanding is commonly linked to grasping explanatory or dependency relations, not merely possessing isolated true propositions. | Philosophical accounts disagree. Motivates an explicit understanding layer without defining it as wisdom. |
| F06 | PHIL | [SEP: Virtue Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-virtue/) | Epistemic evaluation can concern reliable and responsible intellectual agency, not only proposition-level justification. | Virtue language can become circular unless converted into observable dispositions and tests. |
| F07 | PHIL | Aristotle, summarized in [SEP: Aristotle's Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-ethics/) | Practical wisdom identifies context-appropriate means in concert with rightly formed ends; mere cleverness can serve bad ends. | Historically situated normative theory. It establishes a powerful distinction, not a universal moral target. |
| F08 | PHIL | [SEP: Virtue Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/) | Virtue ethics joins discernment, character, motives, emotion, relationships, and flourishing rather than reducing ethics to rule application. | Multiple virtue-ethical theories disagree. Useful as an architecture challenge, not a single value function. |
| F09 | PHIL | Sternberg (1998), [Balance Theory of Wisdom](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.4.347) | Proposes applying tacit knowledge toward a common good while balancing intrapersonal, interpersonal, and extrapersonal interests across time. | Normative and broad; “balance” can conceal power and injustice. Test as stakeholder/horizon coverage, not as automatic compromise. |
| F10 | PHIL | Swartwood (2013), [Wisdom as an Expert Skill](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10677-012-9367-2) | Develops wisdom as expert skill in living well rather than a store of propositions. | Philosophical account; expertise analogies inherit domain, feedback, and measurement problems. |
| F11 | PHIL | MacAskill, Bykvist & Ord (2020), [Moral Uncertainty](https://academic.oup.com/book/31934) | Develops methods for decision when uncertain which moral theory is correct. | Formal methods depend on contested intertheoretic comparison and do not discover moral truth. |
| F12 | PHIL | [SEP: Reflective Equilibrium, Spring 2026](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/reflective-equilibrium/) | Moral inquiry can iteratively revise case judgments, principles, and background theories toward considered coherence. | Coherence is not truth; starting judgments and social conditions can be biased. Useful as a versioned deliberation process. |

## B. Empirical psychology of wisdom

| ID | Type | Source | Bounded finding or argument | Limitation and use here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W01 | EMP/CONV | Baltes & Staudinger (2000), [Wisdom: A Metaheuristic](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.122) | Berlin paradigm scores factual and procedural life knowledge, lifespan contextualism, value relativism, and uncertainty management. | Measures judged think-aloud responses to life dilemmas; it may measure expertise about life more than a wise person or action. |
| W02 | EMP | Ardelt (2003), [Three-Dimensional Wisdom Scale](https://doi.org/10.1177/0164027503025003004) | Operationalizes cognitive, reflective, and affective dimensions in a 39-item self-report scale. | Sample was 180 older adults and the measure is self-report; unsuitable for AI self-certification. |
| W03 | META | Bangen, Meeks & Jeste (2013), [Defining and Assessing Wisdom](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3896261/) | Recurring components include pragmatic/social judgment, prosociality, reflection, uncertainty management, and emotional regulation. | Definitions and methods are heterogeneous and gerontology-heavy; recurrence does not establish necessity. |
| W04 | META | Staudinger & Glück (2011), [Psychological Wisdom Research](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.121208.131659) | Reviews personality, performance, and developmental approaches and their substantial differences. | Field-level synthesis, not a final construct. Supports multiple measurement methods. |
| W05 | EMP | Jeste et al. (2010), [Expert Delphi Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/geront/gnq022) | Experts distinguished wisdom from intelligence on most rated items and associated it with cognitive-emotional development and experience. | Only 30 experts completed phase 1; consensus reflects expert beliefs, not causal validation. |
| W06 | META | Dong, Weststrate & Fournier (2023), [Thirty Years of Psychological Wisdom Research](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221114096) | Meta-analyses find wisdom measures relate heterogeneously to age, intelligence, personality, narcissism, and well-being; results depend on measure type. | Correlational literature with construct heterogeneity. Strong evidence against using age, intelligence, or one self-report as a proxy. |
| W07 | EMP | Brienza et al. (2018), [Situated Wise Reasoning Scale](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000171) | Scenario-specific assessment captures intellectual humility, perspective, uncertainty/change, compromise, and outsider viewpoint with less global self-report bias. | Still self-report about recalled situations; supports state/task evaluation, not a stable trait claim. |
| W08 | SYN/CONV | Grossmann et al. (2020), [Common Wisdom Model](https://doi.org/10.1080/1047840X.2020.1750920) | Proposes perspectival metacognition plus moral aspirations as a common framework. | A unifying proposal, not a validated causal model; the moral component remains under-specified. |
| W09 | META | Grossmann (2017), [Wisdom in Context](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616672066) | Wise reasoning is markedly contextual rather than a uniformly expressed person trait. | Synthesis of a developing literature. Makes context perturbation and within-system variance mandatory. |
| W10 | META | Grossmann, Dorfman & Oakes (2020), [Wisdom as Social-Ecological](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copsyc.2019.07.010) | Wisdom-related reasoning varies with culture, region, socioeconomic setting, and whether problems are personal or social. | Does not mean all standards are relative. Supports evaluating person–situation–institution systems. |
| W11 | EMP | Grossmann & Kross (2014), [Exploring Solomon's Paradox](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535400) | People often reason more wisely about others' conflicts; self-distancing can reduce the asymmetry. | Laboratory/vignette evidence. Supports role-swap and self-interest perturbation tests. |
| W12 | EMP | Grossmann, Oakes & Santos (2019), [Emodiversity and Wise Reasoning](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000548) | Across observational, diary, and experimental studies, wisdom-related reasoning co-occurred with richer emotional experience, independent of emotional intensity. | Association and manipulation do not prove emotion is necessary. Rejects a simple “cold reason equals wisdom” assumption. |
| W13 | EMP | Grossmann et al. (2024), [The Wise Mind Balances Abstract and Concrete](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11226238/) | In North American/UK samples, both abstract and concrete representations related to humility, open-mindedness, perspective, and compromise. | Cultural range is limited; language-derived signals may partly reflect style. Supports level-switching, not abstraction alone. |
| W14 | EMP | Grossmann et al. (2024), [Cross-Cultural Dimensions of Wisdom Perception](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-50294-0) | Across 12 countries, perceived wisdom was largely organized by reflective orientation and socio-emotional awareness. | Measures attribution of wisdom to targets, not target behavior or outcomes; country samples are not exhaustive. |
| W15 | META | Lee et al. (2020), [Wisdom Interventions Meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7221873/) | Trials targeting components such as prosocial behavior, emotion regulation, or spirituality showed aggregate effects. | Heterogeneous interventions and measures; improving a component does not prove an integrated wisdom intervention. |
| W16 | PHIL/TECH agenda | Johnson et al. (2026), [Imagining and Building Wise Machines](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.002) | Defines machine wisdom as navigating intractable problems with task-level and metacognitive strategies and proposes research directions. | A position/research-agenda article, not an implemented or validated architecture; metacognition may be necessary but is not shown sufficient. |

## C. Intelligence, rationality, metacognition, and judgment

| ID | Type | Source | Bounded finding or argument | Limitation and use here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I01 | META | Deary (2012), [Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-120710-100353) | Psychometric intelligence captures stable individual differences with broad predictive relations and an extensively studied general factor. | Human psychometrics do not directly define machine intelligence, goals, morality, or wisdom. |
| I02 | EMP/CONV | Carroll (1993/2009), [Three-Stratum Theory](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511571312.018) | Hierarchical models distinguish narrow, broad, and general cognitive abilities. | Factor structure is descriptive of covariance and remains theoretically debated; do not reify a score as a mechanism. |
| I03 | EMP | Bickley, Keith & Wolfle (1995), [Test of Three-Stratum Theory](https://doi.org/10.1016/0160-2896(95)90013-6) | Confirmatory analysis supported much of the three-stratum structure across ages. | One psychometric study; structure does not capture ends, motivation, values, or practical judgment. |
| I04 | PHIL/TECH | Legg & Hutter (2007), [Universal Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-007-9079-x) | Formalizes intelligence as expected goal achievement across a distribution of environments. | Measure is incomputable in general and relative to goals/environment distribution; a perfectly capable optimizer can be unwise. |
| I05 | EMP/PHIL | Stanovich & West (2014), [Assessment of Rational Thinking](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1032814) | Argues and presents evidence that rational thinking includes dispositions/knowledge not adequately represented by IQ tests. | Boundary and measurement remain debated; rationality and intelligence overlap. Supports separate tests rather than categorical independence. |
| I06 | PHIL | [SEP: Bounded Rationality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bounded-rationality/) | Rationality theories differ over optimization, satisficing, procedures, and environmental/resource constraints. | No single normative standard emerges. Architecture must state which rationality is being evaluated. |
| I07 | PHIL/EMP | Lieder & Griffiths (2020), [Resource-Rational Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X1900061X) | Models cognition as optimizing expected utility of computation under limited time and resources. | Requires chosen utility and computational-cost models; explains efficiency, not moral ends or wisdom. |
| I08 | EMP/PHIL | Todd & Gigerenzer (2007), [Environments That Make Us Smart](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2007.00497.x) | Simple heuristics can be ecologically rational when matched to environmental structure. | A heuristic's success is conditional; “common sense” or simplicity is not universally rational. |
| I09 | EMP/CONV | Nelson & Narens (1994), [Metacognitive Framework](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lnarens/1994/Nelson%26Narens_Book%20Chapter_1994.pdf) | Distinguishes object-level cognition from meta-level monitoring and control. | Framework is not a wisdom model and monitoring can be inaccurate. It supplies an implementable control distinction. |
| I10 | META | Fleming (2024), [Metacognition and Confidence](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-022423-032425) | Separates confidence, uncertainty representation, metacognitive bias, sensitivity, and efficiency. | Mostly laboratory/perceptual paradigms; transfer to moral and life judgment must be tested. |
| I11 | EMP/CONV | Fleming & Lau (2014), [How to Measure Metacognition](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443) | Shows that calibration, discrimination, and signal-detection measures capture different properties. | Methodological review; no one metric proves self-knowledge. Supports a vector of meta-performance. |
| I12 | EMP | Baron & Hershey (1988), [Outcome Bias](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.54.4.569) | Observers rate identical decision processes differently after learning good versus bad outcomes. | Laboratory studies; motivates separate ex-ante process quality and ex-post outcome learning. |
| I13 | EMP | Kahneman & Klein (2009), [Conditions for Intuitive Expertise](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755) | Skilled intuition is plausible when environments contain learnable regularities and decision makers receive adequate practice and feedback; subjective confidence is insufficient. | Consensus synthesis, not a complete expertise test. Central to judging when experience can become reliable judgment. |
| I14 | META | Lerner et al. (2015), [Emotion and Decision Making](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115043) | Emotions influence judgment predictably and can help or harm depending on appraisal and context. | Broad review, not proof that artificial affect is needed. Requires affect-relevant appraisal without romanticizing “gut feeling.” |
| I15 | META/CRIT | Dunn, Dalgleish & Lawrence (2006), [Critical Evaluation of Somatic Markers](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2005.07.001) | Finds ambiguities and insufficient causal evidence in strong interpretations of the somatic-marker hypothesis. | Does not show emotion is irrelevant. Warns against claiming a simulated body signal creates wisdom. |

## D. Expertise, insight, abstraction, analogy, and meta-patterns

| ID | Type | Source | Bounded finding or argument | Limitation and use here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | META | Ericsson & Lehmann (1996), [Expert and Exceptional Performance](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.47.1.273) | Expert performance depends heavily on domain-specific adaptations, representations, and extended practice. | Research emphasizes measurable domains; it does not imply practice alone causes expertise or transfers broadly. |
| E02 | META | Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014), [Deliberate Practice Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810) | Deliberate practice explains meaningful but highly variable portions of performance; less in professions than in games/music/sport. | Study coding and interpretation were debated. Strong evidence against “enough practice is sufficient.” |
| E03 | META | Ericsson & Kintsch (1995), [Long-Term Working Memory](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.2.211) | Experts develop retrieval structures that make domain-relevant knowledge rapidly accessible during performance. | Mechanism is domain/task dependent; it supports structured access, not global wisdom. |
| E04 | EMP/PHIL | Tetlock (2005), [Expert Political Judgment](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7spbt) | Long-horizon geopolitical judgment by credentialed experts was often poor; thinking style and calibration mattered. | Specific domain, period, and outcome measures. Demonstrates that status and expertise labels do not guarantee judgment. |
| E05 | EMP | Mellers et al. (2015), [Identifying and Cultivating Superforecasters](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615577794) | Training, teaming, updating, and aggregation improved geopolitical forecasting in tournaments. | Forecasting is narrower than wisdom and benefits from resolvable events. Supplies calibration/outcome-learning methods. |
| E06 | META | Kounios & Beeman (2014), [Cognitive Neuroscience of Insight](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115154) | Insight involves nonobvious reinterpretation and processes unfolding before the sudden conscious experience. | Mainly constrained laboratory problems; does not establish depth, truth, or social value of real-world insights. |
| E07 | EMP | Danek & Wiley (2017), [False Insights](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.02077) | Incorrect solutions can produce Aha experiences; the phenomenology is multidimensional. | Specific puzzles. Establishes that felt obviousness or eloquence cannot validate an insight. |
| E08 | EMP | Salvi et al. (2016), [Insight Solutions and Accuracy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5035115/) | In tested problems, self-reported insight solutions were often more accurate than analytic ones, especially under deadlines. | Task-specific and does not eliminate false insights. Insight feeling is a fallible signal, not a warrant. |
| E09 | PHIL/EMP | Gentner (1983), [Structure-Mapping Theory](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0702_3) | Analogy preferentially aligns relational systems rather than surface attributes. | Requires explicit representations and does not validate projected inferences. Supplies a disanalogy requirement. |
| E10 | TECH | Falkenhainer, Forbus & Gentner (1989), [Structure-Mapping Engine](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(89)90077-5) | Implements structural alignment and systematicity-biased mappings. | Demonstrates a computational account of mapping, not wise analogy selection or moral transfer. |
| E11 | EMP | Gick & Holyoak (1980), [Analogical Problem Solving](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(80)90013-4) | Participants often failed to retrieve a useful distant analogy without a hint. | Laboratory problems. Shows that possessing a relevant case is not enough. |
| E12 | EMP | Gick & Holyoak (1983), [Schema Induction and Transfer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6) | Comparing two analogs promoted schema induction and later transfer more than single-example abstraction aids. | Controlled setting; schemas can overgeneralize. Motivates multiple cases plus counterexamples for meta-pattern induction. |
| E13 | SYN/META | Gentner (2010), [Bootstrapping the Mind](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6709.2010.01114.x) | Comparison and progressive alignment can create relational abstractions and project new inferences. | Cognitive theory with bounded evidence. Supports meta-pattern formation as a learning process, not an LLM-generated slogan. |
| E14 | SYN | Tenenbaum et al. (2011), [How to Grow a Mind](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1192788) | Proposes that compositional representations, Bayesian inference, and structured priors help explain rapid generalization. | Broad programme, not a settled unified theory. Relevant to learning abstractions and causal theories from sparse cases. |
| E15 | TECH/PHIL | Pearl (2013), [Structural Counterfactuals](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12065) | Structural causal models make intervention and counterfactual assumptions explicit. | Causal structures and parameters are rarely known in life guidance; cannot manufacture causal certainty from anecdotes. |

## E. Morality, prudence, common sense, culture, and collective cognition

| ID | Type | Source | Bounded finding or argument | Limitation and use here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | META | Malle (2021), [Moral Judgments](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-072220-104358) | Distinguishes evaluations, norm judgments, wrongness judgments, and blame, with different information requirements. | Primarily psychological taxonomy; does not choose correct norms. Prevents collapsing all morality into a binary label. |
| M02 | META | Bonnefon, Rahwan & Shariff (2024), [Moral Psychology of AI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-030123-113559) | Reviews people treating AI as moral agent, patient, and proxy, and documents unresolved attribution/governance questions. | Describes perceptions and behavior; does not establish machine moral status or moral competence. |

| M04 | EMP | Darnell, Fowers & Kristjánsson (2022), [Multifunction Assessment of Phronesis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2022.111684) | Attempts to operationalize constitutive, integrative, blueprint, and emotional-regulation functions. | Self-report/psychometric construct; architecture inspiration, not proof of a unified governor. |
| M05 | PHIL/TECH | Hadfield-Menell et al. | M06 | TECH | Bai et al. | M07 | TECH/EMP | Sorensen et al. | M11 | HIST/PHIL | [SEP: Confucius](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/confucius/) | Wisdom is embedded in moral cultivation, judgment of character/deeds, learning, ritual, and relational roles. | Textual traditions and interpretations are plural; not an algorithm or generic “Eastern wisdom.” |
| M12 | EMP/META | Nisbett et al. | M13 | META/CRIT | Henrich, Heine & Norenzayan (2010), [The WEIRDest People in the World](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X0999152X) | Shows that behavioral science often generalizes from unusually narrow Western samples and documents cross-population variation. | “WEIRD” itself is coarse. | M14 | HIST/COG | Hutchins (1995), [Cognition in the Wild](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/1881.001.0001) | Detailed navigation studies show cognition distributed across people, artifacts, procedures, and culture. | Ethnographic case, not proof all cognition is distributed. | M15 | TECH | McCarthy (1959), [Programs with Common Sense](https://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/mcc59/mcc59.html) | Early AI programme identifies open-ended, revisable background knowledge as central to common-sense reasoning. | Historical agenda, not achieved solution. | M16 | TECH | Speer, Chin & Havasi (2017), [ConceptNet 5.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975) | Integrates crowd and lexical sources into a multilingual semantic network for everyday concepts. | Edges are shallow, noisy, culturally uneven, and non-normative; useful priors require provenance and defeaters. |
| M17 | TECH | Sap et al. (2019), [ATOMIC](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33013027) | Provides if–then social commonsense about events, intents, reactions, and effects. | Crowdsourced English-centric generalizations can stereotype and are not situation-grounded facts. |
| M18 | META | Davis (2023), [Benchmarks for Automated Commonsense Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04752) | Surveys over 100 benchmarks and identifies major untested gaps and shortcut risks. | Benchmark survey does not itself test current systems. | M19 | EMP | Woolley et al. | M20 | TECH/EMP | Mann & Helbing (2017), [Optimal Incentives for Collective Intelligence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5441831/) | Models and experiments show incentives can reduce informational diversity through herding; alternative rewards can improve aggregate forecasts. | Forecasting model, not moral deliberation. ## Evidence gaps that materially constrain the programme

1. There is no agreed ground truth for wise decisions in tragic or morally plural cases.
2. Wisdom measures disagree on whether they assess knowledge, reasoning, personality, reputation, or behavior.
3. Evidence for cross-domain transfer of wisdom is far weaker than evidence for domain-specific expertise.
4. Meta-pattern quality lacks a standard measure separating explanatory compression from seductive overgeneralization.
5. Human wisdom research is culturally and linguistically uneven; broad cross-cultural convergence is usually about perceived traits, not action outcomes.
6. No reviewed computational architecture demonstrates integrated wisdom, longitudinal learning from real consequences, and legitimate normative governance.
7. Present LLM findings are version- and task-sensitive and cannot establish permanent incapacity or future capability.
8. It is unresolved whether embodiment, mortality, personal stake, moral responsibility, or consciousness is constitutive of wisdom or can be functionally substituted.
9. Collective systems introduce power, incentive, conformity, privacy, and accountability problems even when aggregation improves accuracy.
10. “Flourishing,” “common good,” “harm,” “autonomy,” and “tradition fidelity” require domain- and polity-specific legitimate specification; they cannot be smuggled in as neutral metrics.

## Research stance after evidence review

The evidence warrants neither “LLMs are already wise” nor “machine wisdom is conceptually impossible.” It warrants a graded programme:

1. define observable, falsifiable wisdom-supporting functions;
2. separate them from adjacent capabilities and rhetorical impressions;
3. build architectures that expose their representations, conflicts, uncertainty, and learning;
4. compare every addition with simpler baselines;
5. reserve the unqualified word *wise* for systems that demonstrate longitudinal, transferable, outcome-sensitive performance under legitimate evaluation.
