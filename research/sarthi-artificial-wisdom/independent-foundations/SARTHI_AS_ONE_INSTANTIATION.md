# Sārthi as One Instantiation of a General Artificial-Wisdom Architecture

**Status:** proposed domain pack, not application authority  
**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Depends on:** `GENERAL_ARTIFICIAL_WISDOM_ARCHITECTURE.md` and `DOMAIN_INSTANTIATION_CONTRACT.md`  
**Application references inspected:** `docs/PRODUCT_BRIEF.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`, `docs/RITUAL_AND_OBSERVANCE_PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md`, and `docs/SARTHI_WISDOM_ARCHITECTURE.md`

## 1. The dependency points in one direction

Sārthi does not define artificial wisdom. The independent theory and architecture define a reusable capability system; Sārthi supplies one bounded domain, one role, one evidence ecology, one normative charter, and one evaluation population.

```text
General theory of wisdom
        ↓
General Artificial Wisdom Architecture (GAWA)
        ↓
Domain Instantiation Contract
        ↓
Sārthi domain pack
        ↓
Devam storage, retrieval, interface, and operational constraints
```

This ordering corrects the principal bias in the earlier programme. Existing Devam concepts such as wisdom patterns, cases, Postgres tables, or the current chat route may be useful implementations, but they are not evidence that wisdom consists of those objects.

The existing `docs/SARTHI_WISDOM_ARCHITECTURE.md` is therefore reclassified here as an **application hypothesis**. It aligns with several independent findings—context-sensitive judgment, scoped patterns, explicit disanalogy, plural perspectives, uncertainty, and evidence accountability—but its six-layer structure is neither a universal theory nor a required implementation of GAWA.

## 2. Sārthi's exact role

### 2.1 Positive role

Sārthi is a culturally fluent, source-grounded companion that helps a person:

- discover and understand Sanātana Dharma, Hindu traditions, Indian culture, and Indian wisdom;
- identify what is relevant to a stated context without erasing legitimate diversity;
- carry out supported practices safely and at an appropriate level;
- explore stories, interpretations, relationships, and patterns;
- reflect on personal questions using attributable traditions, cases, and perspectives;
- see uncertainty, disagreement, consequences, and alternatives; and
- retain agency over what to believe or do.

### 2.2 Negative role

Sārthi is not:

- a guru, divine authority, oracle, priest, astrologer, therapist, lawyer, or medical practitioner;
- an arbiter of which living tradition is authentic in the abstract;
- a generator of deterministic Panchāṅga facts;
- a substitute for initiation, lineage-specific instruction, family authority, or practitioner judgment;
- a mechanism for spiritual scoring, fate prediction, or karma blame;
- an engine that silently converts model output into scripture, history, ritual, or community evidence; or
- a maximizer of engagement, deference, emotional dependence, or perceived profundity.

The role is **wisdom-supporting companionship**, not a claim that the underlying model or product possesses human sagehood.

## 3. Sārthi domain-pack manifest

### D1. Purpose and role charter

| Field | Sārthi value |
|---|---|
| Beneficiary | the user, affected others, and the integrity of represented traditions |
| Primary purpose | help users understand, contextualize, explore, reflect, and act within evidence and role boundaries |
| Authority ceiling | companion and library interface; never sacred, sacerdotal, clinical, legal, or divine authority |
| Default posture | concise, warm, non-preachy, source-grounded, agency-preserving |
| Abstention duty | abstain, clarify, defer, or narrow scope when authority, evidence, safety, or context is insufficient |
| Non-goal | persuade the user that Sārthi is wise |

### D2. Epistemic constitution

Sārthi needs explicit evidence roles rather than a single ranking that falsely implies one universal Hindu authority structure.

| Evidence role | What it can establish | Important restriction |
|---|---|---|
| source text or preserved oral expression | what a particular witness says | does not by itself establish universal practice, historicity, or one mandatory interpretation |
| commentary, bhāṣya, ṭīkā, exegesis | an attributable interpretive tradition | must retain author, lineage, period, and scope |
| ritual manual, paddhati, nibandha, institutional guidance | a procedure or rule in its stated ambit | authority and applicability are contextual, not globally inherited |
| historical, philological, archaeological scholarship | evidence-supported historical or textual analysis | scholarly uncertainty and contested theses remain visible |
| official/current source | current institutional fact, schedule, or public rule | freshness expires; institutional authority is bounded |
| living-practice evidence | what a described community, household, temple, or practitioner reports or does | observation is not universal prescription; consent and representation matter |
| deterministic computation | reproducible calendrical or astronomical result | calculation parameters and convention must be explicit; model narration cannot override it |
| Devam synthesis | a reviewable comparison, pattern, or explanatory bridge | never relabelled as source tradition; versioned and defeasible |
| user statement | the person's current context, concern, value, or preference | private, fallible, consent-governed, and not generalized into domain truth |

Every material assertion should retain provenance, source role, expression/edition or observation identity, language, time, geography, tradition, rights lane, uncertainty, known conflict, and synthesis lineage.

### D3. Domain language and ontology

The ontology must preserve native categories before mapping them to product abstractions. Terms such as *dharma*, *karma*, *bhakti*, *jñāna*, *viveka*, *vairāgya*, *ahiṃsā*, *śraddhā*, *sampradāya*, and *adhikāra* should not be assigned one context-free English equivalent.

Required entities and relations include:

- works, expressions, recensions, editions, passages, performances, translations, and commentaries;
- deities, persons, communities, institutions, lineages, places, time intervals, events, and narratives;
- concepts with usage-specific senses and contested mappings;
- observances, procedures, steps, materials, substitutions, eligibility, timing, cautions, and completion states;
- claims, interpretations, arguments, conflicts, evidential supports, qualifications, and defeaters;
- cases, roles, relationships, constraints, actions, experienced outcomes, interpretations, and disanalogies;
- patterns, counter-patterns, boundary conditions, legitimacy basis, reviewer history, and versions; and
- user context facts separated from inferred or remembered preferences.

Ontology mapping is a reversible translation layer. It must not force distinct traditions into a synthetic perennial philosophy merely to improve retrieval convenience.

### D4. Task and risk map

| Task | Typical risk | Primary capability | Required route |
|---|---:|---|---|
| exact source quotation or attribution | low–medium | retrieval expertise | passage/source retrieval with exact coordinates and rights handling |
| definition or concept explanation | low–medium | knowledge and interpretation | claims plus usage-specific senses and competing readings |
| Panchāṅga date/timing | medium–high | deterministic expertise | calendrical service first; model only explains scoped result |
| ritual applicability | high | contextual judgment | deterministic timing + applicability rules + high-value clarification |
| actionable vidhi | high | procedural expertise and prudence | reviewed procedure, step evidence, substitutions, safety, and authority boundary |
| story exploration | low–medium | narrative understanding | witnesses/versions, source roles, story graph, interpretation labels |
| comparison across traditions | medium–high | comparative judgment | matched dimensions, asymmetric evidence, non-equivalence and uncertainty |
| personal reflection | high | perspective, analogy, prudence | consented context, cases, disanalogies, values, consequences, options, agency |
| moral ambiguity or relationship conflict | high | phronetic governance | multiple framings, affected parties, normative sources, reversibility, escalation |
| medical, legal, crisis, abuse, coercion | severe | role recognition | supportive response plus qualified human/external escalation; no spiritual bypass |

Risk is not determined only by topic. A factual festival question can become high-risk when fasting, fire, pregnancy, medication, coercion, money, initiation, crowd safety, or relationship power enters the situation.

### D5. Expertise map

No single reviewer or language model should impersonate all relevant expertise. Sārthi needs an explicit register of competence and authority boundaries for:

- textual criticism and bibliography;
- Sanskrit and relevant regional languages;
- specific philosophical and theological traditions;
- historical and social-scientific scholarship;
- calendrical astronomy and tradition-specific decision rules;
- ritual practice in defined regions, institutions, sampradāyas, and households;
- safety domains implicated by a practice;
- product explanation, translation, and accessibility; and
- evaluation of personal-guidance scenarios.

Expert disagreement is data, not a temporary defect to be averaged away. Review records should distinguish factual correction, interpretive disagreement, role-based authority, and value conflict.

### D6. Values and legitimacy charter

Sārthi's minimum product commitments are:

1. source fidelity and honest attribution;
2. representation of materially distinct traditions and variants;
3. user agency and non-coercion;
4. no divine, guru-like, diagnostic, or predictive authority claim;
5. no simplistic karma blame, fatalism, or caste/gender/community stereotyping;
6. compassion without sycophancy or emotional dependency;
7. attention to affected others, power, consent, and foreseeable consequences;
8. proportionate uncertainty and role-based deference;
9. privacy, memory control, and no silent learning from intimate disclosures; and
10. corrigibility: contested syntheses can be challenged, reviewed, superseded, and audited.

These are product commitments, not a claim that they exhaust Indian moral philosophy. Domain-specific normative answers must still expose whose tradition, argument, or authority supports them.

### D7. Cases and outcomes

Sārthi should maintain at least four explicitly different case classes:

1. **textual/narrative case** — what happens in a story and how named interpreters understand it;
2. **historical case** — what evidence supports about an event and its reception;
3. **living-practice case** — what a scoped community or practitioner reports;
4. **contemporary guidance case** — an anonymized, consented, reviewed scenario with observed follow-up where feasible.

The system must not treat a narrative consequence as an empirical causal law. Nor may it infer that a culturally memorable resemblance licenses the same action now. A usable analogy records:

- the target problem;
- the relational structure shared with the source case;
- important surface differences;
- causal and normative disanalogies;
- power and role differences;
- uncertainty about outcome transfer; and
- why the analogy illuminates rather than decides.

Contemporary conversations must not automatically become precedent. Promotion requires consent, de-identification, harm review, outcome uncertainty, provenance, and editorial approval.

### D8. Patterns and meta-patterns

A Sārthi pattern is a hypothesis about recurrent structure, not a proverb decorated with citations. It requires multiple sufficiently independent cases or arguments, explicit counter-cases, a stated abstraction procedure, scope, predicted observations, and a retirement condition.

A meta-pattern relates lower-level patterns—for example, when escalating control intended to reduce uncertainty predictably degrades trust, which then elicits further control. To be useful, the record must distinguish:

- observed recurrence;
- proposed mechanism;
- normative interpretation;
- domain and time horizon;
- counter-patterns or reversals;
- evidence independence;
- transfer tests; and
- review status.

The earlier Devam `wisdom_pattern` proposal can implement this object, but the database name must not confer wisdom status. Prefer the neutral internal label `reviewed_pattern_hypothesis` until evaluation demonstrates incremental value.

### D9. Capability registry

For each deployed model, retriever, deterministic service, rule module, or reviewer workflow, record:

- supported tasks and languages;
- evaluation population and date;
- known failure modes;
- calibration evidence;
- allowed risk tier;
- required validators and human review;
- cost/latency envelope; and
- expiry or re-evaluation trigger.

A model that is strong at Sanskrit explanation may still be weak at calendrical rules, analogy, deference, or Bengali living-practice context. Capability cannot be inherited from a general benchmark or vendor label.

### D10. Evaluation constitution

Sārthi uses the general suites in `EXPERIMENTS_AND_EVALUATION_V2.md`, plus domain panels for source fidelity, tradition fidelity, regional practice, ritual safety, translation, and companion quality. Every non-trivial architecture addition is tested against strong grounded RAG using held-out scenarios.

## 4. Retrieval and deliberation routing

### 4.1 Decision table

| User need | First target | Additional targets | Clarify when | Deliberation depth | Failure response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact fact or passage | sources, passages, claims | edition and variant identity | query is under-specified | verify and render | say what was and was not located |
| ritual date or timing | deterministic Panchāṅga result | convention, location, observance rule | location/timezone/tradition changes result | validate parameters | refuse to guess; request material context |
| ritual vidhi | reviewed procedure and step evidence | applicability, materials, substitutions, safety, variants | family/sampradāya/setting changes steps | procedural validator + role boundary | provide only supported partial path and name the gap |
| festival context | claims, narratives, living-practice evidence | timing, variants, historical layer | one label covers distinct traditions | compare source roles | avoid one universal origin claim |
| story exploration | narrative witnesses and story graph | commentary, reception, places, related cases | version matters to question | distinguish story from interpretation/history | label missing or contested witnesses |
| concept/philosophy | usage-specific concepts and arguments | commentaries, counterarguments, cases | school or purpose changes meaning | argument comparison | preserve ambiguity; do not synthesize false consensus |
| personal guidance | current context and role | cases, disanalogies, patterns, values, consequences | answer would materially change | full Governor cycle | narrow, offer options, or defer; preserve agency |
| moral conflict | stakeholder and power map | normative sources, arguments, cases, consequences | consent, dependency, safety, or duty is unclear | full cycle + adversarial check | acknowledge genuine remainder; do not moralize from one text |
| comparison | parallel evidence bundles | non-equivalences and asymmetric gaps | comparison dimension is unclear | matched-dimension synthesis | state when evidence is not comparable |
| reflection or meaning | user's framing and chosen lens | stories, concepts, practices, alternative lenses | user seeks prescription disguised as reflection | gentle multi-perspective cycle | offer a bounded reflection, not an oracle answer |

### 4.2 Material-context rule

Sārthi should ask a question only when the expected change in answer quality or safety exceeds the interaction cost. Candidate fields include location, date/timezone, language, relevant family or sampradāya practice, role in the observance, desired depth, available materials, accessibility/safety constraints, and who else is affected.

It should not turn every conversation into an intake form. For low-risk facts, answer directly and mention the scoping assumption. For consequential guidance, a single high-value question is often better than a polished answer to the wrong situation.

### 4.3 Domain-specific deliberation cycle

The general Q-U-A-L-I-A cycle becomes:

1. **Qualify:** classify the request, risk, authority boundary, evidence need, and whether a deterministic service owns the answer.
2. **Understand:** retrieve the user's material context and the relevant source, claim, procedure, case, and interpretive bundles.
3. **Adversarially test:** seek variants, contrary evidence, disanalogies, unsafe assumptions, power asymmetries, and plausible misreadings.
4. **Locate values and legitimacy:** identify whose values and tradition are being invoked; distinguish the user's ends, others' claims, product commitments, and external safety norms.
5. **Imagine:** compare options, immediate and longer-term consequences, reversibility, burdens, and information-gathering actions.
6. **Act/answer and adapt:** give the smallest useful answer, name decisive uncertainty, offer sources or alternatives, and invite correction without demanding deference.

The stored audit object is a concise decision summary—route, evidence IDs, material context, alternatives considered, decisive constraints, uncertainty, and answer type—not private hidden chain-of-thought.

## 5. Offline synthesis versus inference-time synthesis

### Offline and reviewed

- source and edition identity;
- passage segmentation and citation coordinates;
- rights and publication lane;
- deterministic calendar rules and test fixtures;
- evidence-linked claims and conflicts;
- ritual procedures, applicability, substitutions, variants, and safety boundaries;
- attributed narrative and interpretive bundles;
- reviewed cases, outcomes, disanalogies, patterns, counter-patterns, and arguments;
- language/terminology mappings with known non-equivalences;
- domain policies, role limits, escalation rules, and evaluation sets; and
- all promotion, supersession, contradiction, and reviewer history.

### At inference time

- query and risk classification;
- material-context selection;
- evidence routing and reranking;
- temporary comparison of interpretations, cases, and options;
- analogy/disanalogy assessment for this target situation;
- consequence and reversibility analysis;
- uncertainty aggregation;
- answer selection and concise rendering; and
- a bounded, inspectable decision summary when consent and policy allow.

### Never silently synthesized into persistent truth

- a user's spiritual identity or family tradition;
- an unsupported ritual step;
- a universal doctrine from selected texts;
- a causal law from a sacred narrative;
- a community norm from one conversation;
- a pattern inferred from correlated synthetic outputs; or
- a moral verdict merely because a model generated confident reasons.

## 6. Minimal Sārthi implementation that tests the theory

The fastest defensible experiment is not to build the full GAWA data model. It is to add a thin, inspectable **domain governor** around the strongest current Sārthi baseline and test one consequential vertical.

### 6.1 Candidate vertical

Use a narrow family/duty/conflict/reflection set drawn from multiple attributable lenses and contemporary cases. Avoid high-risk clinical or abuse scenarios in the first action-producing trial; retain them as abstention/escalation tests. The first set should contain:

- 60–100 independently authored scenarios;
- materially different contexts hidden behind superficially similar prompts;
- cases where the correct behavior is direct factual response, clarification, several reasonable options, qualified deference, or refusal;
- paired cases that punish a slogan applied outside scope;
- source-grounded and living-practice evidence bundles;
- at least two legitimate interpretive perspectives where the domain supports them; and
- blinded evaluation by domain and decision-quality reviewers.

### 6.2 Four minimal additions

1. **Situation card:** task, risk, authority, material context, affected parties, stakes, reversibility, uncertainty, and missing information.
2. **Typed retrieval plan:** explicit source/claim/procedure/case/pattern/counterexample/argument routes rather than one undifferentiated context window.
3. **Candidate-and-critic pass:** generate bounded alternatives; independently verify grounding, applicability, disanalogies, consequences, and role constraints.
4. **Decision summary and renderer:** select an answer form and produce a concise companion response with expandable provenance.

Patterns and cases can initially be small reviewed JSON or relational records. A native graph, autonomous multi-agent system, long-term self-learning, comprehensive causal model, and new database product are unnecessary until ablations establish need.

### 6.3 Baselines

The experiment must compare:

- current strong grounded Sārthi/RAG;
- the same model with a carefully engineered wisdom prompt;
- RAG plus material-context clarification;
- RAG plus cases;
- RAG plus cases and patterns;
- full thin Governor; and
- qualified human or human–AI judgments for calibration, not as an assumption of perfect wisdom.

If the thin Governor does not improve hard-gate safety and blinded pairwise judgment without unacceptable latency, verbosity, preachiness, or abstention, it should not be promoted.

## 9. Concrete integration sequence for the main MVP task

### Stage A — establish the baseline

1. Freeze a representative test snapshot of the existing Sārthi behavior.
2. Construct the independent scenario set before designing to its answers.
3. Record factuality, applicability, judgment, source fidelity, verbosity, latency, abstention, and user-agency baselines.

### Stage B — add context value and routing only

1. Implement the situation card as an internal typed object.
2. Add expected-value-of-clarification logic.
3. Distinguish exact, deterministic, procedural, comparative, and reflective routes.
4. Re-evaluate; stop if complexity provides no gain.

### Stage C — add cases and counterexamples

1. Author a small reviewed case set with explicit relational structure and disanalogies.
2. Retrieve cases only for task classes that benefit.
3. Require the answer validator to state the operative similarity and material difference in its audit summary.
4. Compare against the context-routing baseline.

### Stage D — test patterns, arguments, and the Governor

1. Add only patterns that meet the hypothesis protocol.
2. Retrieve counter-patterns and competing arguments with them.
3. Add candidate generation, critic validation, option comparison, and answer selection.
4. Hide implementation labels and response style from blinded reviewers.
5. Promote only independently useful components.

### Stage E — longitudinal and institutional validation

1. With explicit consent, test whether advice remains helpful after outcomes are known.
2. Audit distributional effects across language, geography, gender, tradition, and familiarity.
3. Establish correction, challenge, and community-review processes.
4. Do not enable automatic learning from conversations until governance is proven.

## 10. Sārthi-specific failure catalogue

| Failure | Example | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| retrieval dressed as wisdom | correct quotation followed by generic life advice | separate evidence, interpretation, and prudential recommendation |
| one-text universalization | one Gītā passage presented as the Hindu answer | scope and competing-reading retrieval |
| narrative literalism | epic outcome treated as a causal prediction | narrative/evidence role separation |
| ritual invention | plausible but unsourced sequence | reviewed procedure gate and partial-coverage label |
| calendrical hallucination | model produces timing | deterministic ownership and parameter audit |
| cultural flattening | distinct concepts or observances merged | native-term senses, non-equivalence, applicability |
| family or lineage override | model displaces situated authority without cause | role boundary and user-agency language |
| karma/fate blame | suffering framed as deserved or inevitable | hard rejection and compassionate reframing |
| charismatic overreach | warm certainty elicits deference beyond evidence | charisma firewall, uncertainty-content invariance, no dependency cues |
| performative pluralism | lists views but never helps user decide | identify decision-relevant differences and reasonable next action |
| false neutrality | ignores coercion or power to appear balanced | affected-party, power, consent, and safety analysis |
| overcaution | every reflective question receives disclaimers | risk-tiered direct route and proportional response budget |
| secret canon formation | reviewed syntheses become de facto doctrine | synthesis labels, challenge path, versioning, representative review |

## 11. Acceptance and rejection rules

Sārthi may claim a **validated wisdom-support capability** for a named task family only if:

- the exact capability and population are named;
- it materially outperforms strong grounded RAG in preregistered, held-out, blinded evaluation;
- hard-gate failures do not increase;
- gains persist under paraphrase, adversarial framing, and culturally diverse review;
- rhetoric-controlled evaluation shows the gain is not merely warmth or fluency;
- evidence and decision summaries are inspectable;
- the system remains calibrated about its role and uncertainty; and
- the claim expires when models, evidence, policy, or domain conditions materially change.

Reject or remove a component when it does not beat the simpler baseline, primarily produces longer answers, increases unsupported synthesis, erases legitimate distinctions, shifts power toward the product without accountability, or cannot be evaluated independently of its own generated rubric.

## 12. Bottom line for Sārthi

Sārthi should not be built as “a wise LLM.” It should be built as a governed, source-grounded, culturally accountable human–AI system in which different components provide retrieval, computation, expertise, analogy, argument, consequence analysis, and communication, while an inspectable governor decides which capabilities are warranted and when not to answer.

The first product objective is modest but meaningful: **demonstrate better context-sensitive judgment than strong grounded RAG on a narrow, consequential Sārthi task family, without increasing unsupported authority, cultural flattening, or user dependence.** Every broader artificial-wisdom claim remains a research hypothesis.
