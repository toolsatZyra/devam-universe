# Mahalaya Bengal transition and listening research (2027)

## Frozen denominator and exclusions

This dossier selects exactly the final-disposition item `mahalaya`. The
selected lane is the Bengal-facing Mahalaya calendrical threshold and living
public-service-broadcast/listening practice. It is not a hidden umbrella for:

- North/West `sarva-pitru-amavasya` ancestor applicability and formal Shraddha;
- Tamil `mahalaya-amavasai-tamil`, including named Kaveri tharpanam practice;
- Odia `mahalaya-amabasya-odia` and named Odisha temple practice;
- Durga Puja, Navaratri, Ghatasthapana, Chandi Path or a Devi story/puja owner;
- any current Akashvani, Doordarshan, temple or organiser schedule.

Those are separately frozen denominator or owner routes. Shared words, civil
dates and seasonal association do not merge their procedures.

## Existing material audit

The existing Delhi 2026 Pitru Paksha fixture and remembrance content are
useful but not complete for this lane. They provide:

- a correct warning that a Mahalaya/Pitru Paksha calendar label does not
  decide an ancestor, death-tithi, performer, offering or family procedure;
- a historical Nirnayasindhu Mahalaya interval/applicability reference;
- generic private remembrance, privacy and no-result safety;
- a separately marked Tamil Mahalaya Amavasai tharpanam variant.

They do not complete `mahalaya` because they use a 2026 Delhi fixture, have no
Bengali calendar identity, no public-service-broadcast/listening route, one
short narrative, only 3/4/4 procedural steps, no current programme resolution,
and no explicit separation of Bengal, Sarva Pitru, Tamil, Odia and Devi-owner
work. They remain partial reused evidence and are not silently counted.

## Deterministic date and schedule dependency

A reviewed 2027 Bengali-calendar discovery fixture places **Mahalaya on
Wednesday, 29 September 2027**, Bhadro Krishna Amabasya. A separate 2027
Pitru Paksha fixture places Sarva Pitru Amavasya on the same civil date. The
coincidence explains association; it does not merge the denominator items.

The deterministic rule is the locally accepted Bengali calendar's Mahalaya
Amabasya day, resolved from the local Amavasya interval and day-boundary rule.
The consumer answer must ask for city and accepted calendar. It must not copy
29 September, an Amavasya interval, a dawn time or a programme schedule to
another city or year.

Listening time is not computed from the tithi. An Akashvani/Doordarshan or
other programme time requires a current official schedule. Prasar Bharati
records from 2012, 2013, 2015 and 2023 demonstrate recurring Mahalaya
programming, including dawn and live `Mahishasur Mardini` formats, but do not
establish a 2027 time, station, language, stream, duration or replay right.
Until current information is published, report the listening schedule as
unresolved.

## Meaning and who commonly observes

The West Bengal government puja-permission portal describes Durga Puja as a
season beginning with Mahalaya and closing with Vijaya Dashami, and identifies
Durga's victory over Mahishasura as its festival story. The bounded Mahalaya
meaning in this lane is therefore a Bengal-associated seasonal threshold,
often marked through listening or cultural remembrance. It is not proof that
every Bengali, every resident of West Bengal or every Hindu performs a rite.

Applicable consumers include:

- Bengali-associated individuals or households who already recognise
  Mahalaya and want a safe listening/reflection route;
- participants following a current lawful Akashvani, Doordarshan or other
  authorised programme;
- visitors preparing for a named institution without leading or copying its
  Durga or ancestor rites.

Not automatically included are people seeking Shraddha/tarpan, a Tamil or
Odia Mahalaya route, formal Devi worship, Chandi Path, Ghatasthapana, temple
puja, broadcast piracy, or a claim that one should wake before dawn.

## Source-labelled observance stories

### 1. Mahalaya as a calendrical and seasonal threshold

The Bengali calendar names Mahalaya on Bhadro Krishna Amabasya. West Bengal's
current puja portal places Mahalaya at the opening edge of the Durga Puja
season and Vijaya Dashami at its close. The next-day relationship with Devi
Paksha/Navaratri is calendar- and tradition-dependent; a sampled 2027
Ghatasthapana page even requires an exception because Pratipada is skipped.
Therefore Devam presents a threshold story, not one portable civil date,
universal dawn, universal ancestor rite or instruction to begin Durga Puja.

### 2. A recurring public-service-broadcast practice

Prasar Bharati annual reports and programme records repeatedly document
Mahalaya programming: a special dawn `Mahishasur Mardini` programme in 2012,
Mahalaya programmes in 2013 and 2015, and a live `Mahisasur Mardini` broadcast
on Mahalaya in 2023. Together these official records establish a continuing
public-service-media practice and explain why listening can be a meaningful
consumer route. They do not make one recording, language, station, cast,
script, hour or listening posture universal. The programme's protected text,
music and audio are not copied into Git or the consumer pack. A current
official source controls availability and reuse.

The West Bengal portal gives only the compact Durga-Mahishasura victory
association. The full Devi Mahatmya narrative, theology, Chandi Path and Durga
Puja instructions remain a proposed cross-link to the Devi owner. Devam must
not manufacture a full goddess story from model memory in this lane.

## Typical practice and complete generic routes

The safe generic practice is listening, silence or transition reflection—not
a fabricated puja. Three complete routes are required:

1. **private minimum:** resolve the local Mahalaya day; retain normal sleep,
   food, water, medicine and care; optionally find a current authorised
   programme; use safe volume or silence; hear the two labelled observance
   stories; note one gentle transition or care commitment; close without
   omen, victory, blessing or result claims;
2. **opt-in household listening:** resolve date and current programme; invite
   without forced dawn waking; offer translation/subtitles, headphones,
   silence and an easy exit; label violence and victory as story content, not
   real-person categories; discuss one voluntary reflection; close without
   copying Devi or ancestor rites;
3. **named broadcast/institution participant:** verify the official station,
   stream, language, time, replay/accessibility rules and venue; attend or
   listen within copyright, crowd, hearing, device and institutional rules;
   do not record, rebroadcast, lead, film restricted activity or copy a puja;
   use the organiser's safe close and return to ordinary care.

No pre-dawn alarm, bath, fast, lamp, incense, flower, altar, offering, mantra,
donation, special food, new clothing, broadcast subscription or temple visit
is required for the generic route.

## Materials and substitutions

Nothing is required. Optional supports are:

- current official programme information, replaced by silence or no listening;
- an existing radio/phone/speaker, replaced by another lawful device or none;
- low-volume speaker or headphones, replaced by captions/transcript summary
  where lawfully supplied, hearing protection, silence or no programme;
- a private note, replaced by a spoken thought or nothing.

An image, lamp, incense, flowers, food, water offering, kalash, Devi text,
Shraddha material or donation belongs only to a separately named authority.

## Major variants only

Minor language, spelling, dress, food preference, device type, speaker versus
headphones, image/no-image and reflection/silence remain unified.

Separate routes are required when evidence changes:

- Sarva Pitru Amavasya ancestor applicability, performer, Shraddha/tarpan,
  materials and closing;
- Tamil Mahalaya Amavasai timing, water/tharpanam, priest and safety authority;
- Odia Mahalaya Amabasya timing, temple, local story and ordered practice;
- current Akashvani/Doordarshan programme, station, language, time, access,
  recording/replay and rights rules;
- Devi Mahatmya story, Chandi Path, Durga Puja, Navaratri or Ghatasthapana;
- a named temple, pandal, club, school or community organiser;
- sleep, hearing, sensory, disability, child/dependent, crowd, fire, device,
  privacy, grief or financial safety needs.

No state pack is created merely for translation. Tamil and Odia remain
separate because the frozen inventory and evidence show potential changes to
timing, formal action, water/temple authority and safety—not spelling alone.

## Safety, rights and non-coercion

- Dawn waking, sleep loss, fasting, dehydration, meal delay, medicine change,
  ritual bath, flame, incense, crowd, travel, donation and purchase are not
  required.
- Keep volume safe; provide captions, translation, sensory breaks, an easy
  exit and no-headphone/no-audio choices. Children and dependents retain normal
  sleep and guardian care.
- Mahishasura/victory language is source story context. No real person or
  community is labelled a demon or enemy, and no violence is copied or
  justified.
- Do not reproduce, upload, rebroadcast, transcribe or distribute protected
  programme text, music or audio without permission. Link to current official
  access instead.
- No one owes belief, nostalgia, Bengali identity performance, dawn listening,
  family gathering, ancestor remembrance or Devi worship.
- Later dreams, feelings, broadcasts, technical glitches or events are not
  divine signs, ancestor messages, victory predictions or ritual scores.
- Current emergency, medical, hearing, electrical, safeguarding and venue
  rules override completion; immediate danger uses 112.
