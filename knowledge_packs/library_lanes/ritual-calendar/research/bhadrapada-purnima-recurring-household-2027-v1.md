## Meaning and source-labelled stories

### 1. Calendar identity, not an origin myth

Bhadrapada Purnima is the Bhadrapada-month instance of Shukla Purnima in the
reviewed North/reference naming convention. It marks the completion of the
waxing lunar fortnight in that convention. This is a calendrical identity, not
evidence for one national fast, one deity, one household altar, or one promised
result. Amanta/purnimanta month vocabulary and same-day named observances must
remain visible instead of being flattened into a universal story.

### 2. The Mahabharata's Soma, Rohini, Daksha and Prabhasa account

Mahabharata, Shalya Parva section 35, in the public-domain K. M. Ganguli
translation, embeds the account inside Baladeva's pilgrimage. At Prabhasa,
Janamejaya asks why Soma lost and recovered his splendour. Vaishampayana says
Daksha gave his twenty-seven daughters, associated with the constellations and
time-reckoning, to Soma. Soma favoured Rohini and neglected the others. They
complained to Daksha; Daksha twice told Soma to treat them equally, but Soma
continued as before. Daksha then cursed Soma with a wasting illness. As Soma
wasted, herbs and living creatures also declined.

The gods appealed to Daksha. He said his words could not simply become false,
but prescribed a partial resolution: Soma must behave equally, go to the
Sarasvati's meeting with the western ocean, honour Mahadeva, and regain his
form. Soma reached Prabhasa and recovered his cool rays. The account then says
he would wane for half a month and wax for half a month. It closes by explaining
Prabhasa's name through the recovery of Soma's splendour before Baladeva moves
to the next tirtha.

The Kumbhaghonam Sanskrit mirror supplies the corresponding Shalya Parva
chapter text, including Soma's preference for Rohini, the other nakshatra-named
wives' complaint, and Daksha's equality command.

This is a source story about lunar change and Prabhasa, not Bhadrapada
Purnima's universal origin and not a current puja instruction. The source's
divine polygamous household, command language, curse, illness, sacred-water
bath and cosmic consequences are narrative events. They do not mean that a
person owes marriage, affection, sex, equal intimacy, reconciliation, a cure,
travel, water entry, or obedience to a parent. Devam must not describe illness
as punishment or use the story to pressure a neglected partner to remain in a
relationship.

## Living-practice evidence and boundary

The reviewed current practitioner page associates Bhadrapada Purnima with
Satyanarayana worship, fasting, sacred-water bathing, charity, and an
Uma-Maheshwar vrata. It also makes benefit claims. This evidence establishes
that materially different living routes exist; it does not make them universal
or authorise Devam to copy the displayed vidhi.

The consumer answer should say:

- some families conduct a named Satyanarayana Puja/Katha under their own text,
  elder or priest;
- some practitioner sources name an Uma-Maheshwar vrata;
- Purnima Shraddha/Pitru Paksha applicability depends on death-tithi and family
  authority and is not generic ancestor contact;
- Anvadhan/Ishti belongs to a qualified Vedic-fire authority;
- temples own their current programme, access, offerings, queue and closing;
- fasting, river/pond bathing, donation, mantra, vigil and moonrise worship are
  optional only when a named practice actually requires them and safety allows.

For the selected recurring lane, the safe complete form is: resolve the local
day; hear the two labelled stories; preserve the modern relationship and
health boundary; optionally use silence or a familiar family prayer; notice
the moon only from a safe ordinary place if visible and desired; choose one
small affordable care act or none; close with normal food, water, medicine,
sleep and no promised result.

## Applicability and material variants

### Unified as minor expression only

Across the user-selected North/West and shared-state route, language, spelling,
Purnima/Pournami vocabulary, dress, food preference, familiar deity name,
image/no-image, and silence do not by themselves create separate procedures.

### Separate because the difference is material

- **Satyanarayana Puja/Katha:** named deity, narrative corpus, sankalpa,
  offerings, prasada and officiation; source rights are not cleared here.
- **Uma-Maheshwar vrata:** different deity pair, story, eligibility and fasting
  claims.
- **Purnima Shraddha/Pitru Paksha:** death-tithi, ancestor applicability,
  performer and family/priest authority materially change the answer.
- **Anvadhan/Ishti:** sacred fire, mantra and ritual eligibility require a
  qualified authority.
- **Named temple:** live programme, visitor rules, offerings, queues and closing
  belong to the institution.
- **Safety/access needs:** fasting, food, medicine, pregnancy, childhood,
  disability, grief, water, roof, travel, crowd, flame and financial risk can
  change or stop participation.

No separate state pack is justified merely by translation or familiar
expression. A future state or sampradaya split requires evidence that timing,
authority, ordered actions, materials, safety, or closing materially changes.

## Actionable route design

### Private minimum

1. Resolve local Bhadrapada Shukla Purnima; if unresolved, stop at an undated
   story/reflection.
2. Identify whether the request is generic or actually one of the separate
   named routes.
3. Keep ordinary food, water, medicine, insulin, sleep and hygiene; begin no
   fast, vigil or water entry from this pack.
4. Hear the calendar identity and the complete source-labelled Soma story.
5. State the modern boundary: no one owes relationship access, cure, travel,
   bathing, reconciliation or obedience.
6. Optionally sit in silence, use a familiar family prayer, or notice the moon
   from a safe indoor/window/ground-level place; moon visibility is not a test.
7. Choose one small affordable care act or none, then close without a result
   claim.

### Family form

Use the same sequence with explicit opt-in, a concise bilingual story telling,
no compulsory belief/disclosure/touch, an optional familiar family prayer, and
a safe shared food/care close. Do not improvise Satyanarayana Katha, Shraddha,
fire offerings, mantra, fasting or sacred-water bathing.

### Temple participation

First identify the named institution and whether its event is general Purnima,
Satyanarayana, Uma-Maheshwar, Shraddha, Anvadhan/Ishti, or another programme.
Verify the current official schedule, visitor eligibility, accessibility,
offerings and closing. Participate only in authorised visitor areas, retain an
easy exit, and do not copy institutional actions home.

## Safety and non-coercion

- No fasting, dehydration, meal delay, medicine change or vigil is required.
- No river, pond, sea, ghat, roof, ledge, isolated outdoor place, flame,
  binocular, telescope, travel or crowd exposure is required.
- A child or dependent participates only with ordinary care and guardian
  consent; anyone may stop without explanation.
- Never frame illness, infertility, poverty, grief, relationship conflict or a
  lunar phase as punishment.
- Never promise happiness, prosperity, cure, children, marital fortune,
  ancestor liberation, sin removal or multiplied merit.
- The epic story does not authorise coercion, stalking, forced equality of
  intimacy, forced reconciliation, parent control, caste hierarchy or ritual
  exclusion.
- No donation, priest fee, prasada, flower, lamp, image, moon sighting, mantra,
  Sanskrit, touch, disclosure, forgiveness or belief is required.
- Use 112 for an immediate emergency. Formal medical, water, fire, crowd and
  institutional rules override this pack.
