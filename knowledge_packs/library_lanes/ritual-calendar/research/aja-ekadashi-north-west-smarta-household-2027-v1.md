## Applicability and deterministic timing

The ordinary lane applies to a North/West Smarta household or individual only
after the user's Indian city and accepted ordinary Panchang resolve the dark-
half Ekadashi identified in the source as Bhadrapada Krishna Aja Ekadashi.

Reviewed 2027 Indian discovery evidence places ordinary **Aja Ekadashi on
Saturday, 28 August 2027**. The lane-local calendar census finds the Aja label
in North-reference, Gujarati, Kannada, Marathi and Telugu calendars. The Odisha
calendar places **Kaliyadalan Ekadashi** on the same civil date and
paksha/tithi; the frozen disposition therefore treats it as an alias unless a
changed deity, story, eligibility, ordered practice, close or safety boundary
is later evidenced.

The ISKCON/Gaurabda calendar calls the same 2027 occurrence **Annada
Ekadashi**. That name alone does not create a second story, but its
sampradaya-specific fasting, food and parana authority is materially different
from this ordinary Smarta household lane. Route an Annada/ISKCON user to the
user's current named Vaishnava authority; do not copy its rules into this pack.

The 28 August date is a reviewed fixture, not a portable rule. Recompute the
user's city, sunrise, tithi/day-boundary and accepted authority. Do not copy a
displayed tithi clock interval, parana window, this civil date or a temple
schedule to another city, year or tradition.

## Fixed narrative: Padma Purana 6.56

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande's English expression and the Sanskrit verse page for
*Padma Purana*, Uttara Khanda, chapter 56:

- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365633.html>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/padma-purana-sanskrit/d/doc428111.html>

The source sequence is:

1. Yudhishthira asks Janardana/Krishna the name of Bhadrapada Krishna
   Ekadashi.
2. Krishna names it Aja and attributes sin destruction and benefit in both
   worlds to its observance or hearing.
3. He introduces Harishchandra, an emperor described as true to his word.
4. The source says that through the result of some act Harishchandra lost his
   kingdom, sold his wife and son, and sold himself.
5. He became the slave of a Chandala/Pulkasa and removed the clothes of the
   dead while continuing to hold to truth for many years.
6. Distressed and seeking escape, he meets Gautama. The chapter inserts a
   Brahmana-status claim before Harishchandra bows and recounts his grief.
7. Gautama names the approaching Bhadrapada Krishna Aja Ekadashi and directs
   Harishchandra to fast and keep vigil at night, promising an end to sin.
8. Harishchandra observes the vow. The text attributes the end of long misery
   and sin to it.
9. He is reunited with his wife and living son; heavenly drums and flowers
   appear; he regains an untroubled kingdom.
10. The source says Harishchandra, the city's residents and his retinue reach
    heaven, and compares reading or hearing the account with a horse sacrifice.

This is a developed mahatmya story, not a full biography of Harishchandra. The
chapter does not narrate the wife or son's perspective, consent or experience;
it does not explain the preceding act; and it does not supply a safe modern
household puja sequence. Do not fill those gaps from model memory or merge a
different Harishchandra recension into this source-labelled account.

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Sale and enslavement:** selling a spouse, child or oneself is source
  content, not permission, duty or a model of truth. A person's absent consent
  is not consent. Devam must not normalize trafficking, bonded labour,
  coercive debt, domestic abuse, child exploitation or ownership of people.
- **Wife and child's absent agency:** the chapter centres the king and later
  says he was reunited with wife and son. It does not record their wishes,
  safety, labour, grief or consent. Do not turn reunion into proof that earlier
  coercion was harmless or forgiven.
- **Caste and death-work hierarchy:** the translation uses Chandala/Pulkasa and
  a Brahmana-priority claim. Preserve those as historical source language; do
  not rank dignity, purity, occupation or spiritual worth by caste, tribe,
  birth, surname or work. Cremation and mortuary workers deserve equal dignity
  and safe professional conditions.
- **Handling the dead:** removing clothes from bodies is a narrative fact, not
  a household action. Do not approach, move, undress, wash or handle a body;
  use authorised family, institutional, medical, police and funeral processes.
- **Truth under exploitation:** truthfulness does not require staying in
  danger, obeying abuse, revealing private information, surrendering property
  or refusing qualified help. Survival under coercion is not moral failure.
- **Misery and sin:** poverty, bereavement, debt, caste harm, job loss,
  exploitation, illness or distress is not proof of sin, karma, impurity or
  deficient observance.
- **Instant restoration:** reunion, a living son, kingdom, heaven, sin removal
  and the horse-sacrifice comparison are attributed source results, not
  promises of resurrection, custody, employment, wealth, legal success,
  absolution or afterlife outcome.
- **Fast and vigil:** source instructions are not general health advice. A
  complete ordinary route remains fed, hydrated and rested.

## Consumer meaning

A safe ordinary reflection is: **seek honest help without surrendering anyone's
freedom or dignity, and let truth include safety, consent and accountability**.

The account can support a private check on one truthful, non-coercive next
step: correct a small factual error, ask qualified help about a real problem,
keep a consented commitment, or stop participating in exploitation. It cannot
be used to demand confession, disclosure, debt payment, unpaid labour,
obedience, reconciliation, fasting or forgiveness from another person.

## Complete ordinary household routes

### Minimum private form

1. Confirm ordinary Aja/Kaliyadalan for the user's city and accepted ordinary
   authority; stop and reroute if the user follows Annada/ISKCON or another
   family, sampradaya or institutional rule.
2. Keep normal food, water, medicine, insulin, child feeding, hygiene and
   sleep. Begin no fast, vigil or parana.
3. Remember Vishnu/Janardana with a familiar name, family prayer or silence;
   no image or material is required.
4. Hear the bounded Padma Purana 6.56 sequence, including loss of kingdom,
   sale of wife/son/self, enslavement, death-work, Gautama's instruction and
   the attributed reunion/restoration/heaven claims.
5. State the boundaries: absent consent is not consent; no person can be sold
   or owned; suffering is not proof of sin; caste and work do not rank dignity;
   ritual does not erase harm or guarantee restoration.
6. If useful, choose one private, lawful, non-coercive truth-and-care action
   already within the person's responsibility, or choose no action. Do not
   contact another person, disclose private facts, transfer money/property or
   enter a dispute from this guide.
7. Close with normal food, water, sleep, privacy, safety and accountability;
   claim no absolution, reunion, resurrection, kingdom, wealth or heaven.

### Consent-led family form

Use the same fed, hydrated, rested and material-free core. Hearing, prayer and
reflection are voluntary. Do not stage the sale, enslavement, corpse work,
death, child loss or reunion; do not assign family roles; do not question a
person about debt, caste, abuse, bereavement, custody, employment, truthfulness
or private history. No one owes confession, reconciliation, forgiveness,
money, labour, fasting, vigil or participation.

### Named temple or institution participation

Verify the current institution's date, public programme, access, food,
fasting, parana, crowd, donation, photography and safeguarding rules. Attend
only as a voluntary public participant. Do not copy a temple's mantra, food
rule, vigil, fast, fee or parana into the household route. A remote, cancelled
or home form is complete.

## Safety, privacy and dignity

- A complete ordinary form is non-fasting, fed, hydrated, rested, no-vigil,
  no-flame, no-body-contact, no-funeral-work, no-purchase, no-donation,
  no-transfer, no-contact, no-disclosure, image-free, writing-free and
  material-free.
- Never sell, buy, pledge, traffic, confine, threaten or claim ownership over a
  person. Do not use religion, debt, marriage, family duty, caste, employment
  or the story to demand labour, sex, custody, obedience or surrender.
- A spouse's, child's, worker's or distressed person's absent agency is not
  consent. Do not pressure reunion, reconciliation, forgiveness or contact.
- Do not approach, touch, move, undress, wash, photograph or handle a dead body
  or enter a cremation/funeral workspace from this guide. Follow authorised
  family, institutional, medical, police and funeral directions.
- Do not rank dignity by caste, tribe, birth, surname, family, occupation,
  wealth, observance, disability, health, employment or contact with death.
- Keep normal medicine, meals, hydration, child feeding, hygiene and sleep. Do
  not begin a fast, vigil or parana, and do not drive or perform safety-critical
  work while sleep-deprived.
- Do not tell anyone that poverty, debt, bereavement, exploitation, illness,
  job loss, distress or abuse is punishment, karma, impurity or moral failure.
- Serious harm is not erased by fasting, vigil, hearing, truth claims or regret.
  Preserve immediate safety, evidence, accountability, restitution, reporting,
  qualified support and law.
- Do not promise reunion, resurrection, custody, restored employment or
  property, debt relief, wealth, sin removal, absolution, heaven or any result.
