## Applicability and deterministic timing

The ordinary lane applies only after the user's Indian city and accepted
ordinary Smarta Panchang resolve Ashvina Krishna Ekadashi. Reviewed 2027
Indian discovery evidence places **Indira Ekadashi on Sunday, 26 September
2027**. An Amravati daily calendar shows both **Indira Ekadashi** and
**Ekadashi Shraddha** on that date; this coincidence does not merge the two
applicability lanes.

The lane-local calendar census records Indira in the north-reference,

Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Odisha or Andhra Pradesh while
the identity, safe household sequence, authority and close remain materially

## Fixed Padma Purana story: Indrasena, Narada and his father

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande's English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara
Khanda, chapter 58, together with the displayed Sanskrit:

- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365635.html>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/padma-purana-sanskrit/d/doc428113.html>

The complete developed sequence is:

1. Yudhishthira asks Krishna the name of Ashvina Krishna Ekadashi.
2. Krishna names Indira and attributes removal of great sin, benefit to
   ancestors in an adverse rebirth and Vajapeya merit to it and its hearing.
3. King Indrasena rules Mahishmati, has sons and grandsons, wealth and grain,
   and is devoted to Vishnu and spiritual contemplation.
4. Narada visits. Indrasena welcomes him and asks why he has come.
5. Narada says that in Yama's world he saw Indrasena's father, who despite
   many meritorious acts was there because of a defect or interruption in a
   vow connected with a former birth.
6. The father asks his son to give the merit of the Indira vow so that he may
   reach heaven. Narada conveys the message and tells the king to observe.
7. Indrasena asks for the date and procedure. Narada begins on Dashami with
   morning and midday bathing, śrāddha, one meal and sleeping on the ground.
8. For Ekadashi the source says to wash the face without cleaning the teeth,
   vow to remain without food and pleasures, bathe before a Śālagrāma stone,
   worship Vishnu with incense and sandal and keep a night vigil.
9. On Dwadashi it directs Vishnu worship and śrāddha with wheat flour,
   barley, rice, sesame, beans, wheat and gram, feeding and gifts to
   Brahmanas, and a controlled-speech meal with kin, daughter's son and sons.
10. Indrasena observes with the palace household, sons and servants. The
    source attributes a rain of flowers and his father's travel on Garuda to
    Vishnu's abode to the vow.
11. Indrasena later installs his son as king and reaches heaven. The chapter
    closes with claims of freedom from sin, enjoyment and long residence in
    Vishnu's world from reading or hearing.

This is a source-labelled royal ancestor, vow-defect, merit-transfer and
afterlife story. Its formal procedure is source context, not the ordinary
consumer vidhi.

## Corroborating Narada Purana formal expression

Reviewed *Narada Purana*, Purva-bhaga, chapter 120, verses 41b-44:
<https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/narada-purana-english/d/doc1857985.html>

It names Indira on Ashvina Krishna Ekadashi and describes fasting, midday
śrāddha before Śālagrāma, Padmanabha worship next morning, feeding Brahmanas
and dakshina. It attributes uplift of many ancestors to the rite. This
corroborates that a formal Indira expression is materially bound to ancestor,
food, image, recipient, fast and authority questions. It is not evidence that
every household or every Indira observer performs śrāddha.

## Separate Ekadashi Shraddha denominator

The frozen selected scope independently names `ekadashi-shraddha`. Existing
Devam Pitru Paksha remembrance material explicitly does not decide who should
perform śrāddha. A formal answer can change according to the deceased
relationship, death tithi, family lineage, sampradaya, performer authority,
materials, food rules, priest/officiant, location and closing. Those are
material differences, not small regional decoration.

Therefore the ordinary Indira lane must not:

- ask for a deceased person's private details or death tithi;
- decide eligibility, heirship, gender or who performs;
- prescribe piṇḍa, tarpaṇa, rice, sesame, grain, water, feeding or dakshina;
- ask anyone to handle, bathe or worship a Śālagrāma;
- claim that a deceased person suffers because of an incomplete vow or a
  living relative's omission;
- promise merit transfer, ancestor uplift, heaven, Vishnu's abode, a message
  from the deceased, dream contact or any afterlife outcome; or
- count this pack as completion of Ekadashi Shraddha.

If the user elects a formal family rite, route them to the authority they
actually accept without inventing a universal national procedure. A private,
material-free remembrance remains a complete ordinary Indira form.

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Afterlife certainty:** Yama's world, Garuda travel, heaven, Vishnu's abode
  and adverse rebirth are source claims. Do not present them as verified facts
  or guarantees.
- **Blame and ritual debt:** the father's condition is attributed to a vow
  defect/former-birth obstruction. Death, grief, illness or adversity is not
  proof of sin, failed ritual or a relative's negligence.
- **Merit-transfer pressure:** the father asks his son to transfer merit. No
  child, descendant, relative, carer or other person owes ritual labour,
  fasting, payment or observance to rescue a deceased person.
- **Caste and rebirth hierarchy:** the English expression uses a degrading
  birth-status phrase and the formal procedures privilege Brahmana
  recipients. Preserve these as source facts without slurs in consumer copy;
  caste or birth never ranks dignity, grief, prayer, remembrance, food,
  safety or participation.
- **Male-line assumptions:** sons, grandsons and a daughter's son appear in
  the story and close. Gender, marital status, fertility, adoption, kinship,
  caste and priestly access are not eligibility conditions for the ordinary
  lane.
- **Grief and privacy:** never compel disclosure, public naming, photographs,
  contact, reconciliation, forgiveness or a story about the deceased. A user
  may remember privately, stop, choose silence or do nothing.
- **Formal restrictions:** no fasting, tooth-cleaning restriction, sleeping
  on the floor, vigil, incense, sandal, food preparation, feeding or donation
  is imported into the safe ordinary route.

## Consumer meaning

A bounded ordinary meaning is: **remember a deceased or absent elder with
care while continuing one safe value or responsibility that is genuinely
yours, without claiming knowledge of the afterlife or making grief a debt**.

The remembered person need not be an ancestor, parent, biological relative,
male or publicly named. A person who does not wish to remember anyone may
reflect on continuity and care in general or skip the observance. This is a
Devam synthesis, not a claim that the source prescribes the material-free
form.

Optional no-purchase actions include privately recalling one quality,
continuing one already accepted care responsibility, checking on a living
dependent or elder with consent, or preserving an existing lawful memory or
photo privately with the relevant consent. No contact, disclosure, social
post, donation, money, forgiveness, reconciliation or action is required.

## Complete ordinary household routes

### Minimum private form

1. Confirm local ordinary Indira Ekadashi and distinguish it from separately
   applicable Ekadashi Shraddha or another authority.
2. Keep normal food, water, medicine, insulin, hygiene, child feeding, sleep
   and dental care. Begin no fast, vigil or parana.
3. Choose private remembrance, a general reflection on continuity, silence
   or no observance. No deceased person's name or details are required.
4. Remember Vishnu/Padmanabha with a familiar name, family prayer or silence;
   no image, mantra, offering or material is required.
5. Hear the complete Indrasena sequence: Narada's message, attributed vow
   defect, requested merit transfer, formal Dashami-to-Dwadashi procedure,
   observance and attributed afterlife result.
6. State the boundary: these are source claims, not proof that a deceased
   person suffers, a descendant owes rescue or a ritual changes the afterlife.
7. Optionally continue one safe value or care responsibility already yours,
   or choose no action.
8. Close with normal food, water, rest and privacy. Claim no merit transfer,
   ancestor uplift, heaven, sin removal, message or guaranteed result.

### Consent-led family form

Use the same fed, hydrated, rested and material-free core. Give an easy no and
a private exit. Ask whether anyone wants a name spoken, not for a death tithi,
cause, image or family history. Do not rank grief, ancestors or performers;
assign son, heir, priest, debtor, rescuer or Brahmana-recipient roles; stage
Yama or afterlife messages; pressure tears, contact, forgiveness, disclosure,
fasting, food, gifts or donation. A silent or absent participant is complete.

### Named temple or institution participation

Verify the institution's current date, visitor status, Indira versus Ekadashi
Shraddha programme, fasting/food rules, Śālagrāma and image authority,
ancestor-name collection and privacy, priest/officiant eligibility,
materials, fees/donation, accessibility, crowd plan, photography and exit.
Remain only in the authorised visitor area. Do not copy a formal rite home or
provide names, money, food or personal history under pressure. A cancelled,
remote or home form is complete.

## Materials and substitutions

No material is required. Optional existing supports are a familiar Vishnu or
