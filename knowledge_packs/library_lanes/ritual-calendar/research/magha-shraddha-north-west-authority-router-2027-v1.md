# Magha Shraddha North/West authority-router research (2027)

## Frozen denominator

This dossier selects exactly the accepted `magha-shraddha` denominator item.
It is a Pitru Paksha observance selected through Magha Nakshatra in the relevant
aparahna, not an alias for the ordinary death-tithi Shraddha on the same civil
day. It remains separate from:

- Dwadashi, Trayodashi or Chaturdashi Shraddha;
- Magha Trayodashi when Magha Nakshatra and Trayodashi materially overlap;
- Maha Bharani and Sarva Pitru Amavasya;
- Bengal Mahalaya listening, Tamil Mahalaya Amavasai and Odia Mahalaya
  Amabasya;
- a named Gaya, ghat, temple, priest, sampradaya or family procedure.

The lane is weighted to North/West and the selected major states. A state does
not receive a separate route for language, spelling, clothing, familiar food,
photograph/no-photograph or prayer/silence alone.

## Existing material audit

The Delhi 2026 Pitru Paksha fixture and bilingual remembrance pack label Magha
Shraddha on 7 October 2026 with Magha Nakshatra. They correctly warn that this
label does not decide a particular ancestor or formal performer. This is useful
partial evidence, not a complete lane: it has no complete Magha-aparahna
selection rule, overlap router, current 2027 answer, Magha-specific narrative,
formal-authority route or explicit rejection of the current page's universal
son and benefit language. It is therefore reused but not silently counted.

The completed Maha Bharani and Sarva Pitru lanes supply compatible privacy,
grief, no-contact and participant-only boundaries. They do not own Magha's
Nakshatra rule or applicability.

## Deterministic timing dependency

The reviewed 2027 New Delhi Pitru Paksha list places **Magha Shraddha on Monday,
27 September 2027**, the same listed civil day as Dwadashi Shraddha. This is a
comparison fixture only.

The current practitioner rule states:

1. Magha Shraddha is selected during Pitru Paksha when Magha Nakshatra prevails
   in the relevant aparahna;
2. if it occupies part of aparahna on two days, select the day with the longer
   Magha-aparahna overlap;
3. if Magha Nakshatra and Trayodashi both prevail in aparahna, the source calls
   the combined case Magha Trayodashi Shraddha;
4. a formal route additionally needs the named authority's Kutup, Rohina,
   aparahna, performer, ancestor and closing rules.

Never copy 27 September, any Magha interval, Dwadashi identity, formal window,
priest schedule or temple/ghat programme to another city, year or authority.
If current city, Panchang or authority is unresolved, the material-free private
remembrance route remains the complete generic answer.

## Meaning, attributed claims and who commonly observes

Current practitioner material associates Magha Nakshatra with the Pitrs and
describes Magha Shraddha as a special Pitru Paksha occasion. It particularly
mentions ancestors whose death date is unknown or whose rite was missed. It
also attributes to Hemadri a rule that all sons, living together or apart,
perform separately, and makes very large claims about merit, ancestor
satisfaction, Vishnu-loka, health, longevity, wealth, progeny, peace, marriage,
fertility, lineage and removal of Pitru Dosha.

These statements are evidence of a living textual/practitioner lane, not Devam
facts, guarantees or automatic eligibility. Devam does not turn “all sons” into
a generic policy, assign gendered duty, diagnose Pitru Dosha, or infer unknown-
death-date applicability without a named current authority.

The bounded consumer lane applies to:

- a person or family whose established elder, priest, text, sampradaya or
  institution says Magha Shraddha applies to a named ancestor/context;
- a bereaved user seeking private remembrance while formal applicability is
  unresolved;
- a participant attending a named rite after timing, role and materials have
  been confirmed by its current owner.

No descendant, son, daughter, spouse, widow, widower, adopted person, unmarried
person, childless person, child or mourner automatically owes performance,
fasting, offerings, donation, priest payment, ancestor rescue or defect repair.

## Source-labelled stories

### 1. Magha's Pitru Paksha identity

The observance's living identity is a Magha-Nakshatra selection within Pitru
Paksha, decided through aparahna rather than a fixed Gregorian date or ordinary
death tithi. The 2027 New Delhi fixture happens to place it beside Dwadashi;
that does not make Dwadashi the defining rule. The current source's Pitrs,
unknown-date and benefit statements remain attributed practice claims. This is
not evidence that every family performs the same rite, every ancestor is
eligible, or omission causes harm.

### 2. Nachiketa and Yama in the Katha Upanishad

The fixed lawful narrative is Friedrich Max Muller's 1884 public-domain
translation of the Katha Upanishad in *Sacred Books of the East*, volume 15.

Vajasravasa gives possessions in a sacrifice. His young son Nachiketa sees that
the cows being given are exhausted and repeatedly asks to whom he will be
given. His angry father says that he gives him to Death. Nachiketa reaches
Yama's abode and waits three nights while Yama is absent. When Yama returns, he
offers three boons in recompense for the waiting guest.

Nachiketa first asks that his father become calm, recognise him and welcome him
without anger. Yama grants reconciliation. For the second boon Nachiketa asks
about the fire teaching associated in the text with heaven; Yama teaches it and
names it for him. For the third boon Nachiketa asks what happens after death,
noting that people disagree. Yama offers long life, descendants, wealth, power,
music and pleasure instead. Nachiketa refuses what is temporary. Yama then
distinguishes the good from the merely pleasant and teaches about Self,
knowledge, discipline and death. The text closes by saying Nachiketa received
the knowledge and attained its goal.

This is reflective source context, not Magha Shraddha's origin or formal vidhi.
No child leaves home, approaches danger, fasts, waits without food or water,
obeys abuse, reconciles with an unsafe parent, performs fire rites or seeks
contact with Yama or a dead person. The story proves no afterlife fact, ancestor
message, compulsory reconciliation or guaranteed relief from grief.

## Complete generic routes

The pack must provide three fully ordered routes:

1. **private minimum:** resolve the local date if possible; ask whether a named
   authority says a formal route applies; retain normal care; hear the two
   labelled stories; remember through a name, value, safe memory or silence;
   choose one optional living-care act or none; close without contact, defect or
   result claims;
2. **opt-in family remembrance:** distinguish Magha from same-day tithi and
   overlap cases; invite with an easy no; protect privacy and grief; retain
   ordinary food, water, medicine and sleep; tell both bounded stories; allow
   different, absent or painful memories; use only a familiar family prayer if
   wanted; close without assigning performer or interpreting signs;
3. **named formal participant:** identify the current family/priest/
   sampradaya/institution; verify ancestor applicability, death-date exception,
   performer, local Magha-aparahna overlap, tithi overlap, Kutup/Rohina window,
   sequence, materials and venue; bring only requested safe affordable items;
   remain participant-only; retain consent, health, water, fire, privacy and
   cost limits; use the owner's safe close and copy nothing home.

## Materials and substitutions

Nothing is required for private or family remembrance. Optional supports are a
labelled story, written name, consented photograph, flower, ordinary drinking
water, familiar safe food, private note or supervised electric/light source.
Each can be replaced by a spoken name, safe memory, silence, ordinary care,
electric light or nothing.

Pinda, sesame, kusa, ritual water, tarpan vessels, honey, kheer, fire, lamps,
recitation texts, priestly dress, Brahmin feeding, animal feeding, offerings,
clothing donations and dakshina belong only to a named formal authority. The
generic pack never supplies their quantity, preparation, recipient, mantra,
sequence or disposal.

## Major variants only

Minor language, spelling, dress, ordinary food preference, photograph/no-
photograph, familiar prayer and silence remain unified.

Separate routes are required only when evidence materially changes:

- known versus unknown death-date applicability and missed-rite claims;
- performer eligibility, including the Hemadri/all-sons claim, under a named
  current authority;
- Magha Trayodashi or another tithi overlap affecting the selection rule;
- named family, priest, sampradaya, Gaya, ghat, temple or institution timing,
  ordered action, materials, access and closing;
- Maha Bharani, ordinary tithi Shraddha and Sarva Pitru Amavasya;
- grief, abuse, estrangement, adoption, lineage uncertainty, child/dependent,
  food/medicine, water/fire/crowd, privacy, accessibility or cost needs.

A future state split needs source evidence of changed timing, applicability,
performer, ordered action, materials, safety or closing—not popularity or
translation alone.

## Safety and non-coercion

- No fast, dehydration, meal delay, medicine change, ritual bath, river/ghat
  entry, fire, smoke, crowd, travel, animal feeding, food disposal, donation,
  recitation, purchase or priest payment is required.
- No child or dependent copies Nachiketa's departure, danger, waiting, abusive
  command, unsafe reconciliation or fire. Guardian safety overrides completion.
- No one owes forgiveness, positive memory, family disclosure, belief,
  photograph use, touch, public grief, gotra claim or unsafe-relative contact.
- Dreams, birds, animals, food, water, flame, coincidences and feelings are not
  ancestor messages.
- Do not diagnose Pitru Dosha or promise ancestor satisfaction/liberation,
  Vishnu-loka, merit, health, longevity, wealth, progeny, marriage, fertility,
  lineage repair, prosperity, peace or reunion.
- Do not shame or assign duty by gender, kinship, marriage, fertility,
  adoption, lineage or participation status.
- Immediate danger uses 112. Medical, water, fire, animal, food,
  safeguarding, venue and legal rules override ritual completion.
