## Fixed Padma Purana story: Mucukunda, Candrabhaga and Shobhana

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande's English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara
Khanda, chapter 60, together with the displayed Sanskrit:

- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365637.html>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/padma-purana-sanskrit/d/doc428115.html>

The complete developed sequence is:

1. Yudhishthira asks Janardana the name of Kartika Krishna Ekadashi. Krishna
   names Rama and attributes removal of great sin to it.
2. King Mucukunda is described as friend of Indra, Yama, Varuna, Kubera and
   Vibhishana, a truthful Vishnu devotee whose kingdom has had troublesome
   people removed.
3. His daughter Candrabhaga is “given” in marriage to Shobhana, son of
   Candrasena. The source does not narrate her consent to that marriage.
4. Shobhana visits his father-in-law's home when Ekadashi approaches.
   Candrabhaga worries because he is very weak, cannot bear hunger and her
   father's rule is severe.
5. On Dashami Mucukunda has a drum beaten to announce that nobody may eat on
   Vishnu's day.
6. Shobhana asks Candrabhaga what to do. She says nobody in her father's house
   may eat; elephants, horses, young animals and other animals may receive
   neither grass/food nor water; a human who eats will be censured. She tells
   him to make his mind strong.
7. Shobhana says he will fast and accepts what destiny brings. His choice
   occurs inside royal, household and social coercion and is not free,
   health-informed consent.
8. Hunger oppresses his body and causes extreme distress. Other Vishnu
   devotees worship and keep vigil, while the night is painful for him. At
   sunrise Shobhana dies.
9. Mucukunda has him cremated with royal wood. Candrabhaga does **not** abandon
   her body with her husband; after the funeral rites she remains at her
   father's house. The chapter gives no consumer funeral procedure.
10. The source attributes to the Rama vow Shobhana's arrival in an ornate but
    later-described-as-unstable divine city on Mandara mountain, with throne,
    royal ornaments, gandharvas and celestial nymph attendants.
11. The Brahmana pilgrim Somasharman sees Shobhana, who asks about Mucukunda,
    Candrabhaga and the city. Somasharman asks how he obtained the marvellous
    city.
12. Shobhana says his Rama fast produced the unstable city and asks that it be
    made stable. He attributes the instability to observing without faith and
    asks Somasharman to tell Candrabhaga.
13. Somasharman returns and tells Candrabhaga he saw her husband and city.
    She asks to be taken there and says her vow merit will stabilise the city
    and reunite them.
14. Somasharman takes her to Vamadeva's hermitage near Mandara. After hearing
    the account, Vamadeva sprinkles her with water and Vedic mantras. The
    source attributes a divine body and condition to the mantra and vow.
15. Candrabhaga reaches Shobhana. He seats her on his left. She says she has
    observed Ekadashi with faith since after age eight and that her merit will
    make the city stable and abundant until the final deluge.
16. The couple enjoy divine forms and pleasures together. The chapter compares
    Rama with a wish-gem and wish-cow, equates bright and dark Ekadashis, and
    promises enjoyment, salvation, sin freedom and honour in Vishnu's world.

This is a source-labelled story of coercive fasting, death, survival,
bereavement, messenger contact, merit, transformation and attributed reunion.
It is not a fasting success story or modern grief procedure.

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Royal coercion:** Mucukunda's drum announces a household/kingdom-wide food
  ban. Government, employer, school, institution, elder or family authority
  must not coerce fasting, vigil or worship. Consent under threat of censure is
  not free consent.
- **Death from fasting:** Shobhana is known to be weak, becomes extremely
  distressed from hunger and dies at sunrise. Do not call his death auspicious,
  successful, destined, deserved or proof of devotion. Stop restriction and
  seek urgent care for danger signs.
- **Animal deprivation:** the source withholds food and water even from young
  animals. Never restrict an animal's food or water for a human observance.
  Do not approach or handle an unfamiliar animal from this guide.
- **Child fasting:** Candrabhaga says she observed from after age eight. This
  is a source fact, not permission to fast, dehydrate or sleep-deprive a child.
  Children keep normal food, water, medicine, school and sleep.
- **Candrabhaga's knowledge and pressure:** she recognises Shobhana's weakness
  but relays her father's rule and censure. Do not make a spouse responsible
  for enforcing a dangerous family practice or blame her for a coerced system.
- **Marriage and gender:** Candrabhaga is “given” in marriage without narrated
  consent. Shobhana calls for her merit to stabilise his city. A spouse is not
  property, ritual labour or a rescue resource; no one owes marriage,
  widowhood conduct, merit, reunion or service.
- **Survival after husband's death:** the source explicitly says Candrabhaga
  does not abandon her body with him and remains at her father's home. Preserve
  this fact. Never romanticise self-harm, sati or death with a spouse.
- **Funeral and remains:** cremation and funeral rites are story events, not
  authority to approach, touch, move, wash, dress, photograph or handle a dead
  body, pyre, ash, remains or memorial object.
- **Bereavement contact:** Somasharman reports seeing Shobhana in a divine city.
  Do not claim to contact the dead, relay messages, diagnose an afterlife
  location, interpret dreams/signs or pressure a grieving person to believe.
- **Merit and wife-as-rescuer:** Candrabhaga's accumulated merit stabilises the
  source-city. No woman, spouse, child, relative or survivor owes ritual merit
  to rescue, stabilise or reunite with another person.
- **Vedic sprinkling and divine body:** Vamadeva's act is a source event, not
  permission to sprinkle another person, recite restricted/formal mantras,
  promise transformation or improvise a teacher-led rite.
- **Faith blaming:** Shobhana attributes instability to lack of faith. Illness,
  death, grief, poverty, instability or failed hopes are not proof of weak
  faith or improper observance.
- **Caste and royal hierarchy:** Brahmana/best-Brahmana and royal ranking are
  textual facts, not measures of dignity, truth, prayer, safety or access.
- **Miracle guarantees:** no divine city, body, reunion, wealth, pleasure,
  salvation, sin removal or Vishnu-world result is guaranteed.

## Consumer meaning

A safe Devam synthesis is: **care must protect life, food, water, rest and
free choice; religious pressure never makes a dangerous restriction sacred**.

The chapter's most important safety fact is not the attributed city but that
Shobhana's known weakness and distress are followed by death. A second bounded
reflection is that Candrabhaga remains alive after his death; survival is not
disloyalty. These are Devam safety interpretations, not claims that the source
authors a modern consent or bereavement ethic.

An optional no-purchase action is to check that food, drinking water, medicine
and rest remain available to people and animals already under one's lawful
care, or to remove one pressure phrase from a family plan. Do not enter another
home, confront an authority, feed an unfamiliar animal, change medical care or
take responsibility beyond your safe role. No action is required.
