Checked 2026-08-13. This evidence freeze covers the ordinary named
`ekadashi-shravana-putrada` item and its currently non-material `Pavitra
Ekadashi` regional alias. It does not prescribe fertility, fasting, parana,
pavitraka craft or a universal national ritual.

## Applicability and deterministic timing

The ordinary lane applies to a North/West Smarta household or individual only
after the user's Indian city and accepted ordinary Panchang resolve Shravana
Shukla Putrada Ekadashi.

Reviewed 2027 Indian discovery evidence places **Shravana Putrada Ekadashi on
Thursday, 12 August 2027**. The Gujarati calendar labels the same civil date
**Pavitra Ekadashi**. The frozen Ekadashi disposition maps Pavitra to Shravana
Putrada because the reviewed date and paksha/tithi match; no separate state
pack is created without evidence of a changed deity/story, eligibility,
ordered practice, close or safety boundary.

The date is a reviewed fixture, not a portable rule. Recompute the user's city,
ordinary authority and any separate Vaishnava, family or institution result.
Do not copy tithi clock times or a parana window.

## Fixed narrative: Padma Purana 6.55

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara Khanda,
chapter 55:
<https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365632.html>

The source sequence is:

1. Yudhishthira asks Krishna about Shravana Shukla Ekadashi.
2. Krishna introduces a sin-removing account and a Vajapeya-merit comparison.
3. King Mahijit of Mahishmati is distressed because he has no son; the text
   says a sonless man has no happiness in this world or the next.
4. Mahijit publicly lists his just rule, including not taking Brahmana, deity
   or deposited wealth and punishing wrongdoers.
5. Brahmanas, subjects and the family priest consult and enter a forest seeking
   an answer.
6. They meet Lomasha, described as an ancient, fasting, self-controlled sage
   whose hair falls once each kalpa.
7. The people ask how the king can have a son. Lomasha meditates on a former
   birth.
8. He says Mahijit was formerly a poor Vaishya trader who, while thirsty,
   stopped a cow and calf from drinking and drank the water himself.
9. Lomasha attributes the king's childlessness to that act and his kingship to
   other merit.
10. He directs the people to observe Shravana Shukla Putrada, stay awake and
    transfer the vow's merit to the king.
11. The source says the queen conceived and later gave birth to a bright son.
12. The chapter then gives a separate, detailed pavitraka passage: on Shravana
    Shukla Dvadashi with the Sun in Cancer, a ring/thread object is made for
    Vasudeva from gold, silver, copper, silk, lotus, kusha, kasha or cotton cut
    by a Brahmana woman; it includes bathing, three-strand purification,
    preliminary consecration, preceptor and Brahmana honour, vigil, caste- and
    community-labelled practices and different hymn instructions.
13. It says men should fashion pavitrakas every year, threatens fruitless
    worship if the rule is not followed and attributes son-pleasure, sin
    removal, heaven and salvation.

This is a developed source story plus a formal ritual passage. It is not modern
fertility medicine, reproductive ethics, animal-care instruction, caste policy
or a self-authorizing household craft manual.

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Male-heir hierarchy:** the account treats a son as the desired and
  happiness-giving outcome. Devam must affirm equal dignity of daughters,
  sons, nonbinary children, adopted children, foster relationships, people
  without children and people who do not want children.
- **Infertility blame:** the story attributes childlessness to a prior-life
  act. Devam must never tell a person that infertility, miscarriage,
  pregnancy loss, disability or a child's sex is punishment, karma, impurity
  or moral failure.
- **Absent reproductive agency:** the unnamed queen's conception is narrated
  as the result of others' vow and merit transfer. Her consent, wishes,
  health, sexual history and medical circumstances are absent. Do not turn the
  story into permission for family pressure or reproductive surveillance.
- **Miracle causation:** the chapter attributes conception and birth of a son
  to the vow. Do not promise conception, pregnancy, a male child, healthy
  birth, fertility-treatment success or removal of reproductive grief.
- **Collective coercion risk:** subjects fast and transfer merit for the king.
  No person, employee, subject, relative or community owes fasting, vigil,
  merit, money, prayer or reproductive labour for another person's wish.
- **Animal-water causation:** depriving a cow and calf of water is the stated
  former-life act. Preserve compassion and water access as story meaning, but
  do not tell users to approach, touch, feed, water, restrain or rescue animals
  from this guide. Use qualified local animal carers where needed.
- **Caste and community hierarchy:** the chapter names Brahmana, Kshatriya,
  Vaishya, Bhilla and Shudra groups and assigns materials/hymns by status. Do
  not rank dignity, eligibility, spiritual worth or instruction access by
  caste, tribe, birth, occupation or surname.
- **Threat and promised result:** fruitless worship, sin removal, son-pleasure,
  heaven and salvation remain source claims, not guarantees.

## Consumer meaning

The safest useful reflection is: **hold hopes without demanding another
person's body or a child's sex, protect fair access to ordinary care, and do
not turn devotion into coercion or expensive display**.

This synthesis preserves the account's concern with denied water and communal
care while rejecting infertility blame, son preference, forced merit transfer,
caste hierarchy and miracle guarantees.

## Complete ordinary household routes

### Minimum private form

1. Confirm ordinary Shravana Putrada/Pavitra for the user's city and accepted
   ordinary authority; stop if a named Vaishnava, family or institution result
   differs.
2. Keep normal food, water, prescribed medicine, insulin, child feeding,
   hygiene and sleep. Begin no fast, vigil or parana.
3. Remember Vishnu/Vasudeva with a familiar name, family prayer or silence; no
   image or material is required.
4. Hear the source-labelled Mahijit–Lomasha account in full sequence, including
   the denied water, collective vow, unnamed queen, attributed son-birth and
   formal pavitraka passage.
5. State the boundaries: childlessness is not punishment; no child or sex is
   owed; the queen's absent agency is not consent; ritual does not cause or
   guarantee conception.
6. If useful, choose one private equal-care action already within the person's
   responsibility: preserve someone's access to ordinary drinking water with
   consent, keep a medical appointment, support an existing child or elder
   without sex preference, or contact a qualified animal carer about an actual
   welfare concern. No disclosure or purchase is required.
7. Close with normal care, privacy and accountability; claim no son,
   conception, pregnancy, cure, sin removal, heaven or salvation.

### Consent-led family form

Use the same fed, rested and material-free core. Participation, source hearing,
hope and care actions are optional and private. Never ask who is trying to
conceive, who has miscarried, who uses fertility care, who is pregnant, why a
person has no child or what sex a child should be. Do not compare daughters and
sons or pressure marriage, sex, conception, adoption, treatment, fasting,
donation or merit transfer.

### Named temple or institution participation

Verify the institution's current date, public programme, access, food, plant,
thread, metal, flame, photography, donation, crowd and safeguarding rules.
Attend only as a voluntary public participant. Do not copy its pavitraka,
mantra, caste role, fast, vigil, gift or parana into a household guide. A
remote, cancelled or home form is complete.

## Materials and substitutions

No material is required. Optional existing supports are a familiar Vishnu or
Vasudeva image/text and a private reminder. Each has a no-image, no-writing and
silent substitution.

Do not require or source gold, silver, copper, silk, cotton, lotus, kusha,
kasha, thread, rings, Tulasi, lamps, flames, food, gifts or paid items. The
chapter's pavitraka materials and caste/preceptor sequence require a separately
owned formal-practice lane; they are not consumer substitutions.

## Safety, privacy and dignity

- A complete ordinary form is non-fasting, fed, hydrated, rested, no-vigil,
  no-flame, no-animal-contact, no-purchase, no-pavitraka, no-thread, no-ring,
  no-disclosure, image-free, writing-free and material-free.
- Never ask about pregnancy, abortion, miscarriage, fertility, sexual history,
  medical records, adoption plans or desired child sex. Do not shame or
  obstruct lawful confidential care.
- Do not tell anyone that infertility, pregnancy loss, disability, illness or
  a child's sex is punishment, karma, impurity or moral failure.
- Do not promise a son, conception, pregnancy, healthy birth, fertility cure,
  treatment success, sin removal, heaven, salvation or family approval.
- Do not approach, touch, feed, water, restrain or rescue an unfamiliar animal
  from this guide. Use qualified local help for an actual welfare concern.
- Do not rank dignity by caste, tribe, birth, family, gender, child status,
  sampradaya, occupation, wealth, observance, disability, health or
  reproductive history.
- No one owes fasting, vigil, merit transfer, prayer, money, sex, pregnancy,
  childbirth, reproductive labour or a child of any sex for another person's
  hope.
- Serious harm is not erased by fasting, merit transfer, pavitraka, hearing or
  regret. Preserve accountability, qualified care and law.

The ICMR *Guidelines for Management of Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus* ground the
no-fasting-without-clinical-guidance boundary. The Medical Termination of
Pregnancy Act, 1971, including section 5A confidentiality, grounds the
reproductive-privacy boundary. Neither is individual medical or legal advice.

## Major variants only

Keep Shravana Putrada and Pavitra as aliases within one ordinary lane while
the reviewed date, story, eligibility, ordered safe practice and close remain
the same. Language, familiar Vishnu/Vasudeva name, image/no-image, silence,
dress and voluntary participation are minor.

Require separate routes for:

- Pausha Putrada and its Suketumat story;
- materially different Vaishnava, family or institution timing/parana;
- the formal pavitraka craft, preceptor, caste-labelled role, hymn and annual
  obligation;
- any fertility, conception or reproductive rite;
- clinician-directed fasting or professional care;
- a named temple's live programme; and
- another story, deity focus or observance.
