# Naga-Garuda observance batch: frozen evidence matrix (2027)

Reviewed 2026-08-13. Exactly three accepted denominator items are included:
`nag-panchami`, `nagula-chavithi`, and `garuda-panjami`. Coverage before this
batch is 180 of 208.

## Source-labelled narrative

Ganguli's public-domain Mahabharata, Adi Parva Astika Parva, supplies a fixed
shared narrative spine. Kadru and Vinata receive boons; Kadru becomes mother of
the snakes and Vinata mother of Garuda. After a wager and deception Vinata is
enslaved. Garuda asks how to free her; the snakes demand amrita. He obtains and
delivers it, securing his mother's freedom. Elsewhere in the Astika sequence,
Janamejaya begins a sacrifice intended to destroy the snakes after Takshaka
kills his father; Astika asks that the sacrifice stop, and the king ultimately
grants the request. Packs label coercion, deception, predation, revenge and
mass killing as narrative events, never ritual instructions or justification
for harming people or wildlife.

## Living practice and animal-safety boundary

Current calendar pages describe images, serpent names, prayer, fasting and
milk offerings; TTD documents a named Garuda Vahana procession. These are
attributed living practices, not a national vidhi. The safe household route
uses an existing image/drawing or no object. It never directs a user to seek,
capture, handle, confine, display, feed, pour liquid into, disturb or kill a
snake; never directs entry into an anthill or burrow; and never requires animal
feeding. Milk is not a required material.

Government/WHO guidance controls emergencies. NDRF advises not trying to catch
or kill a snake and to seek proper medical treatment. India's National Action
Plan identifies the belief that snakes are fond of milk as a harmful myth and
warns against non-medical practices. WHO states that antivenom is the specific
treatment capable of preventing or reversing most snakebite envenoming effects.
In a suspected bite: move away from danger, keep the person still, seek urgent
medical help/112, and do not substitute mantra, cutting, sucking, tourniquets,
stones, food or drink for clinical care.
