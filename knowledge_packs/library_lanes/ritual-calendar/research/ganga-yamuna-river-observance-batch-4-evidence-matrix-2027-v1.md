## Source-labelled stories

### Bhagiratha, Shiva, Ganga and Jahnu

Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 42-44 supplies the fixed narrative spine. The
ancestral task passes to Bhagiratha, who performs austerities so Ganga may
descend and reach the ashes of Sagara's sons. Ganga's force must be received;
Shiva catches her in his matted hair and later releases streams. She follows
Bhagiratha, floods Jahnu's sacrificial ground, is swallowed by him and is
released after the gods and sages appeal, becoming Jahnavi. Bhagiratha leads
her to the ocean and the ancestors; Brahma acknowledges the accomplishment.

This source explains the descent/Jahnavi identities used by current Ganga
Dussehra and Ganga Saptami pages. It is not proof that bathing removes moral
harm, a command to enter water, a model for extreme austerity, or permission
to subordinate living people to ancestor duty.

### Ganga Puja regional expression

The current Assamese calendar explicitly places Ganga Puja on Jyeshtha Shukla
Dashami, 13 June 2027, the same lunar identity as Ganga Dussehra. The discovery
evidence establishes a distinct Bengali/Assamese consumer label, but not one
universal household sequence. Safe household gratitude and an owner-led
regional/temple route are therefore the complete bounded answer.

### Yamuna identity

The current Yamuna Chhath page identifies Chaitra Shukla Shashthi as Yamuna
Jayanti and gives special importance to Braj, Mathura and Vrindavan. It names
Yamuna as a goddess and, in its stated tradition, as Krishna's wife. The
current Shri Yamuna Maharani Temple at Vishram Ghat lists Yamuna Chhath as a
special-puja occasion. These are current attributed identities; they do not
make every riverbank, temple practice or Krishna story universal.

## Typical practice and safe consumer route

Current pages describe or imply river/ghat bathing, worship, flowers, lamps,
food, recitation, aarti, donation and temple participation. Devam's household
route is: resolve the day; keep ordinary care; clean a stable place; use a
river image, bowl of potable water or no material; hear the labelled story;
offer gratitude without putting anything into a river; choose a practical
water-care action; share ordinary food; and close without promised merit.

The elaborate route is participant-only: verify the named temple/ghat,
schedule, access, crowd, weather, water, boat, flame, offerings, photography,
payment and closing. No app-authored river entry, immersion or disposal.

## Major variants only

- Ganga Dussehra versus Bengali/Assamese Ganga Puja is a named regional owner
  difference even when the tithi/date coincide.
- Ganga Saptami's Jahnu/Jahnavi identity is materially distinct from the
  descent-focused Dashami route.
- Yamuna Chhath's Braj/Mathura temple and river route is materially distinct
  from a private household form and from November Surya Chhath.
- Language, one safe flower, bowl/no bowl, image/no image and prayer/silence
  are minor expressions, not state variants.

## Safety and lawful-use boundary

The Gujarat Institute of Disaster Management holy-dip advisory supports crowd,
ghat, designated-area, child-supervision, instruction-following and emergency
boundaries. National Mission for Clean Ganga directions prohibit immersion of
idols, puja material and other religious offerings in rivers and water bodies.
No river entry, drinking untreated water, boat ride, flame, smoke, litter,
animal feeding, fasting, donation or purchase is required. Respect local water
quality, weather, police, disaster-management, temple and environmental rules;
use 112 for immediate danger in India.
