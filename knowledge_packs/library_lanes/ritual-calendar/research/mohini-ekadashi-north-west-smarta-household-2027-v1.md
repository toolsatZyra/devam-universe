## Fixed source-labelled account

The reviewed N. A. Deshpande English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara
Khanda chapter 49, lines 54-82, is framed by Yudhishthira asking Krishna about
Mohini. Krishna says Rama had asked Vasistha after suffering separation from
Sita. Vasistha identifies Mohini and tells the story of Dhrishtabuddhi.

In Bhadravati, the prosperous Vaishya Dhanapala builds public water places,
wells, temples, gardens, tanks and houses and is devoted to Vishnu. His fifth
son Dhrishtabuddhi is described through moralising language about sex, gambling,
alcohol, prohibited food, failure to worship and spending family wealth. His
father expels him and kin abandon him. The translation says women it calls
"harlots" also abandon him after his money is gone. Hungry, unclothed and
distressed, he steals. Royal officers repeatedly bind, release, whip and torment
him before banishing him. In a forest he kills deer, pigs, birds and other
animals for food.

He reaches Kaundinya's hermitage. The chapter says slight contact with the
sage's wet garment removes his sins and misfortune. Dhrishtabuddhi asks for a
way to salvation; Kaundinya prescribes Mohini fasting. The text says he observes
the vow, his sins vanish, he receives a divine body and travels on Garuda to
Vishnu's world. It attributes extraordinary merit to observing, reciting and
hearing the account.

## Story boundaries and excluded conflations

- This chapter is **not** a narration of Vishnu's Mohini form distributing
  amrita, the Samudra-manthana, Bhasmasura or Shiva-Mohini traditions. The name
  alone does not license importing those stories into this lane.
- Rama's separation from Sita is the framing reason for his question, not a
  promise that ritual resolves abduction, grief, separation, trauma or unsafe
  relationships. Sita's experience must not be reduced to Rama's distress or
  used to pressure a survivor toward reunion.
- The translation's sexual and occupational labels are source-era moralising
  language, not Devam descriptions. Adult consensual sexuality and sex workers
  are not impurity, temptation or causes of another person's conduct. Do not
  repeat the slur "harlot" in ordinary consumer copy.
- Gambling or alcohol-related harm can be named as conduct and health/safety
  risk without diagnosing addiction or defining a person's worth. Qualified
  treatment and harm-reduction support are not replaced by prayer.
- Theft, exploitation and killing animals are serious actions in the story.
  Accountability, restitution, safety and law remain. They do not justify
  family abandonment, homelessness, starvation, stripping, binding, whipping,
  torture, banishment, humiliation or dehumanisation.
- Contact with a sage's garment and immediate absolution are theological
  narrative claims, not mechanisms. No touch, wet cloth, bathing, priestly
  contact, private access, fast or payment removes wrongdoing or illness.
- The forest killings are not hunting instructions. India Code identifies
  section 9 of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 as prohibition of hunting,
  subject to the Act's qualified provisions. Devam gives no hunting or
  wildlife-emergency advice; use lawful authorities and immediate safety routes.
- "Sins vanish," divine body, Garuda journey, Vishnu's world and merit equal to
  gifts are source claims, not guarantees or substitutes for real accountability.

## Bounded present-day meaning

A safe ordinary meaning is **interrupt harm without abandoning human dignity**.
Privately identify one pattern within one's own control—such as unsafe spending,
lying, harmful substance use, gambling, theft, cruelty, boundary violation or
avoidance of qualified help—and choose one proportionate interruption. Examples
include blocking a gambling transaction, removing access to a weapon, returning
or replacing property through a safe lawful route, asking a qualified service
for help, arranging safe food or shelter without coercion, protecting wildlife
by contacting the appropriate authority, or respecting permanent no-contact.

Do not force disclosure or select a pattern for another person. A survivor,
family member, sex worker, unhoused person or person with substance-use or
mental-health needs does not owe rescue, reconciliation, forgiveness, access or
participation. Serious or immediate danger requires emergency or qualified
support; ritual remains optional accompaniment.

## Safe ordinary household route

- Confirm the local date and authority; note the chapter's bright/dark-half
  inconsistency rather than deriving timing from it.
- Choose normal food, water, medicine, hygiene and sleep; the non-fasting form
  is complete.
- Remember Vishnu with a familiar name, prayer or silence.
- Hear a bounded chapter-49 account. Omit slurs and graphic animal inventory;
  label punishment, garment contact, absolution and promised results as source
  events, and state that Mohini-avatar stories are separate.
- Privately choose one lawful harm-interruption step within one's own control.
- Close with gratitude, normal care and no claim of absolution, restored body,
  recovery, reconciliation, heaven or a guaranteed result.

No material is required. Optional: an existing Vishnu image/text, water or
ordinary food for oneself, a verified helpline/resource, or a secure private
reminder. Silence, no image, no writing, no purchase and no contact are complete.

## Safety and safeguarding

- Do not begin or intensify fasting. Never change insulin, medicine, meals,
  hydration, hygiene or sleep; an inherited fast requires qualified advice.
- No vigil, bathing, wet garment, touch, private priest meeting, confession,
  donation, animal contact, forest visit, hunting, punishment or travel is
  required.
- Never bind, beat, whip, starve, strip, isolate, expel, shame, threaten or make
  someone homeless to correct conduct. For immediate danger, use proportionate
  lawful safety and emergency routes.
- Do not call a sex worker, sexually active person, person using alcohol, person
  who gambles, accused person, unhoused person or distressed person impure,
  wicked, fallen, prostitute/harlot or beyond dignity.
- Do not diagnose addiction, possession, curse, mental illness or criminality
  from a story. Do not stop medication or substitute ritual for treatment,
  rehabilitation, legal advice, safeguarding or crisis care.
- Serious theft, violence, exploitation, wildlife harm or abuse is not erased
  by touch, fasting, prayer or regret. Preserve safety, evidence, restitution,
  reporting options and legal duties without demanding victim contact.
- Do not approach wildlife, enter forests, carry weapons or reproduce hunting.
  Use current lawful wildlife/emergency guidance for an actual situation.
- No sin removal, absolution, purity, divine body, recovery from addiction,
  restored relationship, relief from grief, heaven, salvation or other result
  is guaranteed.

## Major-variant decisions

- **Within lane:** language; image/no image; water/ordinary food/no offering;
  private reminder/no writing; familiar prayer/silence; individual/family;
  non-fast or already inherited clinically safe fast. These minor differences
  do not create state lanes.
- **Separate timing/authority:** Gauna or Vaishnava date, food, fast, mantra,
  vigil and parana rules.
- **Separate story owner:** Mohini-avatar, Samudra-manthana, Bhasmasura or
  Shiva-Mohini narratives. Shared naming is not enough to merge them.
- **Separate professional route:** substance-use, gambling, mental-health,
  homelessness, criminal, survivor, wildlife, medical, legal or safeguarding
  support. Ritual may accompany but never replace it.
- **Separate institution:** a named temple's current programme, priest-led rite,
  access, safeguarding, fees or offerings.
- **Separate named observance:** Varuthini, Apara or another Ekadashi.

No major state-specific difference was evidenced for the ordinary household
lane across the reviewed ordinary Hindu, Telugu and Indian ISKCON fixtures.
Minor language, food and family expression remain in one lane. A materially
different Gauna/Vaishnava civil date is already a frozen separate applicability
item rather than a state variation.
