## Fixed Padma Purana expression

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande's English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara
Khanda, chapter 38, together with the displayed Sanskrit:

- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365615.html>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/padma-purana-sanskrit/d/doc428093.html>

The complete chapter is broader than the commonly retold Mura episode:

1. Mahadeva, Narada and Shiva frame a Mahadwadashi inquiry. Shiva lists Jaya,
   Vijaya, Jayanti and Papanashini conjunctions and assigns fast, gift,
   sacrifice, sin-removal, vision and inexhaustible-fruit claims.
2. After Yudhishthira's fifth horse sacrifice, he asks Krishna about fasting,
   nakta and eating once. Krishna locates the cold-season Margashirsha dark
   observance and distinguishes householder and ascetic nakta timings.
3. The chapter ranks bathing places but says home bathing is excellent and
   warns against troubling living beings in water. It gives an earth prayer,
   anger/greed restraint and one-pointed attention.
4. It orders avoiding conversation with Shudras, heretics, liars, critics of
   Brahmanas and other named people; directs Vishnu worship, food offering,
   house lamps, no sleep or sex, scripture recreation, night vigil, Brahmana
   gifts and apology.
5. It praises equal bright/dark Ekadashi observance above pilgrimage, eclipse
   bathing, sacrifices and cow gifts; promises removal of birth, uncountable
   merit and no rebirth; and ranks fast, nakta and one meal.
6. Yudhishthira then asks how Ekadashi arose. Krishna tells of Mura, who defeats
   and expels the gods. Indra asks Shiva for help; Shiva sends the gods to
   Vishnu. Indra praises Vishnu and describes Mura's seizure of divine roles.
7. Vishnu and the gods go to Chandravati. Vishnu's disc kills hundreds in
   Mura's army, but Mura defeats the gods and Vishnu in a close battle lasting
   a thousand divine years.
8. Vishnu withdraws to Badarikashrama, sleeps in the one-door Simhavati cave,
   and is pursued by Mura, who intends to kill him while asleep.
9. An armed maiden emerges from Vishnu's lustre/body. She fights Mura and
   reduces him to ash with her humkara. Vishnu wakes, asks who killed Mura and
   calls the deed ferocious and compassionate. She explains her protection;
   Vishnu asks her to choose a boon.
10. After asking him to repeat his promise three times, she asks to become
    chief among sacred places, remove difficulties and grant well-being,
    wealth, righteousness and salvation to people who fast, take nakta or eat
    once. Vishnu assents and calls her Ekadashi/a power.
11. The chapter repeats bright/dark equality, listening, sunrise/Dwadashi/
    Trayodashi and mixed-tithi rules, thousandfold merits and Vishnu-world
    results.

This full sequence is the source-labelled story and practice context. Do not
replace it with a one-line "goddess kills evil" moral, invent a nonviolent
episode, or detach the story from its warfare, sleep attack, female divine
agency, boon and fasting claims.

## Secondary fixed corroboration

*Narada Purana* chapter 120, verses 56b-59a, independently names Utpanna or
Utpatti on Margashirsha Krishna Ekadashi. It directs fasting; Dwadashi Krishna
worship with scents/upacaras; feeding leading Brahmanas; dakshina; then eating
with friends and kin; and promises an aerial chariot and Vishnu's region.

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Violence and dehumanising labels:** the account contains mass killing,
  combat, pursuit of a sleeping person and beheading/immolation language in
  reception variants. Preserve source facts without using demon/asura language
  for real people, groups, illness, disagreement or inner traits. Do not enact
  weapons, battle, killing, pursuit, sleep attack, humkara destruction or an
  enemy ritual.
- **Female divine agency:** Ekadashi emerges armed, defeats Mura, speaks for
  herself and negotiates a boon. Do not erase her agency into "Vishnu killed
  Mura," sexualise her, turn her into a submissive helper, or claim women must
  protect sleeping men. Mythic violence is not gender advice.
- **Sleep and safety:** Vishnu's source sleep is not a vigil command. Never
  deprive a person of sleep, approach or startle a sleeper, test alertness, or
  treat exhaustion as spiritual weakness.
- **Caste and social exclusion:** the command not to speak with Shudras,
  outcastes and other named groups is a historical source fact, not guidance.
  Never rank dignity, food, speech, prayer, safety or access by caste, birth,
  occupation, belief, community or purity label.
- **Automatic absolution and cosmic results:** fasting is said to erase sins,
  prevent rebirth, defeat enemies, bring wealth/righteousness/salvation and
  lead to Vishnu's region. Ritual never erases harm, evidence, reporting,
  accountability, restitution, repair or legal duty; none of those outcomes is
  guaranteed.
- **Fasting hierarchy:** the chapter ranks fasting, nakta and one-meal forms
  and directs day/night restrictions. No one owes food/water restriction,
  altered medicine or sleep. A fed, hydrated, rested form is complete.
- **Sexual conduct and privacy:** source abstinence belongs to its formal vow.
  Do not ask about or regulate consensual adult intimacy from the ordinary
  guide. Route safety, abuse, reproductive and medical needs appropriately.
- **Food, gifts and Brahmana hierarchy:** food offering, Brahmana gifts,
  apology, feeding, scents, upacaras and dakshina are formal source practices.
  Do not solicit money, food, property, gifts or apology, stigmatise another
  person's food, or rank recipients by birth.
- **Flame, bathing and materials:** a house lamp and bathing instructions are
  source facts. Do not introduce flame, smoke, river/tank entry, ritual earth,
  water handling or food offerings. Ordinary hygiene is enough.
- **Literal cosmic control:** the emergence and boon are source-labelled
  religious narrative, not evidence that a household act creates a deity,
  kills an enemy or controls cosmic outcomes.
