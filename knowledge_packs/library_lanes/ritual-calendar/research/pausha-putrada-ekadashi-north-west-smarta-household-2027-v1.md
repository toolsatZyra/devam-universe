# Pausha Putrada Ekadashi North/West Smarta household research dossier

Reviewed 2026-08-13. This dossier supports the ordinary Smarta household and
ordinary temple-participant lane only. The 2027 Gauna/Vaishnava next-day lane,
formal fertility rite, priest-led vrata and medical fast remain separate.

## Applicability and deterministic timing

- Primary applicability: an ordinary North/West Indian Smarta household or
  individual whose accepted local Panchang resolves Pausha Shukla Ekadashi as
  Pausha Putrada Ekadashi.
- North-reference, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh and other reviewed regional
  calendar fixtures place the ordinary observance on **Monday 18 January
  2027**. They separately place **Gauna Pausha Putrada Ekadashi** and, where
  shown, **Vaishnava Pausha Putrada Ekadashi** on **Tuesday 19 January 2027**.
- The Tuesday result is not an alternate spelling or state decoration: it
  changes the civil day, authority and parana applicability and remains one of
  the frozen six material Ekadashi lanes.
- Product resolution must use the user's Indian city, accepted Panchang and
  tradition. Never copy the discovery-page clock times or a parana window.
- Language, ordinary fruit/flower, image/no-image, dress, family-known prayer,
  hope/no reproductive hope, and non-fast/fast choice do not create state
  copies. A named temple programme remains live and institution-controlled.

## Fixed source-labelled account

N. A. Deshpande's English translation of *Padma Purana*, Uttara Khanda,
chapter 41 provides a fixed reviewed expression. Yudhishthira asks Krishna
about the Ekadashi in Pausha's bright fortnight. Krishna names it Putrada,
associates it with Vishnu and introduces king Suketumat of Bhadravati and queen
Campaka. The couple have no son. The chapter repeatedly voices a patriarchal
world in which a male heir continues lineage and ancestral offerings; the king
becomes despondent and thinks of ending his life before rejecting suicide and
leaving for the forest. Hungry and thirsty, he reaches a lake and meets sages
identified as the Visvedevas. They call the day Putrada Ekadashi and tell him
to observe its vow. The chapter says he follows their advice, breaks the fast
on Dvadashi, and that the queen later bears a son who becomes king.

Consumer retelling rules:

1. label the account as Padma Purana, Uttara Khanda chapter 41—not history, a
   universal origin, medical evidence or a prediction;
2. preserve, rather than silently modernise, the chapter's male-heir preference,
   ancestral-line assumptions, couple's grief and suicidal thought;
3. explicitly state that son preference is a feature of this textual world,
   not Devam guidance: daughters and children of every gender have equal worth,
   and parenthood, marriage and biological children are never duties imposed by
   this lane;
4. do not imply that infertility, pregnancy loss, childlessness, adoption,
   assisted reproduction or having daughters reflects sin, deficient devotion,
   family failure or unpaid ancestral debt;
5. do not assign the queen responsibility for conception or make any partner
   responsible for another person's health, lineage or spiritual result;
6. do not turn the chapter's fast, Dvadashi close or attributed birth into a
   fertility treatment or guaranteed outcome; and
7. treat suicidal thought as a safety signal, not a devotional turning point to
   imitate. A consumer in immediate danger needs emergency and trusted-person
   support, not fasting or isolation.

The story may support a bounded reflection on holding family hopes without
coercion, caring for existing children and dependants, and choosing a concrete
act of equal care. It must not become a conception procedure.

## Proposed safe consumer routes

1. **Minimum non-fasting household route:** resolve the ordinary Smarta date;
   sit safely; remember Vishnu/Krishna or use silence; hear the source-labelled
   account with its patriarchal assumptions named; write one hope without
   demanding an outcome; choose one equal-care action for a child, dependant,
   partner or community member; close with food, water, medicine and rest as
   normally needed.
2. **Standard family route:** the same core with an identifiable short reading,
   a consent-led conversation about equal worth and family pressure, and one
   concrete care/service action. No one discloses fertility, pregnancy, loss,
   adoption, medical status or family plans.
3. **Ordinary temple participant route:** verify the live schedule, exact date,
   access, crowd, food, donation, photography and safeguarding rules; attend as
   a visitor only; do not copy priest-led sankalpa, mantra, vrata, havan,
   fertility promise or parana.

## Materials, substitutions and close

- Required material: none. A quiet place is enough.
- Optional: water, locally available fruit, an existing Vishnu/Krishna image or
  text, and paper for one care action. No image, silence, tap water, no fruit
  and a mental note are complete substitutions.
- Do not buy a fertility product, paid ritual, gold, special medicine or costly
  offering from this guide. Never share medical records, intimate history, OTP,
  bank credentials or identity documents with an unverified claimant.
- No flame is required; use an electric light or daylight. Supervise and fully
  extinguish any flame.
- The non-fasting route closes immediately with gratitude and the care action.
  An inherited fast or parana remains under the same local family/temple/
  sampradaya authority and the person's clinician. Devam supplies no clock time.

## Health, grief and safeguarding

- A non-fasting form is complete. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people,
  older adults, people with diabetes or another health condition, anyone taking
  medicines with food, people with eating-disorder history, and people doing
  heat or heavy labour must not start or intensify a fast from Devam.
- ICMR's type-1-diabetes guideline explicitly treats day-long Ekadashi fasting
  as high risk and supports exemption without guilt. Do not alter insulin,
  medicines, meals or hydration.
- This lane is not fertility, obstetric, mental-health or crisis care. Ritual
  cannot diagnose infertility, ensure conception, determine a child's sex,
  prevent loss or replace qualified care.
- Never pressure a person to conceive, continue a pregnancy, disclose a loss,
  pursue or avoid treatment, adopt, marry, remain married, or perform a vrata.
- If the story's suicidal thought is personally activating, stop the observance,
  do not remain alone, contact a trusted person and seek local emergency or
  crisis support appropriate to the person's location. Devam must not present a
  ritual as crisis treatment.
- Children participate only with a responsible guardian; no adult may use
  blessings, secrecy, touch, private meetings, gifts or photographs to bypass
  safeguarding or consent.
