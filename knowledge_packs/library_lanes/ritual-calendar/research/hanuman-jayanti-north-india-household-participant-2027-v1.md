# Hanuman Jayanti North/shared lane research dossier

## Selected denominator

- Inventory item: `hanuman-jayanti-north-india-participant-v1`.
- Authored lane: `hanuman-jayanti-north-india-household-participant-2027-v1`.
- Consumer boundary: a household, individual, family-led, or ordinary temple-
  participant answer for the Chaitra Purnima observance used widely in North
  and West India. It does not reproduce priest-led deity service, the full
  Hanuman story, or the Hanuman Chalisa text.
- 2027 reference fixture: Tuesday 20 April, Chaitra Shukla Purnima. The cited
  Nathdwara fixture gives Purnima from 04:51 on 20 April (shown as 28:51+ on
  19 April) until 03:56 on 21 April (27:56+ on 20 April). These are local
  reference times, not a national clock rule.

## Reuse and source audit

No existing user-complete Hanuman Jayanti ritual pack was found. The inventory
already normalizes Hanuman Janmotsava/Jayanti spellings to this lane and keeps
Tamil Hanuman Jayanthi distinct. The canonical registry already owns the Dutt
Ramayana expression and `devam:text:hanuman-chalisa`; this lane therefore sends
typed links instead of copying owner content.

The retained Uttara Kanda source-vault object has SHA-256
`076d03a68387f8ccd43b0cc211829f017ef120746d7b0f68a401f1c6fb4b221d`.
In Section XL, Rama names Hanuman's courage, patience, intellect, knowledge and
service in the search for Sita. Agastya then gives one childhood account:
Anjana and Kesari are named, Vayu is described as begetting the child, the
hungry child reaches toward the rising sun as fruit, Indra strikes him, and
Vayu withdraws the air. This is one source-labelled account, not a universal
birth theology or a complete life story.

No state variation is created merely because a state name differs. Maharashtra
and Rajasthan current sources support the shared Chaitra lane. Bihar evidence
also proves why one temple's Kartika date must not be promoted to a state-wide
rule. West Bengal, Odisha, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Andhra Pradesh are
not silently treated as identical; their named date/practice lanes remain for
their own evidence-backed work where materially different.

## Action boundary

The complete minimum form is non-fasting and material-light: resolve the local
date, hear the source-labelled account, use a family-known prayer/name or
silence, choose one concrete act of courageous and reliable service, and close
with thanks. The standard household form may add a clean image/book, flower,
water, fruit/sweet, an owned Chalisa or Ramayana reading, and a lamp only when
safe. None is compulsory. Temple participation requires a live schedule and
institution rules; the visitor does not infer abhisheka, sindoor/oil service,
mantra, havan, or deity handling from observation.

## Rejected claims and safeguards

- No guaranteed protection, strength, wish fulfilment, healing, exorcism,
  victory, merit, or medical benefit.
- No universal fast, sunrise puja, Sanskrit text, 108 repetitions, sindoor,
  oil, laddoo, betel, coconut, or temple visit.
- Do not ingest sindoor or temple oil. Do not use unknown loose powder on skin,
  eyes, mouth, children, food, or animals. Keep oil off walking surfaces and
  fire. A CDSCO committee record and a national lead assessment document the
  risk of lead in some commercial sindoor.
- Real monkeys are wildlife, not ritual participants. Do not feed, touch,
  crowd, photograph closely, or leave food accessible; Wildlife Institute of
  India material links feeding with habituation, snatching and attacks.
- Fasting is optional. DGHS advises insulin users not to fast and says people
  with diabetes should if possible refrain; treated type-2 users need clinician
  advice on drug timing. Prescribed food, water and medicine take priority.
- Prasad requires clean preparation/storage and allergen disclosure. Temple
  queues require live accessibility, weather, crowd and exit checks.
