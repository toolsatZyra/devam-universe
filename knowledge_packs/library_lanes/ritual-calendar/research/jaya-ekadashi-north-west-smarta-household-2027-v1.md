## Fixed source-labelled account

N. A. Deshpande's English translation of *Padma Purana*, Uttara Khanda,
chapter 43 identifies Magha Shukla Ekadashi as Jaya. The chapter places Indra
in heaven overseeing music and dance. Malyavat and Pushpadanti/Pushpavati are
celestial performers attracted to one another; their attention affects the
timing and execution of their performance. Indra interprets this as disobedience
and curses both into what the translation calls goblin forms on earth.

The chapter describes them in the Himalaya suffering cold, bodily distress,
grief and loss of peace. On Jaya Ekadashi they have no food or water, kill no
living being, remain by an ashvattha tree, tremble, cling to each other for
warmth and do not sleep. The text interprets this involuntary deprivation and
wakefulness as observance; on Dvadashi their forms are restored and they return
to heaven. The chapter attributes release, sin-destruction and other vast
results to the vow.

Consumer retelling rules:

1. label this as Padma Purana, Uttara Khanda chapter 43—not history, proof of
   ghosts/goblins, a universal origin or guaranteed curse removal;
2. omit the chapter's extended objectifying inventory of Pushpavati's body from
   ordinary consumer copy while noting that the reviewed expression describes
   attraction in explicitly gendered and objectifying language;
3. do not frame mutual attraction, consensual affection, a performance error or
   sexuality as sin. The curse is a source event involving a powerful ruler's
   extreme punishment, not Devam disciplinary guidance;
4. do not treat supernatural transformation as a diagnosis of mental illness,
   disability, trauma, unusual behaviour or another person's identity;
5. do not reproduce hunger, thirst, cold exposure, sleeping outdoors, isolation,
   wakefulness or physical clinging. These are involuntary story events, not a
   fast, vigil, intimacy instruction or proof of devotion;
6. do not teach that victims must endure punishment together, forgive an
   authority, return to an unsafe workplace or relationship, or obtain ritual
   purity before deserving dignity; and
7. report release, Vaikuntha, absolution and sacrificial merit only as the
   chapter's claims, never consumer outcomes.

A bounded present-day reflection is to resist dehumanising labels, notice when
power responds disproportionately to error, make a proportionate repair when
one has caused harm, and protect dignity without using a ritual to erase legal,
workplace or interpersonal accountability.

## Materials, substitutions and close

- Required material: none. A quiet, warm and safe place is enough.
- Optional: an existing Vishnu/Krishna image or text, water/fruit, and paper for
  one repair. No image, silence, no food offering and a mental note are complete.
- No outdoor tree, mountain, cold, bare ground, fasting, water restriction,
  touch, partner or all-night practice is required.
- No flame is required. Use an electric light/daylight or none; supervise and
  fully extinguish any flame.
- The non-fasting route closes immediately with warmth, hydration, food,
  medicine, rest and the chosen action. An inherited fast/parana remains under
  the same local family, temple or sampradaya authority and clinical guidance.

## Safety and safeguarding

- A non-fasting form is complete. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people,
  older adults, people with diabetes or another condition, anyone taking
  medicines with food, people with eating-disorder history, and people doing
  heat/heavy labour must not start or intensify fasting from Devam.
- ICMR's type-1-diabetes guideline treats day-long Ekadashi fasting as high risk
  and supports exemption without guilt. Never change insulin, medicines, meals
  or hydration.
- If someone is cold, dehydrated, faint, confused, unsafe outdoors or deprived
  of sleep, stop the observance and address immediate safety; do not interpret
  symptoms as spiritual progress or supernatural transformation.
- Touch, embracing, relationship discussion, music, dance, performance,
  photography and public storytelling require consent. Children stay with a
  responsible guardian; no adult uses ritual authority for private access.
- A religious story cannot diagnose possession or justify restraint, beating,
  isolation, forced fasting, medication withdrawal or exorcism. Seek qualified
  health/emergency support for distress or danger.
- Workplace, teacher-student and leader-participant power remains subject to
  consent, safeguarding, grievance and law. Ritual does not require silence,
  reconciliation or return to an unsafe authority.
