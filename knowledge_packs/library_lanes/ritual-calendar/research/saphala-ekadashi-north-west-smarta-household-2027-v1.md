# Saphala Ekadashi North/West Smarta household research dossier

Reviewed 2026-08-13. This dossier supports one named household and ordinary
temple-participant lane. It does not support a universal Ekadashi vrata,
Vaishnava date override, priest-led rite, medical fast or guaranteed result.

## Applicability decision

- Primary route: ordinary North/West Indian Smarta household or individual who
  wants a safe Vishnu remembrance on a locally resolved Saphala Ekadashi.
- Included states for discovery and wording: Delhi/NCR, Haryana, Rajasthan,
  Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Punjab, Maharashtra and Gujarat. Bihar,
  Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, Odisha, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and
  Kerala may use the route only when their own accepted Panchang identifies the
  same ordinary Smarta observance; the lane does not replace local calendars.
- Ordinary invited temple participation is a live-check route. Temple vrata,
  archana, abhisheka, sankalpa, mantra, donation, food and parana remain under
  that temple's authority.
- Minor differences in language, fruit, flowers, image/no-image, dress, short
  familiar prayer and voluntary non-fast/fast choice stay in this lane.
- A different civil date, Gauna/Vaishnava selection, inherited vrata sequence,
  medical fasting plan or named temple liturgy is material and stays outside.

## Deterministic timing evidence

The frozen North-reference calendar and current DrikPanchang discovery page
place Saphala Ekadashi on Sunday 3 January 2027 and Thursday 23 December 2027.
The name is Pausha Krishna Ekadashi; its repetition in one civil year is not a
second named authoring item. These are discovery fixtures, not portable clock
times. Product resolution must use the user's city, accepted Panchang and
tradition profile. Any next-day parana window must be computed or confirmed
from that same profile and must never be copied from a foreign-city page.

ISKCON and other Vaishnava calendars can select or label observances through
their own rules. The generic household lane asks the user which authority they
follow and routes away when the result differs.

## Fixed source-labelled narrative

N. A. Deshpande's English translation of *Padma Purana*, Uttara Khanda,
chapter 40 identifies Saphala as Pausha Krishna Ekadashi and presents a dialogue
between Yudhishthira and Krishna. The chapter first describes Vishnu worship
with names, fruits, incense, lamps and night vigil. It then tells of Lumpaka,
the eldest son of king Mahishmata of Champavati. The text describes Lumpaka as
violent, thieving and expelled; cold, weakness and circumstance leave him
without food or sleep near a tree associated with Vishnu. He places gathered
fruit at its root and asks that Vishnu be pleased. The text interprets this
unplanned abstinence, offering and wakefulness as the observance, after which
he regains the kingdom, changes his conduct and eventually relinquishes it.

Consumer retelling rules:

1. label this as the Padma Purana chapter's account, not history or a universal
   origin of Ekadashi;
2. preserve the story's violence, wrongdoing, expulsion and accidental nature
   without turning deprivation or exposure into recommended fasting;
3. do not teach that ritual merit excuses theft, violence or other harm;
4. do not turn restoration of kingship, wealth, family or liberation into a
   promised consumer result; and
5. extract the bounded reflection that change includes stopping harm, making a
   simple offering and relinquishing wrongful conduct—not merely obtaining a
   desired outcome.

The chapter's detailed fruit list and lamp/vigil form belongs to that textual
account. A consumer route may offer locally available fruit and an electric
light as substitutions, and must not require an all-night vigil.

## Living-practice reuse and limits

The existing bilingual recurring Ekadashi packs safely support:

- confirm the exact place/tradition date first;
- remember Vishnu or Krishna with a family-known name or prayer, with silence as
  a valid substitute;
- read or hear a short identifiable source passage;
- choose one act of restraint, repair, generosity or service; and
- let an established family, temple or sampradaya control vrata and close.

They intentionally do not prescribe grains, water restriction, medication
changes, Smarta parana or a universal mantra. That boundary must remain.

## Safety and substitutions

- A non-fasting form is complete. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people,
  older adults, people with diabetes or another health condition, anyone taking
  medicines with food, people with eating-disorder history, and people doing
  heat or heavy labour must not start or intensify a fast from Devam.
- ICMR's type-1-diabetes guideline explicitly discusses day-long Ekadashi fasts
  in its high-risk fasting context and supports exemption without guilt. Devam
  never changes insulin, medicine, meals or hydration.
- Do not reproduce Lumpaka's cold exposure, hunger, faintness, sleeplessness or
  forest isolation. Rest, eat, hydrate and seek appropriate help when unwell.
- No flame is required. Use an electric lamp or no light; any diya must be
  continuously supervised and extinguished before leaving or sleeping.
- Any clean locally available fruit may substitute for the chapter's list. No
  purchase, rare ingredient, betel nut, donation or temple visit is required.
- Do not leave fruit under a public tree, feed unsuitable food to animals, or
  create litter. Consume it safely or give suitable food directly and lawfully.
- A short daytime reflection substitutes for night vigil. Sleep deprivation is
  not a test of devotion.

## Proposed consumer routes

1. **Minimum non-fasting household route:** resolve date; wash hands and settle;
   place water or fruit if desired; remember Vishnu/Krishna or sit in silence;
   hear the labelled short story; name one harmful habit to stop and one repair;
   close safely and carry out the repair.
2. **Standard family route:** use the same core with an identifiable short
   reading, voluntary fruit sharing and a concrete service/reconciliation act.
   Do not add a universal mantra, food rule or parana.
3. **Ordinary temple participant route:** verify the live programme, access,
   crowd and food rules; attend only as an invited participant; follow staff;
   decline touch, food, payment or photography; leave safely. Do not perform or
   imitate priest-led actions from this guide.
