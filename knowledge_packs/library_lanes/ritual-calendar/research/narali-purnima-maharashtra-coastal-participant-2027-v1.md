## Existing-material audit

No complete Narali Purnima pack exists. Raksha Bandhan already names it as a
materially separate same-day observance and cannot be reused as the answer.
The lunar-phase inventory has one exact candidate. Existing source material is
current public description, not a fixed Varuna story or formal community
liturgy.

Akashvani (2025) reports Koli communities in Mumbai, Thane and Palghar offering
coconuts to the sea, praying for calm waters/protection, decorating Koliwadas
and performing group dances. Maharashtra Tourism/Ministry of Tourism describes
the Maharashtra-Konkan fishing-community setting, coconut food, boat repair and
decoration, Varuna prayer and ceremonial sea trip. The National Fisheries
Development Board also describes Shravana Purnima and a fishing-season
association. These establish living provenance; they do not authorize Devam to
teach Koli prayer words, boat rites or fishing operations.

## Claim ceiling

This lane has no verified fixed origin narrative. Consumer story content must
therefore use source-labelled living accounts:

1. a 2025 Akashvani account of Mumbai/Thane/Palghar Koliwadas;
2. a Ministry of Tourism account of preparation, coconut offering, food, song
   and dance; and
3. the livelihood-season meaning described by NFDB.

Do not claim Varuna guarantees calm water, natural-disaster protection, catch,
income or a safe voyage. Do not claim a festival date legally or scientifically
ends the monsoon or overrides a fishing ban, vessel fitness, licence, Coast
Guard/port/fisheries direction, IMD warning or INCOIS sea-state advisory.

## Bounded action design

Minimum household/shore-safe form: resolve local Shravana Purnima; hear the
three labelled living accounts; identify the fishing/coastal community whose
labour and culture the day centres; place a coconut or coconut-free symbol on a
stable dry surface; offer familiar words of gratitude for sea, rain and labour;
take one practical coastal/fisher-community support action; retain/share the
coconut or dispose of materials responsibly; close. No water entry, throwing,
Varuna mantra, boat, voyage, fishing, fast or promised result is required.

An invited shoreline participant confirms the community organizer, legal
access, exact meeting point/time, tide, IMD/INCOIS warning, footwear, barrier,
child supervision, waste and photography. They remain behind safe boundaries,
do not imitate a prayer or throw a coconut independently, and do not board or
launch a vessel unless separately authorized and operationally safe.

## Safety and livelihood boundary

- Never enter surf, rocks, breakwaters, jetties or floodwater for an offering.
  Never turn one's back on waves. Children remain within direct responsible-
  adult control and behind local barriers.
- Do not throw coconuts from crowds or where they can strike people, vessels or
  wildlife. Ordinary participation may retain, cook, share or symbolically
  present the coconut. Follow the community/local authority for managed rites.
- A coconut is not required where allergy, swallowing, blade, weight or food
  safety is a concern. Use a picture, shell already owned or no object.
- Check IMD coastal/fishermen warnings, INCOIS Ocean State/Small Vessel
  Advisory, port/harbour/community instructions and local emergency alerts.
  A clear festival calendar is not a sea-safety clearance.
- Fishing resumption follows Maharashtra Fisheries rules. Its FAQ currently
  states a 1 June-31 July mechanized-vessel ban, but the current year's order,
  vessel/crew/legal status and live advisories must be checked; Devam does not
  issue a sail/fish decision.
- Do not romanticize or costume Koli identity, enter working boats/gear, touch
  nets, photograph people/children, record prayers or post locations without
  permission. Pay fairly for food, craft or performance.
- Segregate coconut shells, flowers, plastic and food. Do not frame marine
  litter as required devotion.
