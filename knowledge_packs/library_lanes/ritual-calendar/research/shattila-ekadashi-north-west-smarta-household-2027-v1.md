## Fixed source-labelled chapter

N. A. Deshpande's English translation of *Padma Purana*, Uttara Khanda,
chapter 42 identifies Magha Krishna Ekadashi as Shattila. It frames the account
as Pulastya's teaching to Dalabhya and gives an elaborate vrata: preparation,
fast, names of Krishna, night vigil, sacrifice, offerings, gifts, and six uses
of sesame—sesame-water bath, rubbing sesame on the body, sacrifice, water
offering, giving sesame, and eating sesame.

The chapter then tells of an unnamed Brahmana woman devoted to vows, worship
and repeated fasting. It explicitly says she tormented her body with fasts and
gave away her house and possessions, yet did not give food to beggars. Krishna,
in a begging form, asks her for alms; in anger she throws a lump of clay into
his vessel. The chapter says that in heaven she receives a beautiful but empty
house corresponding to that gift. She later learns the Shattila account and the
text attributes food, clothing and wealth to the vow and generosity.

Consumer retelling rules:

1. label all of this as Padma Purana chapter 42, not history, a universal origin,
   a guaranteed karmic mechanism or evidence that deprivation causes reward;
2. preserve the chapter's critique that intense bodily mortification without
   feeding or helping others is incomplete; never praise the woman's self-harm
   through fasting;
3. do not teach that a person in need must be tested, humiliated, caste-qualified
   or given a token object; assistance should be useful, consent-based, safe and
   within means;
4. do not promise absolution, heaven, beauty, grain, clothing, wealth, health or
   freedom from poverty;
5. do not copy 108 cow-dung/cotton balls, fire sacrifice, vigil, formal mantra,
   cow/garment/umbrella/sandal gifts or recipient hierarchy into an ordinary
   consumer route; these materially change materials, authority and safety; and
6. treat the six sesame uses as a textual list, not six mandatory actions.

A bounded present-day reflection is: restraint is not a substitute for useful
care; give something the recipient actually needs and can safely use.

## Sesame applicability and safety

- **No sesame is required.** A sesame-free route is complete for allergy,
  preference, availability, household rules or environmental reasons.
- FSSAI material identifies sesame seeds and sesame products as common food
  allergens and requires cross-contact controls. Ask before serving or donating;
  disclose sesame clearly; do not assume oil, sweets, garnish or packaged food
  is safe; use separate clean utensils when accommodating an allergy.
- Do not ingest, taste, touch, bathe with or rub sesame on the body when allergy
  or sensitivity is known or uncertain. Skin application is not a medical test.
- Loose seeds and oil can create slip, choking, pest and drain hazards. Keep
  them contained, away from small children and animals, and never pour oil or
  seed mixtures into drains, rivers, soil or public places.
- Sesame in havan or any fire offering is outside the household route. Smoke,
  burns, ventilation, fuel and institutional authority materially change the
  action. No flame is needed.
- For a symbolic option, a sealed labelled packet may remain unopened and later
  be donated only if a verified recipient requests it and can safely use it.
  Otherwise choose a requested sesame-free food, hygiene item, clothing item,
  time or service.

## Proposed consumer routes

1. **Minimum non-fasting, sesame-free route:** resolve the date; settle safely;
   remember Vishnu/Krishna or use silence; hear the source-labelled account;
   identify the difference between self-denial and useful care; ask or verify
   what one recipient needs; give or schedule one suitable item/service; close
   with gratitude, normal food/water/medicine and no result claim.
2. **Standard family route:** the same core with an identifiable reading, an
   allergy check before any sesame enters the space, and a direct requested
   donation or service. Children do not handle hot oil, flame, loose seeds or
   unverified charity contact.
3. **Ordinary temple participant route:** verify the live date, authority,
   access, crowd, food/allergen, fire, donation and photography rules; attend as
   a visitor only; do not imitate havan, mantra, sankalpa, bath, body rubbing,
   formal gift, fasting or parana from this guide.

## Fasting, close and other boundaries

- A non-fasting form is complete. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people,
  older adults, people with diabetes or another condition, anyone taking
  medicines with food, people with eating-disorder history, and people doing
  heat or heavy labour must not start or intensify fasting from Devam.
- ICMR's type-1-diabetes guideline treats day-long Ekadashi fasts as high risk
  and supports exemption without guilt. Never change insulin, medicine, meals
  or hydration.
- The non-fasting route closes immediately after the useful-care action is
  agreed or completed. An inherited fast/parana remains under the same local
  family, temple or sampradaya authority, with clinical advice where needed.
- Charity must not cause debt or hardship. Verify organisations and payment
  destinations; never share OTP, bank credentials or identity documents. A
  direct small requested item or service is complete.
- Do not photograph or publicise a recipient without free consent. Giving does
  not buy gratitude, access, conversion, labour, touch, data or a spiritual
  result.
