# Gudi Padwa Maharashtra household lane: research dossier v1

Checked: 2026-08-13

## Frozen applicability denominator

This dossier supports one Maharashtra household lane: Marathi-calendar New Year remembrance on Chaitra Shukla Pratipada, with either a securely raised household Gudi or an accessible tabletop/symbolic arrangement. The user may also choose a material-light new-year reflection without constructing a Gudi. A family-established form overrides this guide.

The lane does not teach a public shobhayatra, tall street installation, temple liturgy, priest-led consecration, Chaitra Navaratri kalasha, Ugadi/Yugadi, Cheti Chand, Puthandu, Vishu, Baisakhi, Pohela Boishakh or other regional new-year rites. These may share a civil date or seasonal frame without sharing applicability, household materials, story or authority.

Minor differences in cloth colour/fabric, flower or leaf, sugar ornament, pot metal, rangoli, prayer, food and dress remain within-lane. A different calendar, deity service, public procession, inaccessible height, housing restriction or materially different state tradition requires routing or substitution rather than a duplicate “universal” ritual.

## 2027 timing fixture

The Maharashtra practitioner calendars resolve Gudi Padwa to Wednesday 7 April 2027, Chaitra Shukla Pratipada. A cited 2027 fixture gives Pratipada from 05:20 on 7 April to 04:28 on 8 April, but that clock interval is from a non-Maharashtra location and must not be copied to Mumbai/Pune/Nashik. The accepted product rule is the locally resolved civil day on which Chaitra Shukla Pratipada supports the Marathi New Year observance; sunrise/day-boundary convention and exact tithi must be recomputed for the user's Maharashtra city and accepted Panchang. Every temple or procession time is a live institutional fact.

## Meaning and source-labelled accounts

- Maharashtra Tourism describes Gudi Padwa as the Marathi New Year, the first day of Chaitra's waxing phase, spring/new beginnings and—in some farming contexts—the close of Rabi harvest activity. Its prosperity claims are beliefs, not promised outcomes.
- The same current page records a living Rama-return/victory explanation for the Gudi. Devam must label it as Maharashtra Tourism's contemporary mythic account. It is not asserted as verified history or silently attributed to a named scripture.
- Maharashtra Gazetteers from Sangli, Wardha, Beed and Greater Bombay document household Gudis with a pole, new silk cloth, flower garland and inverted metal vessel, while materials and associated practices differ. These historical ethnographic records demonstrate continuity and variation, not present-day compulsion.
- Maharashtra Tourism records neem and jaggery as a bitter-sweet metaphor for accepting joys and challenges. A Beed Gazetteer records neem eating as already falling out of use. Therefore tasting it is optional, never a completion requirement, and may be omitted for children, pregnancy, allergy, medication, illness, unknown plant identity or simple preference.
- Cleaning, rangoli, festive food, family gathering, public music/processions and environmental activity are living expressions. A household guide may include cleaning, a safe optional rangoli and food, but public event operations remain organiser-owned.

## Safe consumer boundary

The minimum form needs no pole: resolve the day, clean one space, read one source-labelled account, mark a new-year intention and close with one practical household or community action.

The standard household form permits a Gudi only after choosing a stable location away from overhead electrical lines, traffic, stairs, doors, flame and children/animals. A competent adult assembles it at ground level; nobody leans from a window/balcony or climbs an improvised chair. The pole must be stable and restrained, the inverted vessel secured against falling, and the arrangement brought down before wind, darkness, absence or local housing rules make it unsafe. A short tabletop Gudi is a complete substitution.

The Gudi materials are a clean pole/support, familiar cloth, secured lightweight vessel, optional flowers/leaves and optional sugar ornament. No item must be newly purchased. Cloth must not be the Indian National Flag. No material is tied to an electrical pole or placed near overhead lines. Rangoli remains optional, non-slip and away from exits; loose powder should not be used where inhalation, crawling children, animals or mobility aids create risk.

Food is optional. Puran poli, shrikhand, basundi or other household foods require safe water, hand hygiene, pasteurised/boiled milk as applicable, prompt serving/storage and allergen disclosure. Neem-jaggery is descriptive/optional, never medicine, detoxification, immunity, diabetes treatment or guaranteed protection. Unknown leaves are never consumed, and anyone with an allergy, clinician-directed diet, health condition, medicine interaction concern, age-related concern or uncertainty can omit it without making the observance incomplete.

## Major-difference routing

- Full-height secure household Gudi, tabletop Gudi and material-light remembrance: same lane, accessibility substitutions.
- Cloth colour, flower/leaf, sugar garland, pot metal, rangoli, prayer words and feast: minor family/material differences.
- Apartment, renter or housing-society restrictions: same lane; use tabletop/indoor symbolic form rather than unsafe outdoor installation.
- Public Girgaon/Pune shobhayatra or cultural performance: separate organiser/live-event context.
- Temple Gudi puja, deity procession or priest-led rite: separate institutional authority.
- Ugadi/Yugadi in Karnataka/Andhra Pradesh/Telangana: separate selected lane despite shared date.
- Chaitra Navaratri, Cheti Chand and solar new years: separate observances/owners.
- Neem-jaggery ingestion: optional material practice, not a separate state lane and not required.

## Review requirements

- Never claim Gudi Padwa is the one Hindu or Indian New Year.
- Never state the Rama-return account as verified history or as coming from an uncited scripture.
- Never promise prosperity, victory, peace, harvest, health, detoxification or immunity.
- Never require neem ingestion, a tall pole, balcony access, new purchases, a specific pot metal or one cloth colour.
- English/Hindi parity must cover timing, stable setup, overhead wires, falling vessel/pole, ladder/balcony, children/animals, flame, rangoli slip/inhalation, food/allergens, takedown and cleanup.
- Only major calendar, institutional and regional differences create separate lanes.
