## Fixed Padma Purana expression: a mahatmya list, not a plot

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande's English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara
Khanda, chapter 59, together with the displayed Sanskrit:

- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365636.html>
- <https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/padma-purana-sanskrit/d/doc428114.html>

The chapter has no developed king, household or transformation episode beyond
Yudhishthira's question and Krishna's answer. Its complete sequence is:

1. Yudhishthira asks Madhusudana the name of Ashvina Shukla Ekadashi.
2. Krishna names Papankusha and says Padmanabha should be worshipped.
3. The chapter compares saluting Vishnu and reciting Vishnu's names with long
   austerity and visits to all sacred places, promising desired objects,
   heaven, salvation and avoidance of Yama's torments.
4. It says even an accidental fast prevents Yama's torments despite a fearful
   sin.
5. It threatens a Vishnu devotee who censures Shiva and a Shiva/Pashupata
   devotee who censures Vishnu with hell, including a long Raurava punishment.
6. It ranks the Ekadashi fast above thousands of horse sacrifices and hundreds
   of Rajasuya sacrifices and says sin remains in the body until the fast.
7. It attributes heaven, salvation, bodily health, wife, sons, wealth and
   friends to the vow and ranks the day above Ganga, Gaya, Kashi, Pushkara and
   Kurukshetra.
8. It prescribes night vigil and fasting and promises Vishnu's state.
9. It says a male observer emancipates ten persons each from his mother's,
   father's and wife's sides; describes four-armed divine forms reaching
   Vishnu's abode; and promises protection across childhood, youth and old age.
10. It says gifts of gold, sesame, land, cow, food, water, sandals, umbrella
    and garments prevent seeing Yama.
11. It compares days without meritorious acts to a blacksmith's bellows and
    says even a poor man should make the day fruitful through bathing and
    gifts according to capacity.
12. It attributes escape from Yama, long life, wealth, noble birth and freedom
    from disease to sacrifice, bathing, recitation, meditation and other
    meritorious acts, and closes with an unqualified righteousness/heaven and
    unrighteousness/calamity contrast.

This entire māhātmya catalogue is the source-labelled narrative expression.
Devam must not manufacture a hunter, king, demon, miracle or other popular
plot and attach it to this chapter.

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Sectarian hell threats:** the chapter threatens both a Vaishnava who
  censures Shiva and a Shaiva/Pashupata who censures Vishnu. Devam can retain
  the reciprocal warning against contempt while rejecting threats, coercion
  and claims to know anyone's afterlife. No tradition, deity, community or
  person is demeaned.
- **Automatic absolution:** accidental fasting is said to avert torment after
  fearful sin. Ritual never erases violence, abuse, discrimination, fraud,
  neglect, legal duties, evidence, accountability, restitution or repair.
- **Sin embodied until fasting:** food, water, medicine, disability, illness
  or inability to fast is not impurity or proof that sin remains in a body.
- **Health and calamity claims:** illness, disability, short life, poverty or
  calamity is not proof of unrighteousness. Fasting does not guarantee health,
  long life or safety and is not treatment.
- **Wife, sons, wealth and friends:** a spouse, child, child's sex, fertility,
  money, status, friendship or reunion cannot be promised. A wife is not a
  reward and no person is owed as the fruit of observance.
- **Male lineage and emancipation:** the text addresses a man and counts
  maternal, paternal and wife's-side relatives. Gender, marital status,
  fertility, biological kinship, adoption or lineage does not determine
  ordinary eligibility, and no observer owes rescue of thirty relatives.
- **Caste and birth hierarchy:** Brahmana recipients and “noble family” are
  source facts, not a ranking of dignity, spiritual worth, food, education,
  safety, prayer or participation.
- **Donation pressure:** gold, sesame, land, cow, food, water, sandals,
  umbrella and garments are formal source gifts. Poverty does not create a
  duty to give. Do not transfer land or animals, prepare food, solicit money or
  direct a recipient from this guide.
- **Animal transfer:** a cow is not a generic material. Ownership, welfare,
  transport, veterinary, recipient and legal duties cannot be supplied by an
  ordinary ritual pack.
- **Vigil and fasting:** no one owes food restriction, water restriction or
  sleep deprivation. The complete ordinary form remains fed, hydrated and
  rested.
- **Hierarchy of places and rites:** comparisons with named pilgrimage places
  and sacrifices are the chapter's praise rhetoric, not a reason to demean
  another practice, place or community.

for another tradition and without imagining that observance erases
