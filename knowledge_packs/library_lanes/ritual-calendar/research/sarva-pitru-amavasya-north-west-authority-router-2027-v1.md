# Sarva Pitru Amavasya North/West authority-router research (2027)

## Frozen denominator

This dossier selects exactly `sarva-pitru-amavasya`, the lunar-phase
disposition's North/West-and-selected-shared ancestor-remembrance close. It is
separate from:

- generic Bengal-facing `mahalaya` transition/listening;
- Tamil `mahalaya-amavasai-tamil` and named water/tharpanam practice;
- Odia `mahalaya-amabasya-odia` and named temple/local practice;
- ordinary death-tithi Shraddha, Maha Bharani and Magha Shraddha;
- a named Gaya, ghat, temple, priest, sampradaya or family procedure.

The same civil date, new-moon interval or use of “Mahalaya” does not merge
these owner routes.

## Deterministic timing dependency

Reviewed 2027 practitioner fixtures place **Sarva Pitru Amavasya on Wednesday,
29 September 2027** in sampled North/West Indian cities. A Patna Amavasya page
shows the local Ashwina Krishna Amavasya interval beginning 10:54 on 29
September and ending 08:05 on 30 September. A Pandharpur formal-Shraddha page
shows local Kutup, Rohina and aparahna windows on 29 September. These values
are fixtures, not portable times.

The calendar rule requires:

1. the user's city and accepted North/West lunar calendar;
2. the local Ashwina Krishna Amavasya interval and civil-day selection;
3. for a formal Shraddha route, the named authority's Kutup/Rohina/aparahna
   rule and any tithi/ancestor exceptions;
4. live venue, priest or institution information where applicable.

Never copy the date, 10:54-08:05 interval, 11:55-15:55 formal windows,
performer, ancestor list or closing to another city, year or authority. If
timing or authority is unresolved, the material-free private remembrance is
the complete generic answer.

## Meaning, claims and who commonly observes

The official Ministry of Tourism page describes Pitru Paksha as a yearly
period for paying homage to ancestors. Current practitioner material describes
Sarva Pitru Amavasya as the final Pitru Paksha tithi and particularly important
for known and unknown ancestors, missed tithis and some other applicability
cases. It also lists Shraddha, tarpan, pinda, food, donations, lamps and
recitations, and makes claims about ancestor satisfaction, liberation, debt
release, family welfare, marriage, progeny, wealth and peace.

These are source-attributed living-practice statements, not Devam facts,
guarantees or universal duties. The page's statement that the day is observed
throughout the Hindu community is rejected as an applicability rule. A label
does not automatically make every Hindu, son, man, widow, daughter, adopted
person, unmarried person or child a performer.

Applicable consumers include:

- an individual seeking private remembrance of known, unknown or uncertain
  family ancestors without initiating formal Shraddha;
- an opt-in family wanting safe remembrance while formal details are resolved;
- a participant whose current elder, priest, sampradaya or institution has
  already determined applicability, performer, timing and procedure.

No user is assigned a lineage, gotra, ancestor, debt, defect, performer role,
fast, donation, water action, animal feeding, recitation or priest payment.

## Source-labelled stories

### 1. The Pitru Paksha closing identity

Current calendar material presents Sarva Pitru Amavasya as Ashwina Krishna
Amavasya at the close of Pitru Paksha. The public-cultural account describes
the period as ancestor homage; practitioner material adds a wider known-and-
unknown-ancestor applicability claim. Devam preserves this as the observance's
living identity while separating local timing, family authority and formal
procedure. It is not evidence that every family performs the same rite or that
the dead return, consume offerings, grant results or punish omission.

### 2. Nachiketa and Yama in the Katha Upanishad

The fixed lawful story expression is Friedrich Max Muller's 1884 public-domain
Katha Upanishad in *Sacred Books of the East*, volume 15.

Vajasravasa gives possessions in sacrifice. His young son Nachiketa sees that
the cows being given are worn out and repeatedly asks to whom he will be
given. In anger his father says, “to Death.” Nachiketa goes to Yama's abode
and waits three nights while Yama is absent. On returning, Yama offers three
boons to compensate the waiting guest.

Nachiketa first asks that his father become calm, recognise him and welcome him
without anger. Yama grants it. For the second boon Nachiketa asks about the
fire teaching associated in the text with heaven; Yama teaches it and names it
for him. For the third boon the boy asks what happens after death, noting that
people disagree. Yama tries to redirect him and offers long life, descendants,
wealth, power, music and pleasures. Nachiketa refuses what is temporary. Yama
then distinguishes the good from the merely pleasant and teaches about Self,
knowledge, discipline and death. The text closes by saying Nachiketa received
the knowledge and attained its goal.

This is not Sarva Pitru Amavasya's origin or Shraddha vidhi. No child leaves
home, approaches danger, fasts, waits without food/water, obeys abuse,
reconciles with an unsafe parent, handles ritual fire or seeks contact with
Yama or a dead person. The story does not prove an afterlife fact, ancestor
message, compulsory reconciliation or guaranteed grief relief.

## Complete generic routes

Three routes answer “what do I do?” without inventing a national ritual:

1. **private minimum:** resolve the local date if possible; ask whether a
   named authority says a formal route applies; retain normal food, water,
   medicine and care; hear both labelled stories; remember one known/unknown
   ancestor through a name, value, safe memory or silence; choose one optional
   living-care act or none; close without contact, debt or result claims;
2. **opt-in family remembrance:** resolve timing and distinguish remembrance
   from Shraddha; invite with an easy no and privacy; retain ordinary care;
   tell both stories with child/afterlife boundaries; allow different, absent
   or painful memories; optionally share normal safe food or care without
   calling it pinda/tarpan; close without performer assignment or omen reading;
3. **named formal participant:** identify the current family/priest/
   sampradaya/institution; verify ancestor applicability, death-tithi,
   performer, local Kutup/Rohina/aparahna, sequence, materials and live venue;
   bring only requested safe affordable items; participate without leading or
   copying; retain health, water, fire, food, privacy, cost and consent limits;
   use the owner's safe close.

## Materials and substitutions

Nothing is required for private or family remembrance. Optional supports are
a labelled story, written name, consented photograph, flower, ordinary drinking
water, familiar safe food, private note or supervised electric/light source.
Each can be replaced by a spoken name, safe memory, silence, electric light,
ordinary care or nothing.

Pinda, til, kusa, tarpan vessels, ritual water, sacred fire, lamps, recitation
texts, priestly dress, Brahmin feeding, cow feeding, offerings and dakshina
belong only to a named formal authority. The pack never generates their list,
quantity, preparation, disposal, recipient, mantra or sequence.

## Major variants only

Minor language, spelling, dress, ordinary food preference, photograph/no-
photograph, familiar prayer and silence remain unified.

Separate routes are required for:

- known versus unknown death-tithi and the authority's ancestor applicability;
- performer/karta rules and named family, priest or sampradaya procedure;
- Tamil Mahalaya Amavasai water/tharpanam and priest/safety authority;
- Odia Mahalaya Amabasya timing, temple, story and ordered practice;
- Bengal Mahalaya transition/listening and current broadcast rights/schedule;
- named Gaya, ghat, river, temple or institution timing, access and materials;
- grief, abuse, estrangement, adoption, lineage uncertainty, child/dependent,
  food/medicine, animal, water, fire, crowd, privacy, accessibility or cost
  needs.

A state route is not created for translation alone. A future split needs
evidence that timing, applicability, performer, ordered action, materials,
safety or closing materially changes.

## Safety and non-coercion

- No fast, dehydration, meal delay, medicine change, ritual bath, river/ghat
  entry, fire, smoke, crowd, travel, animal feeding, food disposal, donation,
  recitation, purchase or priest payment is required.
- No child or dependent leads or attends without safe guardian support. The
  Nachiketa story never authorises child sacrifice, abandonment, starvation,
  obedience to danger or unsafe reconciliation.
- No one owes forgiveness, positive memory, family disclosure, belief,
  photograph use, touch, public grief, gotra claim or contact with an unsafe
  relative.
- Do not interpret dreams, birds, animals, food, water, flame, coincidence or
  feelings as ancestor messages. Do not promise liberation, satisfaction,
  debt release, curse/defect removal, marriage, progeny, wealth, prosperity,
  peace or reunion.
- Do not shame or automatically assign duty to any gender, kinship, marital,
  fertility, adoption, lineage or participation status.
- Water, food, fire, animal, safeguarding, medical, venue and legal rules
  override ritual completion. Immediate danger uses 112.
