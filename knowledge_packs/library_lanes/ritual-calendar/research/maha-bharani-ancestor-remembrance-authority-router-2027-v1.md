# Maha Bharani ancestor-remembrance authority-router research (2027)

## Frozen lane

This dossier selects exactly the accepted `maha-bharani` denominator item. It
is a Nakshatra-dependent Pitru Paksha observance, not an ordinary Panchami or
Chaturthi Shraddha alias and not the Kerala Kodungallur Bharani festival.

The existing 2026 Delhi Pitru Paksha files correctly say that a Maha Bharani
calendar label does not decide whether it applies to a particular ancestor or
who may lead a formal rite. They are partial reusable applicability evidence,
not a complete 2027 lane.

## Deterministic timing and observed conflict

Current evidence demonstrates a material location/date dependency:

- a New Delhi 2027 Shraddha list places **Maha Bharani on Monday, 20 September
  2027** and labels it Ashwina/Bharani Nakshatra;
- an Indian regional calendar also displays 20 September 2027 with Bharani;
- a Drik Panchang 2027 Pitru Paksha page sampled for Leavenworth, Kansas places
  Maha Bharani on **19 September 2027**.

This is not a contradiction to smooth away. The practitioner rule says Maha
Bharani is selected when Bharani Nakshatra prevails during the relevant
Pitru Paksha **aparahna** window. It may coincide with Chaturthi, Panchami or,
in some years, Tritiya; it is not fixed to a tithi. Current city, accepted
Panchang, nakshatra interval and aparahna/day-selection rule must be computed.

Use the location-pinned New Delhi discovery URL:

`https://www.drikpanchang.com/shraddha/nakshatra/bharani-shraddha-date-time.html?year=2027&geoname-id=1273294`

Do not copy 20 September, 19 September, any Kutup/Rohina/aparahna clock,
tarpana time, priest schedule or temple/ghat programme to another place, year
or authority. If the deterministic resolver or accepted family authority is
missing, report the formal route unresolved.

## Meaning and who commonly observes

Current practitioner evidence describes Bharani Shraddha during Pitru Paksha
and associates Bharani Nakshatra with Yama. It also records competing practice
scope: some describe it as once after a death, while Dharmasindhu-based wording
allows annual performance. Benefit language comparing it with Gaya Shraddha is
an attributed practitioner claim, not a Devam guarantee.

The consumer route therefore applies to:

- a person or family whose established elder/priest/text says Maha Bharani
  applies to their deceased family member;
- a bereaved user seeking a private remembrance while formal applicability is
  unresolved;
- a participant attending a named family, temple, priest or institution-led
  rite without assuming officiating authority.

It does not apply automatically because someone has died, because the user is
a son/daughter/spouse, because Bharani occurs, or because a calendar card says
Maha Bharani. No descendant, widow, widower, child or mourner owes a rite,
ancestor rescue, debt repayment, forgiveness, contact, food restriction,
donation or priest payment.

## Source-labelled stories

### 1. The observance's calendrical identity

Maha Bharani is a Pitru Paksha selection based on Bharani Nakshatra occupying
the relevant aparahna window. It can fall alongside different tithis. The
identity story is therefore about nakshatra, location and family authority,
not one national civil date or universal ancestor ritual.

### 2. Nachiketa and Yama in the Katha Upanishad

The fixed lawful narrative is Friedrich Max Muller's 1884 public-domain
translation of the Katha Upanishad in *Sacred Books of the East*, volume 15,
checked against the Wikisource transcription.

Vajasravasa gives possessions in a sacrifice. His young son Nachiketa notices
that the cows being given are exhausted and questions his father repeatedly
about to whom he will be given. In anger the father says he gives him to Death.
Nachiketa goes to Yama's abode and waits three nights while Yama is absent. On
return, Yama offers three boons in recompense for the waiting guest.

Nachiketa first asks that his father be calm, recognise him and welcome him
without anger. Yama grants reconciliation. For the second boon Nachiketa asks
about the fire associated in the text with heaven; Yama teaches it and names
it for him. For the third boon Nachiketa asks what happens after a person dies,
noting that some say the person continues and others do not. Yama initially
urges him to choose another boon and offers long life, descendants, wealth,
power, music and pleasures. Nachiketa says these are temporary and keeps the
question. Yama then distinguishes the good from the merely pleasant and gives
the Upanishad's teaching about Self, knowledge, discipline and death. The text
closes by saying Nachiketa received this knowledge and attained its goal.

This is a source dialogue involving an angry parent, a child being verbally
given to Death, three nights without hospitality, reconciliation, sacrificial
fire and teachings about death. It is not Maha Bharani's origin or formal
Shraddha vidhi. No child should leave home, approach danger, fast, wait without
food/water, obey an abusive command, reconcile with an unsafe parent, perform
fire rites or seek contact with Yama/dead persons. The story does not prove an
afterlife fact, deliver a message from a deceased person, erase abuse, or make
grief failure a spiritual defect.

## Typical practice and evidence boundary

Practitioner material names formal Shraddha, tarpan, Kutup/Rohina/aparahna
timing and claims of special benefit. Historical regional material also
records Bharani oblations in some communities. These sources prove that living
formal lanes exist; they do not authorise one national procedure or determine
the user's karta/performer eligibility.

The generic complete answer must offer three routes:

1. **private minimum:** resolve the date if possible; identify whether a named
   authority says the day applies; keep ordinary care; hear the two labelled
   stories; remember the deceased through a name, value, safe memory or silence;
   choose one bounded living-care act or none; close without contact/result
   claims;
2. **family remembrance:** obtain opt-in, protect grief/privacy, tell the
   stories, allow different/no memories, use an existing family prayer only if
   familiar, share normal food if wanted, and copy no formal Shraddha action;
3. **formal participant:** identify the current elder/priest/institution,
   verify applicability, timing, performer and materials, remain participant-
   only, retain health/consent/financial boundaries, and copy nothing home.

## Materials and substitutions

No item is required for the private or family remembrance. Optional materials
are a labelled story summary, written name, photograph used with consent,
flower, ordinary drinking water, safe familiar food or supervised lamp.
Every item can be replaced by a spoken name, private memory, electric light,
silence or nothing. Pinda, til, kusa, fire, ritual vessels, priestly dress,
offerings, dakshina and tarpan materials belong only to the named formal
authority; Devam does not generate their list here.

## Major variants only

Minor language, spelling, dress, food preference, photograph/no-photograph,
prayer/silence and family vocabulary remain unified.

Separate routes are required for:

- once-after-death versus annual practice when a named authority makes that
  distinction;
- different death-tithi/ancestor applicability and karta/performer rules;
- named sampradaya, priest, temple, Gaya/ghat or family procedure;
- ordinary Panchami/Chaturthi Shraddha, Magha Shraddha and Sarva Pitru Amavasya;
- Kodungallur Bharani, which is a different Kerala temple-festival identity;
- grief, abuse, estrangement, minor/dependent, food/medicine, water/fire/crowd,
  accessibility, privacy or financial safety needs.

No state variant is created only for translation. A future regional split
needs evidence of changed timing, eligibility, ordered action, materials,
safety or closing.

## Safety and non-coercion

- No fast, dehydration, meal delay, medicine change, ritual bath, water entry,
  fire, smoke, crowd, travel, animal feeding, food disposal or donation is
  required.
- No child or dependent leads or attends without safe guardian support; the
  Nachiketa story never authorises child sacrifice, abandonment, starvation or
  obedience to danger.
- No one owes reconciliation with an abusive/unsafe person, disclosure of
  family history, forgiveness, belief, touch, photograph use or public grief.
- Do not interpret dreams, birds, food, flame, coincidences or feelings as
  ancestor messages. Do not promise ancestor liberation, Gaya-equivalent merit,
  curse removal, forgiveness, prosperity or reunion.
- Do not shame a widow, widower, daughter, son, childless person, unmarried
  person, adopted person, estranged person or non-participant.
- Immediate danger uses 112. Crisis, grief, medical, fire, water and
  institutional rules override ritual completion.

Reject a portable date or aparahna time; automatic eligibility; universal
male/son karta rule; invented pinda/tarpan/fire procedure; mandatory fast,
bath, donation or priest; ancestor-contact claim; Gaya-equivalent guarantee;
child danger or forced reconciliation; Kodungallur confusion; cosmetic state
variants; source-ID gaps; mojibake; or authored/projected/hosted/released
overclaim.
