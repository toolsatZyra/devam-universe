## Meaning and two source-labelled stories

### Story 1: a solar transition, not one national origin myth

The reviewed calendar names the day from the Sun's sidereal transition from
Simha to Kanya. This supplies a calendrical story of change, timekeeping and a
new solar-month boundary. It does not establish a birth story, one national
deity rite or a universal promise.

A bounded household meaning is to notice a transition without pretending to
control its outcome: acknowledge light and time, protect ordinary care, and
choose one modest act of gratitude or service if desired. This is a labelled
Devam synthesis, not a scriptural command.

### Story 2: Agastya, Rama and the Aditya Hridaya

Reviewed *Valmiki Ramayana*, Yuddha Kanda 6.107 in the IIT Kanpur textual
portal, including Sanskrit, word order, translation and sarga summary:

<https://www.valmiki.iitk.ac.in/sloka?field_kanda_tid=6&field_sarga_value=107&language=dv>

The complete scene is:

1. Rama stands exhausted by battle and absorbed in thought while Ravana is
   ready to fight again. Gods have gathered to witness the encounter.
2. Sage Agastya approaches Rama and announces an ancient secret by which the
   source says he will overcome his enemies.
3. Agastya teaches the Aditya Hridaya, praising Aditya/Surya. The hymn
   attributes auspiciousness, victory, removal of enemies, sorrow and sin, and
   increased life to its recitation.
4. The hymn identifies the Sun with or as containing many deities and cosmic
   functions: Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Skanda, Prajapati, Indra, Kubera, Kala,
   Yama, Soma, Varuna, the ancestors, Vasus, Ashvins, Maruts, Manu, Vayu, Agni
   and the life of beings.
5. It describes the Sun through names and functions connected with rays,
   heat, light, darkness, seasons, time, rain, cold, creation, destruction,
   sacrifice, Veda and witness.
6. Agastya tells Rama to worship the Sun and repeat the hymn three times in
   great difficulty, predicting victory in that battle. He then departs.
7. Rama hears the teaching; the source says his sorrow leaves. He purifies
   himself, looks toward the Sun, recites three times and gains joy and resolve.
8. Rama takes up his bow and advances to the next battle. The source depicts
   Surya among the gods, delighted and urging that Ravana's defeat be hastened.

This is a battle-context Surya story, not Kanya Sankranti's universal origin.
It does not authorise a generic app to prescribe the Sanskrit hymn, count,
purification, sun-gazing, enemy-destruction or victory rite.

## Sensitive source boundaries

- **Battle and enemy language:** Rama and Ravana are combatants in an epic
  war. Never ask a user to identify a real person as Ravana, an enemy, demon,
  obstacle or target. Do not turn “enemy destruction” into violence, revenge,
  harassment, coercion, surveillance or dehumanisation.
- **Victory and outcome claims:** the chapter attributes victory, health,
  longevity, sin removal and relief from sorrow to the hymn. Do not guarantee
  legal, medical, financial, academic, relationship, grief or conflict results.
- **Fatigue and distress:** Rama's exhaustion is a source event. Exhaustion,
  heat illness, depression, anxiety or trauma needs rest and appropriate care,
  not forced prayer, exposure or perseverance.
- **Formal recitation:** the source gives a named hymn and repeated count in a
  battlefield instruction. This lane does not reproduce or prescribe the
  mantra. A familiar prayer, plain-language remembrance or silence is complete.
- **Looking toward the Sun:** Rama's action is a source event, not permission
  to stare at the Sun. Never look directly at the Sun or use lenses, binoculars
  or improvised filters.
- **Purification:** the source's purification act is not permission to withhold
  water, bathe in unsafe water, force bathing, rank another person as impure or
  improvise a restricted rite.
- **Divine identity claims:** the hymn's identifications are source theology,
  not a scientific astronomy statement or a test of belief.
- **War ending:** the scene leads back into combat. Do not reenact weapons,
  killing, threat, triumph over a person or military action.

shared Indian Panchang names the locally resolved Simha-to-Kanya ingress Kanya

## Typical practice evidence and limits

The reviewed practitioner-calendar and prior bounded Devam pack associate
general Sankranti observance with Surya remembrance, bathing, arghya and
charity. These are discovery and synthesis evidence, not proof that each action
is universal or safe for every household.

The generic complete route therefore requires none of them. It provides
material-free Surya remembrance, a source-labelled story, a safety boundary
and an optional no-purchase care/service action. Bathing, river entry, rooftop
or outdoor sun exposure, water pouring, flame, fasting, formal mantra,
sankalpa, priest-led worship, donation amount and temple close require their
own safe authority.

## Complete household routes

### Minimum private form

1. Confirm city, civil date, local Simha-to-Kanya ingress and accepted Kanya
   Panchang. If unresolved, do not declare today Kanya Sankranti.
2. Keep normal food, water, medicine, insulin, hygiene, child care and sleep.
   Begin no fast, outdoor exposure, bath, flame, arghya or mantra.
3. Sit safely indoors or in shade. Remember Surya with a familiar name,
   plain-language thanks or silence. Do not look directly at the Sun.
4. Hear both labelled stories: the solar transition, and the full
   Agastya–Rama Aditya Hridaya battle scene with its attributed claims.
5. State the boundary: no real person is an enemy or demon; no victory, health,
   longevity or sorrow-removal result is guaranteed; no formal recitation is
   required.
6. Optionally notice one transition in ordinary life and choose one small act
   of care, gratitude or service already safe and affordable. No purchase,
   donation, disclosure, contact or action is required.
7. Close with normal food, water, medicine, shade and rest. Do not read later
   events as Sankranti-phala, omen, blessing, punishment or ritual score.

### Consent-led family form

Use the same fed, hydrated, shaded and material-free core. Give an easy no and
private exit. Do not require anyone to face or gaze at the Sun, recite Sanskrit,
disclose a problem, name an enemy, role-play battle, promise change, donate,
touch another person, bathe or handle flame/water. Children retain normal food,
water, medicine, school, play and sleep.

Each willing participant may name one safe source of ordinary support or one
transition they want to meet gently. Do not evaluate the answer or predict an
outcome. Close without hierarchy, forced gratitude or a shared action.

### Named temple or formal-family participation

Verify the current official local date, punya-kala, visitor eligibility,
fasting and food rules, mantra or initiation limits, bathing/arghya practice,
heat and sun exposure, flame, accessibility, child safeguards, crowd control,
photography, donation, emergency plan and exit. The named authority owns its
formal sankalpa, mantra, offerings and close.

Keep medical care and ordinary food/water needs. Never stare at the Sun, enter
unsafe water, climb to an unprotected roof, obstruct a public path or copy a
temple rite home. A remote, home, silent or cancelled form is complete.

## Materials and substitutions

No material is required. Optional existing supports are:

- a local calendar reminder, with a spoken reminder or no reminder as
  substitutes;
- a familiar Surya image or text, with a plain-language name or silence as
  substitutes; and
- an existing seat in a safe indoor or shaded place, with standing, resting or
  no dedicated posture as substitutes.

Do not require direct sunlight, rooftop access, telescope, lens, filter,
water vessel, river, bath, flower, lamp, incense, fire, red cloth, grain,
sesame, sweet, coin, donation, priest, mantra book or paid item. A no-image,
no-writing, no-purchase and material-free form is complete.

## Safety, privacy and dignity

- Never look directly at the Sun. Do not use binoculars, telescopes, cameras,
  magnifiers, smoked glass, film, water reflections or improvised filters.
- Avoid heat and fall risk. Do not climb roofs, ledges, tanks, terraces or
  unstable furniture; children need ordinary supervision.
- Do not enter a river, pond, sea, ghat or crowded water area from this guide.
  Do not force bathing, cold exposure, head immersion or water pouring.
- Keep normal food, water, medicine, insulin, hygiene and sleep. No fast,
  dehydration or health claim is required.
- Use no flame or incense in the complete form. Formal fire practice needs
  responsible authority, ventilation and local fire safety.
- No real person or group is Ravana, an enemy, demon, darkness or obstacle.
  Do not threaten, target, shame, monitor or “destroy” anyone.
- Prayer does not guarantee victory, cure, longevity, relief from sorrow,
  promotion, exam success, court outcome, money, relationship change or safety.
- Nobody owes mantra, belief, gratitude, disclosure, touch, eye contact,
  donation, service, forgiveness, reconciliation or contact.
- Verify any recipient before giving. Set a private affordable limit; no loan,
  debt, transfer, cash handling or proof of generosity is required.
- Do not infer personality, fate, health, danger, social worth or future events
  from Sankranti-phala, omens, birth status or what happens later.
- Caste, birth, gender, marital status, disability, age, work, wealth or
  ritual fluency does not rank access, dignity, food, water or safety.
- Immediate danger in India calls for qualified emergency help such as 112;
  do not wait for ritual guidance.
