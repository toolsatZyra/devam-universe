# Pitru Paksha tithi batch: 13-lane evidence matrix (2027)

Checked 2026-08-13. This is the evidence-first research freeze for thirteen
accepted, still-incomplete ritual-calendar items: `purnima-shraddha`,
`pratipada-shraddha`, `dwitiya-shraddha`, `tritiya-shraddha`,
`chaturthi-shraddha`, `panchami-shraddha`, `shashthi-shraddha`,
`saptami-shraddha`, `ashtami-shraddha`, `navami-shraddha`,
`dashami-shraddha`, `ekadashi-shraddha` and `chaturdashi-shraddha`.

This batch deliberately reuses one evidence spine. It does not count any lane
as authored, projected, hosted, reviewed or released. Dwadashi, Trayodashi,
Magha and Sarva Pitru are already separate completed lanes and are not silently
merged into this batch.

## Shared origin, meaning and story boundary

The source-supported common identity is not thirteen separately invented
origin legends. These are tithi-labelled occasions within the living Pitru
Paksha/Mahalaya remembrance period. Current practitioner material assigns most
days by the lunar tithi on which a deceased family member died, whether that
death tithi occurred in Shukla or Krishna Paksha. The Ministry of Tourism's
Pitra Paksha page is useful current evidence that the period remains a living
ancestor-remembrance observance; it is not a manual for one national vidhi.

The retained 1865 *Nirnayasindhu* Mahalaya section is fixed historical-text
evidence that formal Shraddha rules have a textual history. Its carrier hash is
`a632f570153cb77802b85fdc22e54e00f217960b1d848ee640dd9e610327f02b`.
It is not converted here into a universal modern household procedure.

The fixed lawful narrative expression for all thirteen lanes is Max Muller's
1884 public-domain Katha Upanishad translation. Vajasravasa gives possessions;
his son Nachiketa questions the exhausted cows, is angrily given “to Death,”
waits three nights at Yama's abode, receives three boons and asks what follows
death. Yama offers wealth, longevity and pleasures instead; Nachiketa refuses
what is temporary, and Yama distinguishes the good from the merely pleasant.
This is reflection on death and discernment—not the origin of Pitru Paksha or
any tithi Shraddha, proof of an afterlife, ancestor contact, or formal vidhi.
No child copies Nachiketa's danger, deprivation, unsafe obedience, fire or
reconciliation with an unsafe adult.

These dates are fixtures, not portable answers. Every formal lane requires the
user's city, year, accepted Pitru Paksha calendar, local tithi interval, local
aparaahna and the accepted authority's selection rule. Candidate civil days
must be compared by the tithi's overlap with aparaahna. Kutup, Rohina, venue,
priest and temple times are current local facts. A tie, kshaya/vriddhi case,
same-date overlap or family exception is unresolved until a named authority
decides it.

Purnima is materially different from the twelve Krishna-paksha rows: the
reviewed current source calls it Proshthapadi/Bhadrapada Purnima Shraddha but
also says that a person whose death tithi was Purnima is remembered on
Amavasya Shraddha rather than automatically on Bhadrapada Purnima. The product
must expose that distinction and route uncertainty to Sarva Pitru; it must not
pretend the labels are interchangeable.

## Shared complete consumer-practice skeleton

### Private minimum

1. Ask which named tithi observance the person means; record city and accepted
   Panchang only if they want a formal-date answer.
2. State whether the lane is the ordinary death-tithi route or one of the four
   source-attributed special-applicability claims.
3. Keep meals, water, medicine, sleep, care work and safety unchanged.
4. Hear the tithi identity and bounded Nachiketa story, clearly source-labelled.
5. Remember through a name, one safe memory or value, a private note, familiar
   prayer or silence. Any option can be declined.
6. Optionally do one affordable act benefiting a living person, without calling
   it a required offering, debt payment or guaranteed merit.
7. Close by putting materials away, eating/drinking normally and making no
   ancestor-contact, peace, liberation, prosperity or forgiveness claim.

### Opt-in family remembrance

1. Invite participation with an easy no and no forced photograph, touch,
   disclosure, gotra, positive memory, forgiveness or unsafe-relative contact.
2. Confirm the lane and any special applicability only through volunteered
   family knowledge plus a named accepted authority.
3. Agree a short duration, privacy/no-recording boundary and stop signal.
4. Tell the two labelled stories; allow different, difficult or absent memories.
5. Use only familiar safe words/actions the family already accepts.
6. Close together or separately; retain ordinary food, medicine, water and rest.

### Named formal participant

1. Identify the present family elder, priest, text, sampradaya, temple, ghat or
   institution that owns the rite.
2. Verify ancestor applicability, performer/participant role, city, tithi-
   aparaahna selection, current venue schedule, sequence and closing.
3. Obtain the owner's exact material list, quantities, food/allergen handling,
   fire/water rules, recipients, donation policy and disposal plan.
4. Bring only requested safe affordable items and remain participant-only.
5. Follow medical, legal, safeguarding, venue, water, fire and crowd rules over
   ritual completion. Copy no formal sequence home from this generic research.

## Materials, substitutions and safety

Nothing is required for private or family remembrance. Optional supports are a
source-labelled story, written name, consented photograph, flower, ordinary
drinking water, familiar safe food, note or supervised electric light. Each can
be replaced by a spoken name, safe memory, silence, ordinary care or nothing.

Pinda, sesame, kusa, ritual water, vessels, sacred fire, lamps, recitations,
formal meals, animal feeding, clothes, utensils, offerings and dakshina are
owner-scoped. This batch supplies no quantity, mantra, preparation, recipient,
sequence or disposal instruction.

No fast, dehydration, meal delay, medicine change, ritual bath, water entry,
fire, smoke, crowd, travel, animal feeding, food disposal, donation, purchase or
payment is required. Do not diagnose Pitru Dosha; infer ancestor messages from
dreams, birds, animals, water, flame or coincidence; promise satisfaction,
peace, liberation, reunion, merit, health, wealth, progeny or prosperity; or
assign duty by gender, kinship, marriage, fertility, adoption or lineage.

For Chaturdashi, never blame, speculate about or disclose cause of death;
romanticise suicide; provide method details; or treat a traumatic death as sin,
curse, defect or family failure. Immediate danger uses India's 112 emergency
system. Tele-MANAS is available at 14416 or 1800-89-14416 for current mental-
health support. Medical, legal and safeguarding duties override privacy or
ritual completion.

## Major-variant threshold

The selected North/West and major-state audience shares this safe router. A
separate variant is created only when evidence changes applicability, local
timing selection, performer/participant role, ordered formal action, required
materials, safety or closing. Language, transliteration, dress, ordinary food,
photograph/no-photograph, familiar prayer/silence and mere state popularity are
not variants.

No reviewed source established a materially different state-by-state generic
tithi Shraddha procedure for West Bengal, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Tamil
Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra, Odisha or Andhra Pradesh. A named family,
sampradaya, temple, ghat, Gaya or regional authority may still create a future
material route when current evidence supports it.

## Batch acceptance boundary

Reject any lane that invents a separate origin legend, portable civil date or
clock time, sunrise-only rule, generic pinda/tarpan/mantra/fire/water/feeding or
donation procedure, universal male/son performer, automatic family/death/
marriage/cause classification, fasting imported from Ekadashi, deity worship
imported from a same-tithi vrata, source benefit guarantee, cosmetic state
variant, unlabelled source contradiction, mojibake or completion/release
overclaim.
