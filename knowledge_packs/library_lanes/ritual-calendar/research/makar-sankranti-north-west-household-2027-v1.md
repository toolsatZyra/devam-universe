# Makar Sankranti North/West household lane: research dossier v1

Checked: 2026-08-13

## Applicability decision

This dossier supports one bounded lane: a North/West Indian lay household remembrance and ordinary temple or authorised-ghat participation, using Delhi for the 2027 timing fixture. It does not claim a national ritual. A known family, sampradaya, temple or local Panchang replaces the generic procedure.

The lane deliberately does **not** absorb Tamil Pongal, Assam Magh Bihu, Punjab/Haryana Maghi, Bengal Poush Sankranti, Odisha's Makar observances, Kerala Makaravilakku or priest-led shraddha. Those differ materially in festival sequence, identity, authority, timing or required action. Gujarat Uttarayan kite-flying and Bihar/Chhattisgarh sesame foods are optional regional expressions that do not, by themselves, justify duplicated ritual lanes.

## Timing fixture

DrikPanchang's Delhi page gives Makar Sankranti on 15 January 2027, with the solar ingress at 21:14 on 14 January, Punya Kala 07:15-17:46 and Maha Punya Kala 07:15-09:00 on 15 January. Its stated rule moves the auspicious period to the next daylight period when ingress occurs after sunset. These clock times are a Delhi fixture only. Production resolution must recompute by location and the user's accepted Panchang/tradition.

## Evidence-to-guidance map

- DrikPanchang: practitioner-calendar fixture and descriptive evidence for bathing, Surya worship/naivedya, charity and specialist shraddha. It is not treated as universal household authority.
- Ganguli's public-domain Mahabharata expression: Bhishma waits for the Sun's northern course. This is labelled an epic association, not the sole origin of the festival.
- President of India 2026 greeting and Akashvani: contemporary public descriptions of harvest gratitude, nature/farmers, Surya and regional plurality. These support meaning, not detailed vidhi.
- Madhya Pradesh Tourism's Sidhi fair page: a current, specifically scoped river-bath, donation and sesame/jaggery living-practice example.
- Gujarat Tourism: Uttarayan kite-flying as a Gujarat cultural expression, kept outside the ritual core.
- Bihar and Chhattisgarh Incredible India pages: tilkut and tilgur food associations, kept as material variants only.
- National Green Tribunal and KPDCL: no synthetic, nylon, glass- or metal-coated manjha; avoid power lines. Current local law still must be checked.
- Gujarat Institute of Disaster Management and NTR Police: authorised bathing zones/ghats, crowd discipline, minimal baggage, no soap/plastic/litter and no unsafe current exposure.
- DGHS/MoHFW: fasting is not a safe generic instruction for people using insulin or with diabetes; medication changes require medical advice.
- FSSAI: sesame is an allergen and cross-contact matters.

## Instruction boundary

The pack offers three user-facing forms: a short material-light remembrance, a bounded household form, and ordinary temple/ghat participation. It does not create Sanskrit mantras, repetition counts, vows, priestly rites, shraddha instructions, guaranteed outcomes or a mandatory fast. Food, water and flame offerings are optional and replaceable. Bathing at home is the explicit substitute for unsafe or unavailable river access.

## Major-difference review

Separate applicability lanes remain required when any of these changes materially: calendar identity/date rule; multi-day sequence; deity or agrarian focus; required household act; institution/lineage authority; or safety-critical setting. Mere naming, sweet type or common celebratory recreation stays as a within-lane note.
