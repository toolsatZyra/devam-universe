# Trayodashi Shraddha North/West authority-router research (2027)

## Frozen denominator

This dossier selects exactly the calendar-resolved `trayodashi-shraddha` item,
also called Teras Shraddha in current material. It is a Pitru Paksha Krishna
Trayodashi death-tithi lane, not Dwadashi, Chaturdashi, Sarva Pitru, Pradosha or
an automatic Magha Trayodashi rite.

## Existing material audit

The Delhi 2026 Pitru Paksha fixture and remembrance content provide a date
label, privacy, grief and no-result boundaries, but no complete Trayodashi
applicability, child-bereavement, Gujarat, Magha-overlap or formal-authority
route. They remain partial and are not silently counted.

## Deterministic timing dependency

Current 2027 fixtures demonstrate material location dependence:

- Ajmer places Trayodashi Shraddha on **Tuesday, 28 September 2027**, with
  Trayodashi beginning on 27 September and ending during 28 September;
- Madison, Alabama places it on **Monday, 27 September 2027** under its local
  interval and aparahna.

The formal resolver must compute the local Pitru Paksha Krishna Trayodashi
interval and each candidate civil day's aparahna, then apply the accepted
Shraddha authority's greater-overlap and tie/exception rules. Sunrise alone is
not enough. Never copy either date, interval, Kutup/Rohina/aparahna window,
ancestor, performer, material list or venue schedule to another location,
year or authority.

Magha Nakshatra overlap is a separate decision. The current source claims that
Magha with Trayodashi creates an exceptionally beneficial occasion and that
even water-only Shraddha/tarpan satisfies ancestors for thousands of years.
This is attributed claim evidence, not a generic water instruction, timing
rule, guarantee or reason to merge the completed Magha lane.

## Meaning, applicability and dangerous claims

Current practitioner material states that Trayodashi Shraddha applies to an
ancestor whose death tithi was Trayodashi in either fortnight. It separately
claims suitability for deceased children and records the Gujarat names Kakbali
and Balabholani Teras. These are material applicability and regional-practice
signals, not permission to classify a child, infer a death tithi or generate a
child-specific rite.

Child loss requires a distinct named family/priest/text authority plus strong
bereavement, guardian, privacy and non-coercion boundaries. No parent, sibling,
guardian or child owes public disclosure, blame, ritual leadership, water
offering, animal feeding, donation, reconciliation or a promised outcome.

The current page also names formal Shraddha, tarpan, pinda and timing, and makes
large satisfaction, merit, peace and prosperity claims. All remain attributed
and owner-specific. Devam does not promise that a deceased child or ancestor is
satisfied, rescued, peaceful or communicating.

## Who may use the eventual lane

- a person seeking private remembrance while death-tithi or authority remains
  unresolved;
- an opt-in family whose named authority says ordinary Trayodashi applies;
- a bereaved family whose named authority has explicitly resolved a child-loss
  applicability route and safeguarding;
- a participant in a named Gujarat Kakbali/Balabholani Teras, Magha-overlap,
  family, priest, temple or institution route after current instructions are
  verified.

No gender, kinship, parental, guardian or discipleship status automatically
creates performer duty.

## Source-labelled stories

### 1. Trayodashi's tithi identity

Trayodashi Shraddha is identified by local Pitru Paksha Krishna Trayodashi in
the relevant aparahna and an authority-confirmed death-tithi or special
applicability context. Gujarat names and child-death claims show living variants
but do not establish one national origin or procedure. Magha conjunction is a
separate overlap, not an automatic feature of every Trayodashi.

### 2. Nachiketa and Yama in the Katha Upanishad

The fixed lawful narrative is Friedrich Max Muller's 1884 public-domain Katha
Upanishad. Vajasravasa gives worn-out cows in sacrifice. His young son
Nachiketa repeatedly asks to whom he will be given; in anger his father says
“to Death.” Nachiketa reaches Yama's abode and waits three nights. Yama returns
and grants three boons: reconciliation with his father, a fire teaching, and a
response to the disputed question of what follows death. Yama first offers long
life, descendants, wealth, power, music and pleasures, but Nachiketa refuses
what is temporary. Yama distinguishes the good from the merely pleasant and
teaches about Self, knowledge, discipline and death.

This story is not Trayodashi's origin, child-loss explanation or Shraddha
vidhi. No child leaves home, approaches danger, fasts, waits without care,
obeys abuse, reconciles with an unsafe parent, handles fire or seeks contact
with Yama or a deceased person. It proves no afterlife fact and must never be
used to blame, console with certainty, or prescribe behaviour after child loss.

## Complete generic route boundary

The eventual pack must provide:

1. a material-free private route with local-date resolution, normal care, two
   labelled stories, name/safe memory/silence, optional living care and a
   no-contact/no-result close;
2. an opt-in family route protecting grief, child-loss privacy, different or
   absent memories, food/medicine/sleep and easy refusal;
3. a participant-only formal route that verifies death-tithi or child-loss
   applicability, performer, local Trayodashi-aparahna, Magha overlap, named
   Gujarat/family/institution authority, materials, venue and closing.

No generic procedure may create pinda, sesame, kusa, tarpan, ritual water,
animal feeding, lamps, fire, food, donation, recitation or disposal steps.

## Materials and substitutions

Nothing is required for private or family remembrance. Optional story, name,
consented photograph, flower, ordinary drinking water, familiar safe food or
electric light can each be replaced by a spoken name, safe memory, silence,
ordinary care or nothing. Formal materials belong only to the named owner.

## Major variants only

Minor language, spelling, dress, food preference, image/no-image and prayer/
silence remain unified. Material forks are:

- ordinary Trayodashi death-tithi applicability;
- child-death applicability and bereavement safeguarding;
- Gujarat Kakbali/Balabholani Teras ordered practice and meaning;
- Magha Trayodashi overlap;
- named performer/family/priest/sampradaya/institution procedure;
- named Gaya/ghat/river route;
- safety, grief, abuse, child/dependent, privacy, accessibility and cost needs.

A state split needs changed timing, applicability, action, materials, safety or
closing—not vocabulary or popularity alone.

## Safety and non-coercion

- No fast, dehydration, medicine change, bath, water entry, fire, smoke,
  animal feeding, crowd, travel, food disposal, donation or payment is required.
- Child death is never blamed on a person, omission, karma diagnosis, defect,
  curse or inadequate ritual. No parent or sibling owes positive memory,
  disclosure, forgiveness, belief or public grief.
- No living child copies Nachiketa's danger, waiting, abusive command or fire.
- Do not interpret dreams, birds, animals, water, flame or feelings as messages.
- Do not promise satisfaction for thousands of years, liberation, peace,
  prosperity, reunion or grief relief.
- Immediate danger uses 112; medical, safeguarding, venue and legal rules
  override ritual completion.
