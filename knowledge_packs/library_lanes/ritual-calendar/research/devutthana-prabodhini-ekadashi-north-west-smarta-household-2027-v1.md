The chapter has no developed human plot. Its complete sequence is a layered
conversation and praise/practice catalogue:

1. Yudhishthira asks Krishna about Kartika Shukla Ekadashi after hearing Rama.
   Krishna says he will repeat what Brahma told Narada.
2. Narada asks about Prabodhini, on which Vishnu “keeps awake.” Brahma calls it
   sin-destroying, merit-increasing and salvation-giving.
3. The chapter says sacred waters roar until the awakening day arrives and
   compares one fast with thousands of horse sacrifices and hundreds of
   Rajasuya sacrifices.
4. It promises otherwise unattainable things, splendour, wealth, intellect,
   kingdom, happiness and riches; says one fast burns mountain-sized declared
   sins; and says a vigil burns sins of thousands of former lives.
5. It contrasts correctly and incorrectly performed good acts, making a
   Meru-sized act yield only atom-sized fruit if not performed “properly.”
6. It says observers' dead ancestors rejoice in Vishnu's world and that even a
   person who committed a terrible sin such as Brahmana murder escapes hell
   and reaches Vishnu's highest state through the vow.
7. It praises vigil above sacrifices, pilgrimage and gifts of gold/land,
   calls only the observer fortunate and family-purifying, invokes the
   uncertainty of body/wealth and certainty of death, and directs the fast.
8. It promises sons, grandsons, pleasures, salvation and no rebirth; labels
   the faster wise, yogi, ascetic and self-controlled; and directs giving up
   other meritorious acts for the vow.
9. It says Vishnu washes deed, thought and speech sins through vigil and that
   bath, gift, recitation and worship become inexhaustible. It promises
   Vishnu's abode, brilliance, wealth, grain and all difficult-to-obtain things.
10. It says all merit earned since birth becomes fruitless without the Kartika
    vow and directs avoiding other people's food.
11. It praises sacred-text study, Vishnu-story telling/hearing, even fractions
    of a verse, with sacrifice, cow, family-emancipation, land and eternal-world
    equivalents. It directs honouring the story-knower according to capacity.
12. It praises singing, dancing and instruments. It prescribes many flowers,
    fruits, camphor, agaru and saffron; conch water and fruit arghya during the
    vigil; and a meal, covering and dakshina for the preceptor.
13. It equates hearing Bhagavata/Purana letters with cow gifts and promises
    salvation. It lists ketaki, agasti and mango leaves/blossoms and Tulasi
    leaves/flowers for Vishnu worship with vast promised fruit.
14. It praises seeing, touching, meditating on, describing, praising,
    planting, sprinkling and worshipping Tulasi and attributes long-lasting
    family residence in Vishnu's house to a growing plant.

This entire catalogue is the source-labelled narrative expression. Devam must
