1. Savitri names Satyavan as her own selection after travelling to choose.
2. Narada predicts Satyavan will die within a year; her father tells her to

## Bounded action design

Minimum complete form: resolve the local Amavasya route; hear four source-
labelled story moments; choose a family-known prayer or silence; identify one
mutual-care action that does not erase either person's autonomy; close with
thanks. No spouse, tree, thread, fast, Sanskrit, purchase or image is required.

Standard family form may use an owned image/book, flower, water, fruit/food and
an optional loose symbolic thread. At a live banyan site, obtain permission and
follow caretaker rules. Never nail, cut, scrape, paint, light fire at the roots,
pour oil/milk/sugar, leave plastic, constrict the trunk or aerial roots, block a
path, or demand access. A loose removable gesture, respectful circumambulation,
tree-viewing from a safe path, a household plant, an image or no tree is a
complete substitution. Mobility aids, clockwise movement and any number of
circuits are optional; dizziness/pain or site rules end movement.

## Health, consent and claim ceiling

- The source's three-night fast is narrative fact, not consumer instruction.
  Non-fasting is complete. Never imitate fasting while undertaking walking,
  heat exposure or care work; required food, water and medicine come first.
- No guarantee of a spouse's longevity, health, resurrection, fertility,
  marriage stability, widowhood prevention, merit or protection.
- A spouse is not owned or owed a ritual. No surveillance, coercion, touching,
  sex, reproduction, service, fasting or public declaration is implied by
  marriage. Care is mutual and consent remains revocable.
- Acute headache, chest/heart symptoms, weakness, collapse or breathing change
  in a real person calls for emergency/medical action, not reenactment of
  Satyavan's scene or reliance on prayer.
- Food uses FSSAI hygiene and allergen safeguards. Public trees are living
  organisms and shared spaces; Delhi tree-preservation guidance recognizes
  damaging a tree as regulated harm, and landscape standards require ties to
  avoid abrasion and permit growth.
