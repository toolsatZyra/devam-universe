## Fixed Padma Purana story: Padma Ekadashi, 6.57

Reviewed N. A. Deshpande's English expression of *Padma Purana*, Uttara
Khanda, chapter 57:
<https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-padma-purana/d/doc365634.html>

The source calls the day **Padma Ekadashi**, not Parsva in the chapter title.
Its complete developed sequence is:

1. Yudhishthira asks Keshava the name, deity and manner of Bhadrapada Shukla
   Ekadashi.
2. Krishna says he will recount what Brahma told Narada; Narada asks in order
   to propitiate Vishnu.
3. Brahma names the day Padma, says Vishnu is worshipped and introduces an old
   account.
4. Mandhatri is described as a truthful, righteous emperor whose subjects had
   formerly been healthy, prosperous and free from famine.
5. Rain then fails for three years. Hunger and the interruption of offerings
   and Vedic study trouble the kingdom.
6. The subjects ask the king to secure their welfare, explaining water, rain
   and food through a Narayana theology.
7. Mandhatri says he cannot identify wrongdoing of his own but will strive for
   the subjects' well-being. He goes to forest hermitages and meets Angiras.
8. Angiras claims that in the Krita age only Brahmanas may practise penance,
   blames a Chandala ascetic for the drought and tells Mandhatri to kill him.
9. Mandhatri refuses to kill the innocent person and asks for another
   righteous remedy.
10. Angiras directs Padma Ekadashi observance by the king, subjects and
    attendants and promises rain.
11. The source says the king, subjects and four castes observe together; rain
    falls, land fills with water and grain grows.
12. The chapter prescribes a covered water pitcher with curd and rice,
    umbrella and sandals as Brahmana gifts and gives a Govinda prayer; it
    promises pleasures, salvation and sin removal from hearing.

This is a source-labelled drought and anti-killing story with serious caste
hierarchy and miracle-weather claims. It is not hydrology, meteorology, public
policy, caste law, a donation instruction or a safe modern household vidhi.

## Narada Purana source expression: turning the image

Reviewed *Narada Purana*, Purva-bhaga, chapter 120, especially verses 36b-41a
and the translator's note:
<https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/narada-purana-english/d/doc1857985.html>

This source names Bhadrapada Shukla Ekadashi **Padma vrata** and supplies a
formal sequence absent from Padma Purana 6.57:

- after daily worship, perform a crore-gift rite;
- take a previously installed image to a water reservoir and touch it with
  water;
- return it to a platform and lay it on its left side;
- worship Vamana on Dwadashi;
- feed and give dakshina to Brahmanas; and
- receive attributed worldly pleasure and release.

The translator's note identifies Padma with Parivartini because Vishnu is
supposed to turn during sleep and cites the left-side image instruction. This
is evidence for the **turning meaning**, but it is a formal image/water/
institutional rite, not permission to move a household murti, enter a reservoir
or improvise Vamana worship.

The same chapter's general Ekadashi rules contain fasting, vigil, image,
mantra, homa, caste, purity, food, touch and outcaste restrictions. They are
reported as source context and are not imported into the ordinary route.

## Popular Vamana-Bali and Jal Jhilani layers

Current Swaminarayan/BAPS sources describe **Parivartini-Jal Jhilani
Ekadashi** as Vishnu turning while with Bali in Sutala, connect it with a
Krishna-Gopi boat/yoghurt account, prescribe a waterless fast, and take a
small murti by boat or to water. BAPS teaching material separately places
Vamana Jayanti on Bhadarva Sud 12.

This is a materially different sampradaya and institutional lane: waterless
fasting, murti handling, boat/water access, repeated arti, offerings and named
authority change health, safety, timing, materials, story and closing. It must
not be presented as an Odisha, North, West or all-India household norm.

Popular Vaishnava retellings also connect Parivartini with Vamana and Bali and
attribute them to Brahmavaivarta Purana. No fixed lawful full expression of
that claimed passage was accepted in this research batch. Do not silently
merge it into the Padma Mandhatri story. A future source/story owner may resolve

## Sensitive source facts that must not become Devam's voice

- **Caste scapegoating and threatened killing:** Angiras blames drought on a
  Chandala ascetic and recommends killing him. This is attributed source
  speech, not truth, science or instruction. Mandhatri's refusal is explicit.
  Never blame caste, tribe, birth, occupation, religion, migration, poverty or
  another person for weather or crisis.
- **Caste-restricted penance and collective caste roles:** the source says
  only Brahmanas may practise penance in that age and later lists four castes.
  Devam rejects birth-based limits on dignity, prayer, reflection, safety,
  education, water, food or participation.
- **Drought as moral causation:** drought, flood, crop loss, hunger and disease
  are not proof of sin, ritual failure or an unauthorised person's practice.
  Use current meteorological, public-health and civil authorities.
- **Rain guarantee:** the chapter attributes rainfall and grain recovery to
  the vow. Do not promise rain, water security, harvest, food, health,
  prosperity, calamity removal or salvation.
- **Violence and accountability:** the safe centre is the king's refusal to
  kill an innocent person, not a claim that later ritual resolves structural
  crisis. Ritual does not erase violence, discrimination, negligence,
  corruption or public duties.
- **Collective observance:** subjects and four castes observe together in the
  text. No citizen, employee, dependent, family member or community owes a
  fast, vigil, prayer, donation, caste role or participation to produce rain.
- **Gift hierarchy:** pitcher, water, curd-rice, umbrella, sandals and Brahmana
  recipient are formal source gifts, not ordinary materials. Do not prepare,
  transport, donate or solicit them from this guide.
- **Water and image handling:** taking an image to a reservoir, touching water
  and laying it on one side require owner/institution authority, safe access,
  water quality, crowd control and environmental compliance. Do not imitate.
- **Waterless fasting and vigil:** these are not general health advice. The
  complete ordinary route remains fed, hydrated and rested.
- **Gendered grammar and authority:** the fixed sources address a male devotee
  and king. Do not make gender, marital status, caste or priestly access an
  eligibility condition for the safe ordinary lane.

## Consumer meaning

A safe ordinary reflection is: **in a shared crisis, refuse to scapegoat or
harm an innocent person; protect truthful help, equal dignity and fair access
to ordinary water and food**.

“Turning” may be used only as a gentle personal metaphor: turn away from one
harmful blame or neglect pattern toward one safe responsibility already yours.
It is not a claim that moving an image changes weather or fortune.

An optional no-purchase action may be: close a running tap already under your
control, keep normal drinking water accessible in your household with consent,
report an observed public leak through the existing local authority, or verify
an official weather/water advisory. Do not repair plumbing, enter water,
handle public infrastructure, confront another person or ration anyone's
drinking water from this guide. No action is required.
