## Deterministic answer router

1. Resolve the user's coordinates, timezone, horizon, moonrise/moonset or
   sunrise/sunset, and astronomical contacts from a deterministic eclipse
   engine/current ephemeris.
2. Report the locally visible phase, not merely the global eclipse label.
3. Ask which family, sampradaya, temple or Panchang rule owns ritual treatment.
4. If the event is locally invisible, answer that no visible-event route is
   generated; learning, remembrance or ordinary prayer remains optional.
5. If a penumbral lunar event is not recognized by the selected calendar, keep
   normal care and do not invent Sutak.
6. If a solar phase is visible, direct observation is optional. A user can pray
   indoors and never look at the Sun. If observing, apply NASA safety exactly.
7. Named temple schedules, closures, mantra counts, fasting, bath, donation and
   formal `grahana shanti` remain current family/institution/sampradaya-owner
   routes.

## Consumer practice boundary

The safe common household answer is deliberately small: confirm local
visibility and the family's rule; keep medicines, hydration, child care and
medical advice; optionally clean a stable place, pray/read/listen or sit in
silence during the locally resolved interval; close with thanks and ordinary
safe food/cleanup. No object is required. A lamp, incense, fast, bath, discarded
food, temple visit, donation, mantra count or restriction on a pregnant person
is never inferred. The story and the physical mechanism are presented in
separate labelled paragraphs.
