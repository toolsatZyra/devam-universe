## Adversarial review

- Guha's suspicion about Bharata is a mistaken inference from seeing an army,
  not established motive or guilt.
- Battle preparation, graphic threats, and willingness to die for Rama remain
  dangerous period rhetoric, not self-harm or violence guidance.
- The Nishadas retain local knowledge, names, deliberation, and the elder's
  successful intervention; they are not anonymous labour for a royal party.
- Caste stigma, untouchability language, and old labels for communities are
  exposed as period hierarchy and never endorsed as modern social truth.
- Bharata's grief and self-blame are not converted into actual responsibility;
  Guha and Bharadwaja explicitly challenge his self-condemnation.
- Ganga, Triveni, Yamuna, prayers, omens, celestial voices, flower showers,
  miraculous accommodation, and softened roads remain devotional claims, not
  verified history, science, guaranteed outcome, or universal ritual vidhi.
- The royal procession's boats, porters, attendants, food, lodging, and route
  burden remain material labour and environmental context rather than magical
  frictionless service.
- Bharadwaja's view of succession, monarchy, renunciation, and obedience remains
  attributed period reasoning, not present-day governance advice.
- The hospitality vision includes servants, material abundance, and sensual
  imagery. It is not treated as consent, entitlement, or a model for labour and
  gender relations.
- Indra's fear and Brihaspati's response are a divine narrative frame. They are
  not evidence about political causation or a licence to punish disagreement.
- Traditional route and place names are preserved without claiming that every
  location is a verified modern geographic identification.

No invented dialogue, modern geography, universal ritual instruction, hidden
consent, or reconciliation across Ramayana expressions was added.
