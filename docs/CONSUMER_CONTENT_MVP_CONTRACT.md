# Devam consumer-content MVP contract

## Formal objective

Build and complete the Devam MVP for ordinary Indian users around a
comprehensive, consumer-ready English and Hindi story and living-practice
knowledge base. Lawfully acquire, retain, structure and, where necessary,
translate the complete narrative content needed for the MVP: the full Ramayana
and Mahabharata story universes; Ramcharitmanas daily reading; Hanuman Chalisa;
approachable stories from prioritized Puranic, devotional, Panchatantra,
historical and cultural traditions; practical festival and ritual guidance;
and the connected characters, places, temples, kingdoms, history, yoga,
meditation and living culture that make them meaningful today.

English/Hindi stories and actionable practice are the default database and
product priority. Sanskrit originals, verse counts, edition comparison,
citations and scholarly disputes are not the normal consumer experience.
Unique source bytes, provenance, rights, uncertainty and materially useful
variants remain internal grounding. When only one suitable language is
available, Devam may acquire it first and derive a clearly labelled translation
into the other language.

The corpus feeds Supabase/Postgres, exact retrieval, Sarthi and the game-like
Living Atlas. Work stays lean, avoids duplicated source payloads and retired
proof bureaucracy, and distinguishes the defined MVP scope from the longer
civilizational ambition.

## Audience and product test

The reference user is an Indian adult accustomed to mobile apps, games, short
video and social feeds. They may know the broad outline of a famous epic but
still expect depth. They should not need Sanskrit, textual scholarship or prior
religious study.

A content decision passes only if it helps that person do at least one of these:

- follow a complete story without losing character, place, cause or consequence;
- enter at a character, place or event and move back into the same story;
- understand a festival or ritual and what people commonly do today;
- discover how a story connects to a place, temple, festival, practice, kingdom or later history;
- read a short meaningful daily portion in Hindi or English; or
- ask Sarthi or Search a natural question and receive the useful answer before source apparatus.

## What counts as complete

### Epic or long story

A selected expression is consumer-complete only when:

1. its beginning-to-end source denominator is acquired and structurally reconciled;
2. every source unit maps into an ordered story moment or an explicitly reviewed compression range;
3. English and Hindi preserve the same action, motivation, consequence, character and place continuity;
4. every major arc has sufficient narrative depth rather than a one-line placeholder;
5. character, place and linear traversal all open the same canonical moments;
6. living connections are separately scoped and do not rewrite the epic; and
7. Search, Sarthi and Atlas all read the same shared records.

Completeness does not require a screen per verse or section. It does require
that no material story interval disappears behind a heading or a source count.

### Short devotional or daily-reading work

The complete text or selected lawful expression must be available in a readable
sequence, with approachable English and Hindi meaning, a sensible daily unit,
and optional audio/visual enrichment later. Source text and translation remain
distinguishable internally even when the public surface is simple.

### Festival or ritual

Use `RITUAL_AND_OBSERVANCE_PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md`. The consumer answer leads with
meaning, what people typically do, timing and actionable steps. Applicability,
source and variant machinery remains behind that answer unless it changes the
action or prevents a material error.

### Place, temple, kingdom, history, yoga or meditation

Modern-verifiable information may use authoritative current web research.
Narrative geography, living belief, archaeology, attested history and visitor
information remain separate internally. The public presentation may connect
them spatially, but must not convert a story into a historical claim or a
current practice into a universal rule.

## MVP acquisition order

1. Complete the existing 49-turn Ramayana consumer backbone in English and Hindi.
2. Complete Ramcharitmanas product text and daily reading; add Hanuman Chalisa.
3. Qualify the retained complete-English Mahabharata candidate and build the full bilingual narrative backbone.
4. Finish story depth for Ganesha, Durga/Navaratri/Durga Puja and Diwali while preserving existing ritual actionability.
5. Add the highest-demand Puranic and Panchatantra stories that connect those worlds.
6. Add the places, temples, kingdoms, history, yoga, meditation and living-practice records needed by those journeys.
7. Continue outward through an explicit coverage inventory; never present the MVP slice as all Indian culture.

## Default disclosure rule

The normal interface does not show citations, source hashes, Sanskrit locators,
edition comparison or variant dossiers. A deliberate “source” or “why” action
may open them. Material uncertainty, safety, regional applicability or genuine
disagreement is surfaced in plain language when hiding it would mislead the
user.

## Anti-bloat rule

One canonical narrative moment feeds sequence, map, character, Search, Sarthi
and Atlas views. Do not author separate retellings for each surface. Media is
lazy and replaceable. Source payloads stay in the one-copy vault. New tables or
services must remove duplication or enable a proven consumer path; otherwise
use the existing Postgres model and data-driven renderers.
