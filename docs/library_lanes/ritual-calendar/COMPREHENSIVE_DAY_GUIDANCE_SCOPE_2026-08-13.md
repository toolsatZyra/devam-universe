# Comprehensive ritual-calendar day-guidance scope

## Product question

The selected library must support: "What should I do on this day?" for an
English- or Hindi-speaking user in a priority Indian state. The answer may be a
festival, vrata, recurring tithi practice, ordinary weekday practice, a
household puja, or guidance for an ordinary temple participant.

The answer is not chosen from state alone. State selects an initial calendar
and practice cluster. Location, lunar/solar calendar, sampradaya, institution,
family practice, life stage, setting, health and desired level of observance
are asked only when they materially change the answer.

## Priority geography

The first deep-completeness denominator covers twenty routes: Delhi, Uttar
Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan, Madhya
Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka,
Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Assam. North and West
India are intentionally weighted, while the named major regional calendar
systems remain explicit.

This is not a claim that the other states and union territories have no
practice. They remain expansion backlog after this selected denominator is
complete.

## Exact frozen census

`ritual-calendar-comprehensive-census-v3.json` freezes:

- 487 civil days from 2026-09-01 through 2027-12-31;
- twenty priority state routes;
- household and ordinary temple-participant settings;
- 19,480 day-answer cells (`487 x 20 x 2`);
- ten regional calendar discovery systems; and
- 425 unique raw source labels from 971 dated source occurrences in the 2027
  discovery calendars.

The 425 labels are an audit denominator, not 425 accepted rituals. Every label
must receive one explicit disposition: distinct lane, alias, non-ritual
calendar event, outside selected Sanatana scope, hero-owned cross-link,
or evidence/authority block. Only then is the normalized named-lane denominator
known. No new prose is counted before that normalization is frozen.

Calendar discovery pages identify candidates and timing rules. They are never
sufficient evidence for stories, common practice or actionable steps.

## Major-difference rule

A separate applicability lane is required only when a difference changes what
the user should actually do. Material differences include:

- the date, precedence rule or calendar system;
- deity focus or a source story that changes the observance;
- eligibility, life stage, family-practice or sampradaya condition;
- required materials, fasting or health boundary;
- household, temple-participant, priest-led or initiated authority; or
- ordered actions or closing.

Names, spellings, pronunciation, optional food, flowers, colours, decoration,
songs and local emphasis remain labelled variants inside one lane when timing,
eligibility, safety and actions are unchanged. A state name by itself never
justifies a duplicate pack.

## Temple boundary

Temple guidance means ordinary participation: how to prepare, what may be
brought when permitted, visitor conduct, participation steps, crowd/fire
safety, and closing. A current institution programme needs a freshness date.
Priest-led liturgy, Agamic or Tantric technical procedure, initiation-restricted
practice and consecration are never rewritten as do-it-yourself instructions.

## Completion test

The selected denominator is complete only when all 425 source labels are
dispositioned, every accepted materially distinct lane meets the bilingual
ritual product contract, and all 19,480 day-answer cells resolve to every
applicable lane or a sourced ordinary-day answer. Authored content is still not
projected, hosted, reviewed for release or published.
