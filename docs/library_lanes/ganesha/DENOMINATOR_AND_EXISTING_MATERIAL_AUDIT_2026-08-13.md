## Existing material classification

### Reusable, but not consumer-story completion

- The exact Sanskrit Wikisource Ganesha Purana universe is structurally
  complete for its pinned two-khanda transcription: 92 Upasana chapters and
  155 Krida chapters represented by 62 source-addressed passages. It has no
  supplied English or Hindi translation and no consumer scene sequence.
- Ganapati Atharvashirsha revision 415703 has all 16 fixed Sanskrit units and
  labelled Devam English/Hindi translations. This is a complete translation of
  that revision, not a narrative family, pronunciation guide, critical
  edition, universal ritual authority, or complete Ganesha world.
- The source-bounded `Shriganapatimantraksharavali` pack and seven retained
  Ambuda hymn texts support exact devotional/text doors. They do not complete
  any selected beginning-to-end story.
- The West India Smarta household Ganesh Chaturthi record is classified by its
  own contract as a user-complete internal-beta lane. This lane may reference
  and audit it, but will not copy its prose or claim that it covers every
  Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Andhra-Telangana, Tamil, temple, priest-led, or
  family practice.
- The current Ganeshotsav campaign calendar is a 2026 crosswalk from the
  opening Chaturthi to Anant Chaturdashi. It explicitly does not prescribe one
  mandatory murti-stay duration and is not a complete public-participation
  guide.
- Existing Siddhivinayak Trust and Maharashtra Tourism references can support
  narrowly scoped current facts and festival context. They do not establish
  the other eleven selected temple records.

### Partial or potentially misleading if counted as complete

- The Ganesha Purana structural search doorway can answer where the two
  divisions begin and end. Counting it as story coverage would confuse source
  structure with a consumer narrative.
- Existing Atlas nodes and typed routes make Ganesha discoverable, but they do
  not provide the substantial English/Hindi scene sequences required by the
  consumer contract.
- The existing household ritual's short origin summaries identify a current
  Maharashtra birth-day account and modern public-festival history. They are
  not a full Puranic birth story or a complete history of public Ganeshotsav.
- A temple's official belief statement is evidence for that temple's living
  tradition. It is not automatically textual history, archaeology, or a
  universal Ganesha identity claim.

### Blocked and not counted

- The fixed Mudgala Purana carrier is structurally complete for nine khandas
  but is `internal_only_rights_review_pending`, lacks a usable text layer, and
  has no English/Hindi translation. Its eight-form cycle remains an explicit
  blocked extension and supplies no consumer prose.
- An exhaustive concordance of every birth, broken-tusk, vehicle, marriage,
  moon, or regional-origin variant is outside this first frozen denominator.
  Selected expressions will be labelled, and material variants discovered
  during authoring will be recorded without pretending that the selected
  expression is universal.

## Frozen selected scope

The lane accepts six story families: the complete four-yuga narrative spine of
the selected Ganesha Purana Krida Khanda; the Shiva Purana doorway-birth,
battle, and restoration account; the Shiva Purana family-circumambulation
contest; the selected Ganguli Mahabharata scribe account; the Brahma Vaivarta
Purana Parashurama/broken-tusk account; and its Ganesha-Tulasi account.

It also accepts four distinct practice lanes: the existing West India Smarta
household record, a Maharashtra public-participant lane, a Goa Chavath
household-participant lane, and a Tamil Nadu Pillaiyar/Vinayaka Chaturthi
household-participant lane. The latter three must not borrow a synthetic puja
sequence merely to satisfy a field.

The selected living-place denominator contains all eight Ashtavinayak shrines
and four other regional living temples: Siddhivinayak Prabhadevi, Dagdusheth
Halwai Pune, Varasiddhi Vinayaka Kanipakam, and Uchi Pillayar
Tiruchirappalli. Each record must separate sacred narrative, current temple
identity, documented history, institution-specific practice, and current
visitor information.
